## B-51 · Explain and distinguish "contract of service" and "contract for service", with examples

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q6 (this wording); Dec 2025 Q3 (A-44, 4 marks: "Distinguish between 'Contract of Service' and 'Contract for Service'"). 20-mark form: C-10 and C-22.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** meaning 2 · table 5 · tests and consumer law 3

### Answer

A **contract of service** is between an employer and an **employee (servant)**; a **contract for service(s)** is between a client and an **independent contractor**.

| | Contract of service | Contract for services |
|---|---|---|
| Control | Employer controls **what and how** | Client controls the **result** only |
| Payment | Wages or salary | Fee for the job |
| Tools, risk | Employer's | Contractor's |
| Hiring and dismissal | By employer | Contractor's own staff |
| Tort liability | **Employer vicariously liable** | **Generally not** (with exceptions) |
| Examples | Bank clerk, driver, salaried nurse | Builder, plumber, architect, lawyer, doctor in private practice |
| Consumer Protection Act | **Excluded**: "contract of personal service" (s. 2(42)) | **Included**, if paid |

**Tests.** Control (*Dharangadhara Chemical Works*, AIR 1957 SC 264); integration (Denning L.J.); mixed or economic reality test (*Ready Mixed Concrete*, 1968; *Silver Jubilee Tailoring House*, 1974).

**Consumer law.** An employee cannot complain against his employer of deficiency in the employer's service. *Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha* (1995): paid medical services under a contract *for* service are covered; *Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi* (2024): advocates' services were held not to be "service" ‡.

## B-52 · Write a short note on the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum (Commission)

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q7 (this wording); Dec 2022 Q4 (A-122, composition); Dec 2023 Q11 (C-23, powers and jurisdiction, 20 marks). 20-mark form: C-23.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** composition 2 · jurisdiction 3 · powers and procedure 3 · appeals 2

### Answer

The **District Commission** (District Forum under the 1986 Act) is the first tier of the three-tier system (s. 28).

**Composition.** A **President** (a person who is, was or is qualified to be a District Judge) and **at least two other members**, one a **woman**. Term: four years or up to age 65 †.

**Jurisdiction**
- **Pecuniary (s. 34):** by the **consideration paid**: up to **₹50 lakh** (2021 Rules; the Act said ₹1 crore) ‡.
- **Territorial (s. 34(2)):** where the opposite party resides, works or carries on business; where the cause of action arises; or where the **complainant resides or works for gain** ‡.

**Powers.** Civil-court powers (summoning, discovery, affidavits, laboratory reports: s. 38(9)); interim orders; reliefs under s. 39: replace goods, refund, compensation, remove deficiency, corrective advertisement, costs; execution as a decree (s. 71); penalty for non-compliance, one month to three years or ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh (s. 72) ‡.

**Procedure.** Complaint (nil fee up to ₹5 lakh †); admissibility within 21 days; reply within 30 days; decision in three months; limitation two years (s. 69).

**Appeal.** To the State Commission within 45 days, with 50% deposit (s. 41) ‡.

## B-53 · Who is a consumer? Is a person buying goods for earning his livelihood a consumer?

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q11 (C-22, 20 marks: "Who is a Consumer?"). 20-mark form: C-22.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 4 · exclusions 3 · cases 3

### Answer

**Definition (s. 2(7))** ‡. A consumer is a person who (i) **buys goods** for a consideration paid, promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under deferred payment, including a **user** with the buyer's approval; or (ii) **hires or avails of a service** on the same terms, including a **beneficiary** with the approval of the person who hires it. Online, teleshopping, direct selling and multi-level marketing transactions are included.

**Exclusions.** A person who obtains goods for **resale** or any **commercial purpose**, or services for a commercial purpose, is not a consumer. "Commercial purpose" does **not** include goods bought and used **exclusively for earning his livelihood by self-employment**.

**Cases**
- ***Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute*** (1995) 3 SCC 583: commercial purpose is a question of fact, decided by the dominant purpose; large-scale profit-making is commercial; self-employment for livelihood is not †.
- ***Cheema Engineering Services v. Rajan Singh*** (1997) †: machinery bought for self-employment; consumer.
- ***Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia*** (1998): the child, as a beneficiary, was a consumer.

**Who may complain (s. 35):** consumer, recognised consumer association, same-interest consumers, Government, CCPA, legal heir, parent or guardian of a minor.

## B-54 · Distinguish deficiency in service from defect in goods. What is meant by deficiency in service?

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q3 (A-115, 4 marks: "What is meant by 'Deficiency in Service'?"). 20-mark form: C-22.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** deficiency 4 · defect 2 · table 2 · cases 2

### Answer

**Deficiency (s. 2(11))** ‡: any **fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance** which is required to be maintained by or under any law, or has been undertaken to be performed in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service. It includes (i) any act of **negligence, omission or commission** which causes loss or injury to the consumer, and (ii) **deliberate withholding of relevant information**.

**Defect (s. 2(10))** ‡: any fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the **quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard** required by law or claimed by the trader, in relation to goods.

| | Defect | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Goods | Services |
| Standard | Law or the trader's claim | Law, contract, or undertaking |
| Includes | Quality, quantity, potency, purity | Negligence; withholding of information |
| Relief (s. 39) | Remove defect, replace, refund | Remove deficiency; compensation |

**Cases.** *Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta* (1994): delay, defective construction and harassment by an authority is deficiency. *Spring Meadows Hospital* (1998): negligent treatment. *Pioneer Urban v. Govindan Raghavan* (2019): delay in handing over a flat.

## B-55 · Explain unfair trade practice and unfair contract under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related: C-25. 
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** unfair trade practice 5 · unfair contract 3 · restrictive trade practice and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Unfair trade practice (s. 2(47))** ‡: a trade practice that adopts an **unfair method or deceptive practice** to promote the sale, use or supply of goods or services. It includes:
- **false or misleading statements** about standard, quality, grade, model, warranty or price, or disparaging another's goods;
- **bargain-price advertising** that is not genuine, and prizes or gifts not intended to be given;
- goods not meeting **safety standards**; **hoarding** to raise prices; **spurious goods**;
- **failure to issue a bill or receipt**;
- **refusal to take back defective goods or refund** within the stipulated time (30 days if none);
- **disclosing personal information** given in confidence.

**Unfair contract (s. 2(46))** ‡: a contract between a manufacturer, trader or service provider and a consumer whose terms cause a **significant change in the rights of the consumer**: excessive security deposits, disproportionate penalty for breach, refusal to accept early repayment on payment of penalty, **unilateral termination** without reasonable cause, one-sided assignment, unreasonable charges. Complaints go to the State or National Commission †.

**Restrictive trade practice (s. 2(41))** †: a practice that imposes unjustified cost or restriction on consumers, such as tie-in conditions or delay in supply.

*Cases:* *Pioneer Urban* (2019); *Ireo Grace* (2021).

## B-56 · Explain product liability under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related: C-25. 
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** meaning 2 · liability of manufacturer, service provider and seller 5 · exceptions and comparison with tort 3

### Answer

**Meaning (s. 2(34))** ‡. The responsibility of a **product manufacturer, product service provider or product seller** to compensate for **harm** caused to a consumer by a **defective product** or by deficiency in related services. Harm includes damage to property (other than the product), personal injury, illness, mental agony and loss. A claim is a **product liability action** (Chapter VI, ss. 82–87).

**Manufacturer (s. 84)** †. Liable if the product has a **manufacturing defect** or **design defect**, deviates from the manufacturing specifications, does not conform to the **express warranty**, or lacks **adequate instructions** or **warnings** about danger.

**Service provider (s. 85)** †. Liable for faulty or imperfect service, inadequate instructions or warnings, negligence or conscious withholding of information.

**Seller (s. 86)** †. Liable only if he controlled design, testing, packaging or labelling; altered the product; gave an independent express warranty; the manufacturer is untraceable; or he failed to take reasonable care.

**Exceptions (s. 87)** †. Misuse or alteration by the claimant; adequate warning ignored; certain bulk or sophisticated purchasers.

**Compared with tort.** In tort (*Donoghue v. Stevenson*) the plaintiff must prove negligence; here the manufacturer is liable for a **defect**, a form of strict liability, before the Commission, a faster forum.

## B-57 · Explain the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA): its establishment, powers and functions

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related: C-25. 
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** establishment 2 · powers 5 · related instruments 3

### Answer

**Establishment.** The CCPA was established under s. 10 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Chapter III, ss. 10–27) with effect from **24 July 2020** ‡, to promote, protect and enforce consumers' rights **as a class**, and to regulate violation of consumer rights, **unfair trade practices** and **false or misleading advertisements**. It has a Chief Commissioner, Commissioners and an **Investigation Wing** under a Director-General.

**Powers and functions**
1. **Inquire or investigate**, on complaint or on its own motion.
2. **Class action**.
3. **Order recall** of unsafe goods, **reimbursement** of prices, and discontinuation of unfair practices.
4. Issue **safety notices**.
5. **Misleading advertisements (s. 21):** order discontinuance or modification, and impose a **penalty** on the manufacturer or endorser of up to ₹10 lakh (₹50 lakh for a subsequent contravention) and **bar an endorser** for up to one year (three years for a subsequent contravention) †.
6. **Search and seizure** (s. 22).

**Appeal.** To the National Commission (s. 24).

**Related instruments.** E-Commerce Rules, 2020 ‡; Guidelines on Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022 (9 June 2022) ‡; Guidelines on Dark Patterns, 2023 †.

## B-58 · Explain limitation, appeals and enforcement of orders under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related: C-23, C-24. 
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** limitation and review 3 · appeals 4 · enforcement 3

### Answer

**Limitation (s. 69)** ‡. A complaint must be filed within **two years** of the cause of action; delay may be condoned for sufficient cause, with reasons recorded.

**Review (ss. 40, 50, 60)** ‡. Each Commission may review its own order for an error apparent on the face of the record, within 30 days.

**Appeals** ‡

| From | To | Time | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| District Commission | State Commission (s. 41) | 45 days | Deposit **50%** of the amount ordered |
| State Commission | National Commission (s. 51) | 30 days | 50% deposit |
| National Commission | Supreme Court (s. 67) | 30 days | 50% deposit |

Late appeals may be admitted for sufficient cause. Second appeals lie only on a substantial question of law. **No appeal** lies against an order passed on a **mediated settlement**.

**Enforcement.** An order is enforced as a **decree** of a civil court under Order XXI CPC (s. 71). **Non-compliance** (s. 72) is punishable with imprisonment of **one month to three years**, or a fine of **₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh**, or both; for this the Commission has the powers of a **first-class Magistrate**.

**Decision time.** Three months from notice (five months where testing is needed).

## B-59 · What are the rights of consumers? Explain the Consumer Protection Councils

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Syllabus line: "enforcement of consumer rights". Related: C-25.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** six rights 6 · Councils 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Consumer rights (s. 2(9))** ‡. The right to be:
1. **Protected** against marketing of goods and services **hazardous** to life and property (safety).
2. **Informed** about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard and price.
3. **Assured**, where possible, of **access** to a variety of goods and services at competitive prices (choice).
4. **Heard**, and to have consumer interests considered at appropriate forums.
5. Given **redressal** against unfair or restrictive trade practices or unscrupulous exploitation.
6. Given **consumer awareness** (education).

*Memory aid:* safety, information, choice, hearing, redress, awareness. President Kennedy's message of 1962 named the first four; the UN Guidelines for Consumer Protection (1985) widened the list.

**Consumer Protection Councils (Chapter II)** †. Advisory bodies to promote and protect these rights: the **Central Consumer Protection Council** (s. 3), **State Councils** (s. 7) and **District Councils** (s. 8). They advise on policy, and they do not decide disputes.

**Enforcement.** Rights are enforced by the three-tier Commissions (District, State and National), by the Central Consumer Protection Authority for class action, and by mediation.

## B-60 · Explain mediation and the regulation of e-commerce under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related: C-25. 
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** mediation 4 · e-commerce 4 · conclusion 2

### Answer

**Mediation (s. 37; Chapter V, ss. 74–81)** ‡
- At the first hearing, the Commission may direct the parties to try mediation if they consent in writing within five days.
- A **Consumer Mediation Cell** is attached to each Commission, with a panel of mediators (Consumer Protection (Mediation) Rules, 2020 †).
- A settlement is recorded and the Commission passes an order. **No appeal** lies against an order on a mediated settlement.

**E-commerce**
- "Buys any goods" and "hires or avails of services" include **online** and electronic transactions, teleshopping, direct selling and multi-level marketing (s. 2(7), Explanation (b)) ‡.
- The **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020** ‡ require marketplace and inventory entities to display seller details, appoint a **grievance officer**, and avoid unfair trade practices.
- Complaints may be filed **electronically** and heard by **video conference**; the consumer may file where he **resides or works** (s. 34(2)(d)).
- The CCPA acts against **misleading advertisements** (Guidelines, 2022 ‡) and **dark patterns** (Guidelines, 2023 †).
- The Consumer Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2025 (Bill No. 39 of 2025) on digital marketplaces is pending; it has not become law as far as I could confirm ‡†.

**Conclusion.** Mediation gives speedy settlement; the e-commerce rules extend the Act to online sales.
