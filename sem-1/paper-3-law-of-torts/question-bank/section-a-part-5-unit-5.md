<a id="a-111"></a>
**A-111 · Consumer** · *Related: B-53, C-22*

Under s. 2(7) a consumer is a person who buys goods, or hires or avails of a service, for a consideration paid, promised, partly paid and partly promised, or under deferred payment. It includes any user of the goods, or beneficiary of the service, with the approval of the buyer or hirer, and covers online, teleshopping, direct selling and multi-level marketing transactions ‡.

*Word count: 62*

<a id="a-112"></a>
**A-112 · Who is not a consumer** · *Related: B-53, C-22*

A person who obtains goods for resale or for any commercial purpose, or avails of a service for any commercial purpose, is not a consumer ‡. A person who receives a service free of charge, or under a contract of personal service, is also outside the Act. A person who buys goods for self-employment to earn his livelihood is still a consumer.

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-113"></a>
**A-113 · Commercial purpose and self-employment** · *Related: B-53, C-22*

Goods bought for resale or commercial purpose do not make the buyer a consumer. But "commercial purpose" does not include goods bought and used exclusively for earning a livelihood by self-employment (s. 2(7), Explanation (a)) ‡. *Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute* (1995) held that it is a question of fact, decided by the dominant purpose †.

*Word count: 56*

<a id="a-114"></a>
**A-114 · Complaint and complainant** · *Related: B-52, C-24*

A complaint is an allegation in writing that an unfair or restrictive trade practice or unfair contract has been adopted, goods are defective, services are deficient, an excess price has been charged, hazardous goods are offered, or a product liability claim lies (s. 2(6)) ‡. A complainant (s. 2(5), s. 35(1)) is a consumer, recognised consumer association, Government, the CCPA, a legal heir, or a parent or guardian of a minor.

*Word count: 70*

<a id="a-115"></a>
**A-115 · Deficiency in service** · *Asked: Dec 2023 Q3*

Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by law, or has been undertaken to be performed under a contract or otherwise (s. 2(11)). It includes negligence, omission or commission causing loss or injury, and deliberate withholding of relevant information ‡. Example: delay by a development authority (*Lucknow DA v. M.K. Gupta*, 1994).

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-116"></a>
**A-116 · Defect in goods** · *Related: B-54, C-22*

Any fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard which is required to be maintained by or under any law, or is claimed by the trader in any manner in relation to the goods (s. 2(10)) ‡. Relief under s. 39: remove the defect, replace the goods, or return the price, with compensation.

*Word count: 56*

<a id="a-117"></a>
**A-117 · "Service" under the Act** · *Related: B-51, C-22*

Service of any description made available to potential users, including banking, financing, insurance, transport, processing, supply of electrical or other energy, telecom, boarding or lodging, housing construction, entertainment, amusement, and the purveying of news or other information (s. 2(42)). It does not include service rendered free of charge or under a contract of personal service ‡.

*Word count: 55*

<a id="a-118"></a>
**A-118 · Contract of personal service** · *Related: B-51, C-22*

A contract of personal service is a contract of service between employer and employee, in which the employer controls the work. Such a service is excluded from "service" in the Act, so an employee cannot complain of deficiency against his employer. A contract for services (with a builder, architect or doctor in private practice) is included if paid for (*V.P. Shantha*, 1995). *D.K. Gandhi* (2024) excluded advocates ‡.

*Word count: 67*

<a id="a-119"></a>
**A-119 · Unfair trade practice** · *Related: B-55, C-25*

A trade practice that adopts an unfair method or deceptive practice to promote the sale, use or supply of goods or services (s. 2(47)) ‡. It includes false or misleading statements, non-genuine bargain-price advertising, prizes not intended to be given, hoarding, spurious goods, failure to issue a bill, refusal to take back defective goods or refund, and disclosure of personal information given in confidence.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-120"></a>
**A-120 · Unfair contract** · *Related: B-55, C-25*

A contract between a manufacturer, trader or service provider and a consumer having terms that cause a significant change in the consumer's rights (s. 2(46)) ‡: excessive security deposits, disproportionate penalties, refusal to accept early repayment on payment of a penalty, unilateral termination without reasonable cause, one-sided assignment, unreasonable charges. Complaints go to the State or National Commission †.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-121"></a>
**A-121 · Consumer rights** · *Related: B-59, C-25*

Under s. 2(9): the right to be protected against hazardous goods and services (safety); to be informed of quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard and price; to have access to a variety of goods at competitive prices (choice); to be heard; to seek redressal against unfair trade practices and exploitation; and to consumer awareness ‡.

*Word count: 53*

<a id="a-122"></a>
**A-122 · Composition of the District Commission (Forum)** · *Asked: Dec 2022 Q4*

Under the 1986 Act the District Forum consisted of a President, who was or had been a District Judge, and two other members, one of them a woman †. The 2019 Act renames it the District Commission: a President, who is, has been or is qualified to be a District Judge, and at least two other members, one a woman under the Rules (s. 28) ‡. Term: four years or age 65.

*Word count: 70*

<a id="a-123"></a>
**A-123 · Pecuniary jurisdiction of the Commissions** · *Related: B-52, C-23*

By the value of the consideration paid. The Act said: District up to ₹1 crore, State up to ₹10 crore, National above. The 2021 Rules (30 December 2021) revised them: District up to ₹50 lakh, State above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore, National above ₹2 crore ‡. Unfair-contract complaints go to the State (up to ₹10 crore) or National Commission †.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-124"></a>
**A-124 · Territorial jurisdiction** · *Related: B-52, C-23*

Under s. 34(2) a complaint may be filed where (a) the opposite party resides, carries on business, has a branch office or personally works for gain; (b) any one of several opposite parties does, with the Commission's permission; (c) the cause of action arises, wholly or in part; or (d) the complainant resides or personally works for gain ‡. Clause (d) is new in 2019.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-125"></a>
**A-125 · State and National Commissions** · *Related: B-52, C-23*

The State Commission (s. 42) has a President, a sitting or retired High Court Judge, and at least four members. It hears complaints above ₹50 lakh and up to ₹2 crore, and appeals from District Commissions (s. 41). The National Commission (s. 53) hears complaints above ₹2 crore and appeals from State Commissions (s. 51); appeal lies to the Supreme Court (s. 67) ‡.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-126"></a>
**A-126 · Limitation for consumer complaints** · *Related: B-58, C-24*

A complaint must be filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action arose (s. 69). The Commission may admit a complaint after the period if the complainant shows sufficient cause for the delay, recording its reasons ‡. Where the wrong continues, as with non-delivery of a flat, the cause of action may continue †.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-127"></a>
**A-127 · Appeals under the Act** · *Related: B-58, C-23*

From the District Commission to the State Commission within 45 days (s. 41); from the State Commission to the National Commission within 30 days (s. 51); from the National Commission to the Supreme Court within 30 days (s. 67). The appellant must deposit 50% of the amount ordered. No appeal lies from an order passed on a mediated settlement ‡.

*Word count: 59*

<a id="a-128"></a>
**A-128 · Reliefs under s. 39** · *Related: B-52, C-23*

The Commission may order the opposite party to remove the defect or deficiency, replace the goods, return the price, pay compensation (including punitive damages in a fit case), discontinue the unfair or restrictive trade practice, withdraw hazardous goods, issue corrective advertisement, pay a sum for the benefit of unidentified consumers, and pay costs ‡.

*Word count: 53*

<a id="a-129"></a>
**A-129 · Central Consumer Protection Authority** · *Related: B-57, C-25*

Established under s. 10, with effect from 24 July 2020 ‡, to promote, protect and enforce the rights of consumers as a class. It regulates unfair trade practices and false or misleading advertisements. Its powers include investigation, class action, recall of goods, reimbursement, safety notices and search and seizure (s. 22). Appeals lie to the National Commission (s. 24).

*Word count: 58*

<a id="a-130"></a>
**A-130 · Product liability** · *Related: B-56, C-25*

The responsibility of a product manufacturer, product service provider or product seller to compensate a consumer for harm caused by a defective product or deficient related service (s. 2(34); Chapter VI, ss. 82–87) ‡. A manufacturer is liable for manufacturing or design defects, non-conformity to specifications or express warranty, and inadequate instructions or warnings. Negligence need not be proved †.

*Word count: 58*

<a id="a-131"></a>
**A-131 · Misleading advertisements** · *Related: B-57, C-25*

The CCPA may order the discontinuance or modification of a false or misleading advertisement and impose a penalty on the manufacturer or endorser of up to ₹10 lakh (up to ₹50 lakh for a subsequent contravention), and bar an endorser for up to one year (three years for a repeat) (s. 21) †. The Guidelines of 9 June 2022 regulate endorsements ‡.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-132"></a>
**A-132 · Mediation** · *Related: B-60, C-25*

At the first hearing, the Commission may refer the parties to mediation if they consent in writing (s. 37; ss. 74–81) ‡. A Consumer Mediation Cell is attached to each Commission. A settlement is recorded and the Commission passes an order; no appeal lies against an order on a mediated settlement. It gives quicker and cheaper relief.

*Word count: 56*

<a id="a-133"></a>
**A-133 · E-commerce and the consumer** · *Related: B-60, C-25*

The Act covers goods and services bought online, by teleshopping, direct selling or multi-level marketing (s. 2(7), Explanation (b)) ‡. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 ‡ require marketplace and inventory entities to display seller details, appoint a grievance officer and avoid unfair trade practices. Complaints may be filed electronically and heard by video conference.

*Word count: 53*

<a id="a-134"></a>
**A-134 · Consumer Protection Councils** · *Related: B-59*

Advisory bodies to promote and protect consumers' rights: the Central Consumer Protection Council (s. 3), State Councils (s. 7) and District Councils (s. 8) †. They advise on policy and consumer awareness. They do not decide disputes, which belong to the District, State and National Commissions.

*Word count: 45*

<a id="a-135"></a>
**A-135 · Penalty for non-compliance with an order** · *Related: B-58, C-23*

Under s. 72, failure to comply with an order of a Commission is punishable with imprisonment of one month to three years, or fine of ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh, or both ‡. For this purpose the Commission has the powers of a first-class Magistrate. Orders are also enforced as decrees of a civil court under Order XXI CPC (s. 71) ‡.

*Word count: 59*
