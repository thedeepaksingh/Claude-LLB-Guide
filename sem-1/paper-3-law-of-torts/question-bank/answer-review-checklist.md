# Paper III · Answer-Review Checklist (use after every mock and every practice answer)

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-29 (IST) · **Law stated as on:** 2026-10-29 · **Branch:** `paper/03-law-of-torts`
> **What this is.** A checklist for marking your own answers against the [model answers](README.md) and for finding what to fix before the exam. **The mark splits are mine.** The university publishes no marking scheme, and I could not find one (see the [gaps document](../../../docs/01-gaps-and-open-questions.md) and the [source-verification log](source-verification-log.md)). Treat them as a guide to what a careful examiner would look for.

---

## 1. Before you review: set the answer beside the question

1. Did you **answer the question that was asked**? Compare your first paragraph with the verb in the question (*explain, discuss, comment, distinguish, write a note, state*).
2. Did you keep to the **exam limit**? Section A ≤ 75 words, Section B ≤ 200 words, Section C: no limit printed ("answer is required in detail"), but the models run 700–1,600 words; 700–1,000 handwritten words (my estimate) with the same headings and the leading cases fits a 30–35 minute answer.
3. Did you write the **right number of answers**? A: all five. B: two of three. C: three of five. An extra answer in B or C does not earn extra marks.
4. If the question quotes a statement ("The mere causing of actual loss … is not necessarily a tort"; "It is the consent which is the basis of *volenti*"), did you **explain it, test it with cases**, and say where it is right and where it is not?
5. If it is a **problem** (as in December 2025 Q7 and Q10), did you use **IRAC**: facts in a line, issue, rule with a case, application to *these* facts, conclusion? See section 5.

## 2. Section A (4 marks): tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Definition or meaning in the first sentence | 1 |
| Two or three main points (not a list of ten) | 2 |
| One case, section or example, with the year | 1 |
| ≤ 75 words | required |

**Typical losses:** starting with a long introduction; no case; the wrong year; confusing *damnum sine injuria* with *injuria sine damno*; writing "Forum" for the 2019 Act without saying "Commission"; writing 120 words and running out of time.

## 4. Section C (20 marks): tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Introduction: definition, meaning, background | 3 |
| Five or six headings in a logical order (essentials, then defences or exceptions) | 5 |
| Leading cases named correctly, with facts or holding (at least four) | 5 |
| At least one Indian case or section | 2 |
| Criticism, comparison, current development or a balanced view | 3 |
| Conclusion that answers the question set | 2 |

**Typical losses:** a good definition with no cases; cases listed without facts or holding; a quoted statement left untested; an answer that stops after the first half of a two-part question (for example, "who is a consumer *and* what is deficiency in service"; "ingredients of defamation *and* defences"; "nature of the liability *and* extent of it"); using the 1986 Consumer Act for a 2019 question.

## 5. Problem questions (IRAC)

| Step | What to write | Marks (mine, for a 10-mark problem) |
|---|---|---|
| Facts and parties | The relevant facts, in two lines, with the parties named A and B | 1 |
| Issue | The legal question, in one sentence | 1 |
| Rule | The tort, its essentials, the leading case | 3 |
| Application | Apply each essential to **these** facts; deal with the defence or the contrary view | 4 |
| Conclusion | A clear answer ("A is liable; B can recover damages, reduced for contributory negligence") | 1 |

**December 2025 Q10** (the book): the comment on the book is protected as fair comment (Exception 6 to the old IPC s. 499, now BNS s. 356), but A's remark about Z's *character* is not a comment on the book and may be defamatory. **December 2025 Q7** (the horse): consider duty, breach by the owner (leaving the animal on the road), causation, the scooterist's own care, contributory negligence and apportionment. See [C-20](section-c-part-4-units-4b-4c.md#c-20) and [B-31](section-b-part-3-unit-4a.md#b-31).

## 6. Accuracy check

### 6.1 Mistakes I found in my own drafts and corrected

Check that you have **not** made these mistakes. I found them while checking the model answers.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| In *Nettleship v. Weston* the learner driver's damages were reduced by 25% | The Court of Appeal held the instructor not *volenti* and reduced his damages by **50%** for contributory negligence ‡ |
| *Duck v. Mayeu* as the case for release of a joint tortfeasor | I replaced it with *Cocke v. Jennor* (1614), which I am surer of †; I could not confirm the other case |
| *Mouse's Case* (1608) as a case on contribution between wrongdoers | It is a case on **necessity** (a casket thrown overboard to save a ferry). I removed the contribution claim |
| *Esso v. Southport*, *Cope v. Sharpe* and *Thomas v. Quartermaine* described one way in one answer and another way in unit 2 | The same description is now used everywhere; *Esso* and *Thomas* remain marked † |
| *P. & O. Steam Navigation Co.* facts were mixed up | A horse of the plaintiff was injured by the negligence of Government servants in the Kidderpore dockyard †. The case is the source of the sovereign / non-sovereign distinction |
| *Nobin Chunder Dey* given as a leading case on State liability | I removed it from the answers: I could not confirm its facts |
| *Brooke v. Bool*, *Dulieu v. White* and *Bourhill v. Young* given with detailed facts | The facts are now stated in general terms (a gas leak looked for with a naked flame; a barmaid who became seriously ill; a woman who heard a collision and claimed shock) |
| *Jacob Mathew* facts stated wrongly | Corrected, and the oxygen-cylinder detail is marked † |
| *Sim v. Stretch* described as a case on a maid's employment | It concerned a telegram about a former maid's belongings and "the money you borrowed" †. The holding (the test of right-thinking people) is certain |
| Facts of *Adam v. Ward* and *Horrocks v. Lowe* given in detail | Stated generally and marked †; the holdings (qualified privilege on duty and interest; lost by malice) are certain |
| *Laxman Joshi*: "boy with a fractured elbow" | "A patient with a fracture … died of shock" †; the three duties of a doctor are certain |
| C-25 said the Act "now names telecom" without a caveat | Rewritten: the 2019 Act names telecom in s. 2(42), so *General Manager, Telecom v. Krishnan* may no longer hold † |
| The exception-6 illustration (the book) quoted loosely | Reworded to match the illustration |
| Word counts printed twice in many Section C answers | Fixed; the script now checks that each answer has exactly one count |

### 6.2 Common confusions to avoid

These are standard slips, not errors that I found in my drafts.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *Damnum sine injuria* is a wrong without loss | That is *injuria sine damno*. *Damnum sine injuria* is loss **without** a legal wrong (*Gloucester Grammar School*; *Mogul*; *Bradford v. Pickles*) |
| *Rylands v. Fletcher* applies to any escape | It needs a dangerous thing, brought for the defendant's own purposes, non-natural use, and **escape** from the defendant's land (*Read v. Lyons*) |
| Absolute liability in *M.C. Mehta* has the *Rylands* exceptions | It has **none**, and compensation is related to the size of the enterprise |
| *Volenti* applies because the plaintiff knew of the risk | Knowledge is not consent (*Smith v. Baker*) |
| Contributory negligence is a complete defence | It reduces damages (1945 Act; India follows apportionment) |
| *Donoghue v. Stevenson* is a case on contract | It is the case on duty of care, outside contract |
| *Anns* is still law in England | *Anns v. Merton* was overruled by *Murphy v. Brentwood*; use *Caparo* |
| The Wagon Mound applies the "direct consequences" test | It rejected *Re Polemis* and applied reasonable foreseeability |
| Slander is always actionable per se | Only in the four English classes (crime, disease, unchastity of a woman, trade); in India the position is unsettled † |
| Truth is a defence in criminal defamation | Only if for the public good (BNS s. 356, Exception 1) |
| IPC ss. 499–500 are still the law of defamation | The BNS, 2023 (from 1 July 2024): defamation is s. 356 |
| A consumer includes anyone who buys goods | Not one who buys for resale or **commercial purpose**; but self-employment for a livelihood is not commercial purpose |
| The District Commission hears claims up to ₹1 crore | Under the 2021 Rules the limit is **₹50 lakh** (consideration paid); State above ₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore; National above ₹2 crore |
| The 2019 Act limitation is one year | Two years (s. 69) |
| "Service" includes services of lawyers | *D.K. Gandhi* (2024) excluded advocates; doctors remain covered under *V.P. Shantha* until a larger bench rules |
