**LL.B. (Semester I) · Law of Torts including Consumer Protection Laws (K-1003)**  
**Time: 3 hours · Maximum marks: 100**

*Note: Answer all the questions in Section A. In Section B answer any two of the three questions. In Section C answer any three of the five questions.*

### Section A (very short answer) · 5 × 4 = 20 marks
*Attempt all questions. Each answer must not exceed 75 words.*

1. What is strict liability?  *(4)*
2. What are 'rescue cases'? Is a rescuer *volenti*?  *(4)*
3. Distinguish a contract of service from a contract for service.  *(4)*
4. Explain the doctrine of *res ipsa loquitur*.  *(4)*
5. What is meant by 'deficiency in service'?  *(4)*

### Section B (short answer) · 2 × 10 = 20 marks
*Attempt any two questions. Each answer must not exceed 200 words.*

6. Briefly explain the maxim *injuria sine damno* with the help of decided cases.  *(10)*
7. Discuss vicarious liability with reference to the master-servant relation.  *(10)*
8. What defences are open in an action based on nuisance?  *(10)*

### Section C (long answer) · 3 × 20 = 60 marks
*Attempt any three questions. The answer is required in detail.*

9. Discuss the rule in *Rylands v. Fletcher*. Are there any exceptions to the rule? Discuss.  *(20)*
10. In what classes of cases and on what grounds may one person sue and be sued for a tort committed to and by another? Discuss the master's liability for his servant's wrongs.  *(20)*
11. Who is a consumer? What is meant by deficiency in service? Explain with the help of decided cases.  *(20)*
12. What do you mean by negligence? Explain, with the help of the two rival theories, the nature and definition of negligence. What are its essentials?  *(20)*
13. Define defamation and discuss its essentials. What defences are available in an action for defamation?  *(20)*

## Choice strategy
- **Section B and C:** choose the questions where you can name the leading case and the section. A question you half-know costs more marks than one you know well.
- **Problem questions** (as in December 2025 Q7 and Q10): state the facts in a line, the issue, the rule with a case, apply it to the facts, and give a conclusion (IRAC). Do not copy the textbook answer to a different question.
- **Consumer questions:** quote the section of the **2019 Act** (for example s. 2(7), s. 2(11), s. 34, s. 39). If the question uses the old name "Forum", write "District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum (now Commission)".
- **Do not exceed the word limits in A and B.** A good A answer is 60–75 words.
- Add one **Indian case** and one **criticism or recent development** to earn the last marks in Section C (see the [digest](cases-and-statutes-digest.md)).
