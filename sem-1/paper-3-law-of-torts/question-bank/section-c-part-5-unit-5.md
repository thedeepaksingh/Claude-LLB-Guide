## C-22 · Who is a consumer? What is meant by deficiency in service? Explain with the help of decided cases

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q11 (20 marks; this wording). Short forms: Dec 2023 Q3 (A-115, "What is meant by 'Deficiency in Service'?"), Dec 2023 Q6 (B-51, contract of service and for service), Dec 2025 Q3 (A-44). Related: A-111–A-118, B-53, B-54.
**Marks:** 20 · **Time:** about 30 minutes
**Model rubric (mine):** consumer: definition and limbs 4 · exclusions and commercial purpose with cases 4 · who may complain 1 · service and exclusions 3 · deficiency (and defect) with cases 6 · conclusion 2

### Answer

**Introduction.** The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 gives a cheap and quick remedy to a consumer against a defect in goods or a deficiency in service. It replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and applies to all goods and services, unless a Central notification excludes them.

**1. Who is a consumer? (s. 2(7))** A consumer is a person who:
1. **buys any goods** for a consideration which has been paid, promised, partly paid and partly promised, or under a system of deferred payment, and includes any **user** of such goods other than the buyer, **when the use is with the approval of the buyer**; or
2. **hires or avails of any service** for a consideration on the same terms, and includes any **beneficiary** of the service other than the person who hires it, **when the service is availed of with the approval of the first-mentioned person** ‡.

*Exclusions.* A person who obtains goods **for resale** or for any **commercial purpose**, or avails of a service **for any commercial purpose**, is **not** a consumer. *Explanation (a):* "commercial purpose" does **not** include the use by a person of goods bought and used by him **exclusively for earning his livelihood by self-employment**, and the same applies to services. *Explanation (b):* buying goods and hiring services includes **offline or online transactions** through electronic means, teleshopping, direct selling or multi-level marketing ‡.

*Cases on commercial purpose and the consumer*
- ***Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute*** (1995) 3 SCC 583. The question of commercial purpose is one of **fact**, decided by the dominant purpose. Goods bought for large-scale profit-making are for a commercial purpose; a purchase for **self-employment** by a person earning his living, is not †. The 1993 amendment's Explanation, now in s. 2(7), reflects the decision.
- ***Cheema Engineering Services v. Rajan Singh*** (1997) 1 SCC 131 †: a person who bought machinery to earn his livelihood by self-employment was a consumer.
- ***Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia*** (1998) 4 SCC 39: parents of a child patient could complain about negligent treatment; the child, as the beneficiary, was a consumer.
- ***Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta*** (1994) 1 SCC 243: an allottee of a house or site from a development authority is a consumer of its service.

*Who may file a complaint (ss. 2(5), 35(1))*: a consumer; a **recognised consumer association**; one or more consumers with the **same interest** (representative complaint, with permission); the Central or State Government; the **CCPA**; a **legal heir** of a deceased consumer; the **parent or guardian** of a minor ‡.

**2. "Service" (s. 2(42))** means service of any description which is made available to potential users, including **banking, financing, insurance, transport, processing, supply of electrical or other energy, telecom, boarding or lodging, housing construction, entertainment, amusement, and the purveying of news or other information**. It **does not include** (a) a service rendered **free of charge**, or (b) a service under a **contract of personal service** ‡.

*Contract of service v. contract for service.* A **contract of service** (employer-employee; personal service) is excluded, for an employee cannot complain against the employer of a deficiency in the employer's service. A **contract for services** (an independent contractor: a builder, an architect, a doctor in private practice) is included if paid for. The test is control (*Dharangadhara*), integration, or the mixed test (Unit 3).

*Cases*
- ***Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha*** (1995) 6 SCC 651. Medical services given for payment are "service". The Court divided doctors' services into: (a) free to all patients: **excluded**; (b) paid by all: **included**; (c) free for some and paid by others: **included** for all, since the paying patients cover the cost. A doctor in a contract of personal service with an employer (a salaried doctor) is excluded; the hospital is the service provider.
- ***Regional Director, ESIC v. Francis De Costa*** (1996) 6 SCC 1 †: medical benefits under the ESI scheme were **not** service for consideration, since the contribution is statutory.
- ***Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS National Institute of Communicable Diseases*** (2024 INSC 410): the services of **advocates** are not "service", as the profession is *sui generis* and the relationship is personal service. The Court also said that *V.P. Shantha* should be reconsidered by a larger bench ‡. On 10 February 2026 a bench issued notice on a petition seeking to exclude doctors ‡; no decision has been found, so *Shantha* remains the law.

**3. Deficiency in service (s. 2(11))** means **any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law, or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service**. It includes (i) **any act of negligence, omission or commission** by the person which causes loss or injury to the consumer, and (ii) **deliberate withholding of relevant information** from the consumer ‡.

**Elements:** (a) a service; (b) a standard set by law, contract or undertaking; (c) a shortfall; (d) loss or injury to the consumer.

*Related: "defect" in goods (s. 2(10))* means any fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the **quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard** required by law or claimed by the trader ‡.

| | Defect | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Goods | Services |
| Nature | Quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard | Quality, nature, manner of performance |
| Includes | — | Negligence; deliberate withholding of information |
| Relief | Remove the defect, replace, refund (s. 39) | Remove the deficiency; compensation |

*Cases on deficiency*
- ***Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta*** (1994) 1 SCC 243: delay in delivery of houses, defective construction and **harassment** by officers is deficiency; the Commission may award compensation, recoverable from the erring officer.
- ***Spring Meadows Hospital*** (1998): negligent treatment in a nursing home is deficiency; the hospital is liable for its staff.
- ***Ghaziabad Development Authority v. Balbir Singh*** (2004) 5 SCC 65 †: compensation for delay in giving possession may include interest and a sum for **mental agony**.
- ***Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure v. Govindan Raghavan*** (2019) 5 SCC 725 and ***Ireo Grace Realtech v. Abhishek Khanna*** (2021) 3 SCC 241: a builder's one-sided terms and long delay in handing over a flat are deficiency and an unfair contract; the buyer need not wait indefinitely and may claim a refund with interest.
- ***Imperia Structures v. Anil Patni*** (2020) 10 SCC 783: a buyer may go to the consumer forum despite RERA, as the remedies are concurrent.
- Examples from practice: delay in clearing a cheque by a bank; wrongful repudiation of an insurance claim; failure of a telecom operator to give agreed service; excess billing for energy.

**4. Reliefs (s. 39(1))** include removal of the defect or deficiency, replacement, refund of price with interest, **compensation** for loss, injury, mental agony and harassment, punitive damages in suitable cases, discontinuance of unfair trade practices, corrective advertisement, and costs ‡.

**Conclusion.** A consumer is a person who buys goods or hires services for consideration, other than for resale or commercial purpose; users and beneficiaries with the buyer's approval are included, and livelihood by self-employment is not commercial. "Service" covers most paid services, except free services and personal service. Deficiency is any shortfall in the standard of service, including negligence and withholding of information. The Supreme Court has extended the Act to housing and medical services, and excluded advocates, with the position of doctors under reconsideration.

## C-23 · Discuss the powers and jurisdiction of the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum (Commission). How do the State and National Commissions differ?

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q11 ("Discuss the powers and Jurisdiction of District Consumer redressal forum", 20 marks). Short forms: Dec 2024 Q7 (B-52, "Write a short note on District Consumer redressal forum"), Dec 2022 Q4 (A-122, composition of the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum). Related: A-122–A-128, B-52, B-58.
**Marks:** 20 · **Time:** about 30 minutes
**Model rubric (mine):** composition 3 · jurisdiction: pecuniary, territorial, others 5 · powers 5 · procedure, reliefs and enforcement 4 · State and National Commissions 2 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Introduction.** The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 provides a three-tier system of quasi-judicial bodies: the **District Commission** (s. 28; called the District Forum under the 1986 Act), the **State Commission** (s. 42) and the **National Commission** (s. 53). They are not bound by every technicality of the Code of Civil Procedure, and are meant to give a speedy, cheap remedy.

**1. Composition of the District Commission (s. 28)** ‡. A **President** and **not less than two other members**, one of whom must be a **woman**, appointed by the State Government. The President is a person who is, has been, or is qualified to be, a **District Judge**. The term is **four years or up to the age of 65**, whichever is earlier (Consumer Protection (Qualification…) Rules, 2020 †). *(Under the 1986 Act the District Forum had a President, being or having been a District Judge, and two other members, one a woman †.)*

**2. Jurisdiction of the District Commission**

*(a) Pecuniary (s. 34(1)).* By **value of the consideration paid** (not the compensation claimed). The Act fixed ₹1 crore. The **Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021** (30 December 2021) reduced it to **₹50 lakh** ‡.

*(b) Territorial (s. 34(2)).* The complaint may be filed where: (i) the opposite party (or each of them, where there are several) **ordinarily resides, carries on business, has a branch office or personally works for gain**; (ii) any one of several opposite parties does so, if the others agree or the Commission permits; (iii) the **cause of action arises**, wholly or in part; or (iv) the **complainant resides or personally works for gain** ‡. The last is new in 2019.

*(c) Subject-matter.* Complaints of: (i) an unfair or restrictive trade practice; (ii) defective goods; (iii) deficiency in service; (iv) charging a price in excess of the price fixed by law or displayed; (v) goods hazardous to life and safety offered for sale in contravention of law; (vi) a **product liability** claim (s. 2(6)); and similar. Complaints about **unfair contracts** go to the State and National Commissions †.

*(d) Review (s. 40).* It can review its own order for an error apparent on the face of the record, within 30 days ‡.

**3. Powers**
1. **Civil-court powers (s. 38(9))** for summoning and enforcing attendance of any defendant or witness and examining him on oath; discovery and production of documents; receiving evidence on **affidavits**; requisitioning reports of analysis or tests from a **laboratory**; issuing commissions for examination of witnesses; and other prescribed matters ‡.
2. **Judicial proceedings:** proceedings are deemed judicial proceedings, and the Commission a civil court for the purposes of s. 195 and Chapter XXVI of the CrPC (BNSS equivalent) †.
3. **Interim orders** while the complaint is pending.
4. **Orders on the merits (s. 39(1))**: remove the defect or deficiency; replace the goods; **refund the price**; pay **compensation** for loss or injury and for mental agony; **punitive damages** in a fit case; discontinue the unfair or restrictive practice; withdraw hazardous goods from sale; issue **corrective advertisement**; pay a sum for the benefit of unidentified consumers (at least 25% of the value of goods or services sold); pay **costs** ‡.
5. **Enforcement (s. 71)**: execution as a **decree** of a civil court; and **penalty (s. 72)**: imprisonment of **one month to three years**, or a fine of **₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh**, or both, for non-compliance; for these purposes the Commission has the powers of a **Magistrate of the first class** ‡.
6. **Mediation (s. 37)**: the Commission may refer the parties to mediation if they agree.

**4. Procedure in outline (ss. 35–38)** ‡
1. Complaint with the fee (none up to ₹5 lakh †), in writing or **electronically**.
2. The Commission decides admissibility within **21 days**; otherwise it is deemed admitted.
3. Notice to the opposite party, who must reply within **30 days**, extendable by 15 days.
4. Evidence by **affidavit** and documents; testing of goods in a laboratory where necessary.
5. Decision **within three months** of notice (five months where testing is needed).
6. **Limitation: two years** from the cause of action; delay may be condoned for sufficient cause (s. 69).

**5. Appeals.** From the District Commission to the **State Commission** within **45 days** (s. 41); the appellant who has to pay must deposit **50%** of the amount ordered. From the State Commission to the **National Commission** within 30 days (s. 51), and from the National Commission to the **Supreme Court** within 30 days (s. 67), each with the same deposit. No appeal lies against an order on a mediated settlement ‡.

**6. Comparison of the three Commissions**

| | District | State | National |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section | s. 28 | s. 42 | s. 53 |
| Composition | President (District Judge) + at least 2 members (one a woman) | President (High Court Judge) + at least 4 members | President (Supreme Court Judge) + at least 4 members † |
| Original jurisdiction (consideration paid), after the 2021 Rules | up to ₹50 lakh | above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore | above ₹2 crore |
| Unfair contracts | — | up to ₹10 crore | above ₹10 crore |
| Appellate jurisdiction | — | Appeals from District (s. 41, via s. 47) | Appeals from State (s. 51) and from the CCPA (s. 24) |
| Revisional | — | Over District Commissions | Over State Commissions |
| Further appeal | State Commission | National Commission | Supreme Court |

**Conclusion.** The District Commission is the court of first instance for most consumer disputes up to ₹50 lakh. It has the powers of a civil court, can grant a wide range of reliefs, and its orders are enforceable as decrees and by penal sanctions. Appeals go to the State and National Commissions and finally to the Supreme Court.

## C-24 · How is a complaint written? Write down the procedure for formulating a complaint before the District (or State) Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q13 ("How complaint is written? Write down the procedure of formulation of the complaint before State Consumer Dispute Redressal Forum") and Dec 2022 Q10 (the same for the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum), both 20 marks. Related: A-114, B-58.
**Marks:** 20 · **Time:** about 30 minutes
**Model rubric (mine):** who may complain, grounds and pre-filing steps 4 · format and contents of the complaint 8 · procedure after filing 4 · State Commission differences and appeals 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Introduction.** A consumer complaint is a **written statement of the grievance**, drafted like a plaint, in clear and simple language. A lawyer is not required. The complaint is made under s. 35 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, to the Commission with jurisdiction.

**1. Who may complain, and about what (s. 35(1), s. 2(6)).** A consumer; a recognised consumer association; one or more consumers with the same interest; the Central or State Government; the CCPA; the legal heir of a consumer; the parent or guardian of a minor. The grounds (s. 2(6)): an **unfair or restrictive trade practice**; **defects** in the goods; **deficiency** in the service; **excess price** charged; goods hazardous to life and safety offered for sale; a **product liability** claim ‡.

**2. Steps before drafting**
1. Collect the **documents**: bill, receipt, invoice, warranty card, policy, correspondence, photographs.
2. Write to the opposite party and, ordinarily, send a **legal notice** giving a reasonable time to put the matter right. It is not a legal requirement but is good practice.
3. Choose the **right Commission**: by **value of the consideration paid** (District up to ₹50 lakh; State above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore; National above ₹2 crore: Rules, 2021 ‡) and by **place** (s. 34(2): where the opposite party resides or works; where the cause of action arose; or where the complainant resides or works for gain) ‡.
4. Check **limitation**: two years from the cause of action (s. 69), with condonation of delay for sufficient cause ‡.

**3. Format and contents of the complaint (in order)**
1. **Heading:** "Before the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, [place]" (or the State Commission). The Dec 2022 paper uses "Forum" (old name).
2. **Cause title:** Consumer Complaint No. ___ of 20__. *[Name, address, age and occupation of the complainant]* v. *[Name and address of the opposite party: the manufacturer, seller, service provider]*.
3. **Jurisdiction:** pecuniary and territorial grounds, and that the complaint is within time.
4. **Facts:** in numbered paragraphs in time order: (a) the purchase or hiring; (b) the price and payment (bill number and date); (c) the defect or deficiency; (d) complaints made and the replies; (e) the legal notice and the reply (or silence).
5. **Cause of action:** the date and place at which it arose and any continuing wrong.
6. **Legal grounds:** that the complainant is a **consumer** (s. 2(7)); the goods are **defective** (s. 2(10)) or the service has **deficiency** (s. 2(11)); that there is an **unfair trade practice** (s. 2(47)), etc.
7. **Prayer (reliefs), under s. 39:** (a) replace the goods or remove the defect, or refund ₹__ with interest; (b) pay ₹__ as compensation for loss, mental agony and harassment; (c) pay ₹__ as costs; (d) any other relief.
8. **Valuation and fee:** the value of the consideration paid and the fee (nil up to ₹5 lakh †; the Rules fix a graded fee).
9. **Verification and affidavit** by the complainant; **list of documents** annexed; copies for each opposite party; **signature** of the complainant (or authorised agent or advocate).
10. **Filing:** in person, by registered post, or **online** (e-Daakhil, since succeeded by the e-Jagriti portal †).

**Model skeleton (the names and amounts are placeholders):**

> BEFORE THE DISTRICT CONSUMER DISPUTES REDRESSAL COMMISSION, [PLACE]
> Consumer Complaint No. ___ of 20__
> [Complainant], r/o [address] …… Complainant
> Versus
> [Dealer / Manufacturer / Service provider], [address] …… Opposite Party
> COMPLAINT UNDER SECTION 35 OF THE CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019
> The complainant respectfully states: 1. That on [date] the complainant bought [goods] from the opposite party for ₹[price], paid in full, bill no. [__] (Annexure A). 2. That the goods developed [defect] on [date] within the warranty period (Annexure B). 3. That the complainant informed the opposite party on [dates], but no remedy was given (Annexures C–D). 4. That a legal notice was sent on [date] and received on [date]; there was no reply (Annexure E). 5. That the goods are defective within s. 2(10) and the opposite party has committed deficiency in service within s. 2(11). 6. That the Commission has jurisdiction under s. 34 as [reason]; the claim is within two years (s. 69). 7. That the cause of action arose on [date] at [place]. PRAYER: that the opposite party be directed to (a) replace the goods or refund ₹[ ] with interest; (b) pay ₹[ ] as compensation for mental agony and harassment; (c) pay ₹[ ] as costs. Place/Date. Complainant. VERIFICATION and AFFIDAVIT.

**4. Procedure after filing (ss. 36–39)** ‡
1. The Commission examines **admissibility** within **21 days**; it cannot reject without hearing the complainant (s. 36).
2. **Mediation** may be suggested (s. 37).
3. **Notice** to the opposite party, who must file a **written version within 30 days**, extendable by 15 days (s. 38).
4. The complainant may file a **rejoinder** and affidavits; documents and evidence by affidavit; goods may be sent for **laboratory analysis**.
5. **Hearing** and arguments; **final order** within three months (five if testing is needed).
6. **Review** within 30 days (s. 40); **appeal** within 45 days to the State Commission with 50% deposit of the amount ordered (s. 41); **enforcement** as a decree (s. 71); **penalty** for non-compliance: one month to three years, or ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh, or both (s. 72).

**5. Complaint before the State Commission (Dec 2024 Q13)**
- **Original complaint** where the consideration exceeds ₹50 lakh but not ₹2 crore (2021 Rules), or an **unfair contract** claim not exceeding ₹10 crore: drafted in the same form but addressed "Before the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, [State]", the fee is higher, and jurisdiction is shown under s. 47.
- **Appeal against a District Commission order (s. 41):** a **memorandum of appeal** within 45 days, headed "Appeal No. __ of 20__ against the order dated __ in Complaint No. __", with the parties as appellant and respondent, a **certified copy** of the order, the facts, **grounds of appeal**, a prayer to set aside or modify the order, an application for condonation of delay if needed, and proof of **deposit of 50%** of the amount ordered.

**Conclusion.** A complaint must show who the parties are, why the Commission has jurisdiction, the facts, the defect or deficiency, the cause of action and the relief claimed, with the proof annexed. The procedure is simple, there is no need for a lawyer, and the Commission is required to decide within three months.

## C-25 · Explain the new features of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019: the Central Consumer Protection Authority, product liability, unfair contracts and e-commerce

**Asked as:** not yet set at 20 marks. Syllabus line: "enforcement of consumer rights". Related: A-119–A-121, A-129–A-135, B-55–B-60.
**Marks:** 20 · **Time:** about 30 minutes
**Model rubric (mine):** why a new Act 2 · CCPA 5 · product liability 5 · unfair contract and unfair trade practice 3 · e-commerce, mediation, jurisdiction and penalties 4 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Introduction.** The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 was replaced by the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (assent 9 August 2019; most provisions in force from 20 July 2020 †, and the Central Consumer Protection Authority from 24 July 2020 ‡). The new Act was needed because of online selling, misleading advertising, defective products and delay in the old forums. Its main additions are these.

**1. Definitions**
- **Consumer** now expressly includes persons buying **online**, by teleshopping, direct selling or multi-level marketing (s. 2(7), Explanation (b)) ‡.
- **Service** (s. 2(42)) lists **telecom** and **housing construction** among others ‡.
- **Unfair trade practice (s. 2(47))** ‡: an unfair method or deceptive practice to promote sale, use or supply: false or misleading statements, bargain-price advertising that is not genuine, prizes not intended to be given, goods that do not meet safety standards, **hoarding** to raise prices, **spurious goods**, failure to issue a bill, **refusal to take back defective goods or refund** within the stipulated time (30 days if none), and **disclosure of personal information** given in confidence.
- **Restrictive trade practice (s. 2(41))** †: a practice that imposes unjustified cost or restriction, such as tie-in conditions or delay in supplying goods.
- **Unfair contract (s. 2(46))** ‡: terms that cause a **significant change in the rights of the consumer**: excessive security deposits, disproportionate penalty for breach, refusal to accept early repayment of a debt on payment of penalty, **unilateral termination** without reasonable cause, one-sided assignment, unreasonable charges. A complaint about an unfair contract goes to the State or National Commission.

**2. Central Consumer Protection Authority (Chapter III, ss. 10–27)** ‡
- **Established** by the Central Government to promote, protect and enforce the rights of consumers **as a class**, and to regulate violation of consumers' rights, **unfair trade practices** and **false or misleading advertisements**. It has a Chief Commissioner and Commissioners and an **Investigation Wing** under a Director-General.
- **Powers:** to inquire or investigate on complaint or on its own motion; **class action**; to **recall** unsafe goods, order **reimbursement** of prices and discontinuation of practices; to issue **safety notices**; **search and seizure** (s. 22).
- **Misleading advertisements (s. 21):** it may order discontinuance or modification, and impose a **penalty on the manufacturer or endorser** of up to ₹10 lakh (up to ₹50 lakh for a subsequent contravention) and **bar an endorser** for up to one year (three years for a subsequent contravention) †.
- **Appeal** lies to the National Commission (s. 24).

**3. Product liability (Chapter VI, ss. 82–87)** ‡
- **Meaning (s. 2(34)):** the responsibility of a **product manufacturer, product service provider or product seller** to compensate for harm caused to a consumer by a **defective product** or by deficiency in related services.
- **Harm** includes property damage (other than the product itself), personal injury or illness, mental agony, and loss; a claim is made by a **product liability action** (s. 83).
- **Manufacturer (s. 84)** †: liable if the product has a **manufacturing defect** or **design defect**, deviates from the manufacturing specifications, does not conform to the **express warranty**, or lacks **adequate instructions or warnings** about danger. This is a form of strict liability: negligence need not be proved for the defect, in contrast with negligence in tort (*Donoghue v. Stevenson*).
- **Service provider (s. 85)** †: liable for faulty or imperfect service, inadequate instructions or warnings, negligence, or conscious withholding of information.
- **Seller (s. 86)** †: liable only if he exercised substantial control over design, testing or packaging; altered the product; made an independent express warranty; the manufacturer is unknown or outside the jurisdiction; or he failed to take reasonable care in assembling, inspecting or maintaining, or to pass on a warning.
- **Exceptions (s. 87)** †: misuse or alteration by the complainant; adequate warning given and ignored; certain sophisticated users and bulk purchasers.

**4. E-commerce and advertising.** The Consumer Protection (**E-Commerce**) Rules, 2020 ‡ require marketplace and inventory e-commerce entities to display seller details, appoint a grievance officer, and refrain from unfair trade practices. The **Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements, 2022** (9 June 2022) ‡ and the **Guidelines on Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023** † support the CCPA.

**5. Mediation (s. 37; Chapter V, ss. 74–81)** ‡: the Commission may refer the parties to mediation at the first hearing if they consent in writing; each Commission has a **Consumer Mediation Cell**; there is **no appeal** against an order on a mediated settlement.

**6. Procedure and jurisdiction**
- **Territorial jurisdiction** where the complainant resides or works for gain (s. 34(2)(d)); **e-filing** and **video-conference hearings**; admissibility within 21 days; **pecuniary limits** by consideration paid (District ₹50 lakh; State ₹2 crore; National above, after the 2021 Rules) ‡.
- **Consumer Protection Councils** at Central, State and District levels (ss. 3–8): advisory bodies †.

**7. Penalties (ss. 88–93)** †: imprisonment up to six months or a fine up to ₹20 lakh or both for non-compliance with the CCPA's directions; for a false or misleading advertisement, imprisonment up to two years and fine up to ₹10 lakh (five years and ₹50 lakh for a subsequent offence); and penalties for adulterated and spurious goods, rising to imprisonment for life if death results. For non-compliance with a Commission's order (s. 72): imprisonment of one month to three years, or a fine of ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh, or both ‡.

**8. Table: 1986 and 2019 Acts**

| | 1986 Act | 2019 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Bodies | Forums and Commissions | Commissions, plus the **CCPA** |
| Product liability | Not a chapter | Chapter VI |
| Mediation | No | Yes |
| E-commerce | Not covered by the text | Covered |
| Unfair contract | Limited | Defined (s. 2(46)) |

**9. Current position.** The Consumer Protection (Amendment) Bill, 2025 (Bill No. 39 of 2025) was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 5 December 2025 on unfair practices in digital marketplaces; it has not become law as far as I could confirm ‡†. The reference of the question whether doctors are covered by the Act is before the Supreme Court (notice of 10 February 2026) ‡.

**Conclusion.** The 2019 Act moves from a purely remedial law to a regulatory one: it adds a regulator (the CCPA) for class-wide action, a separate product-liability regime that does not need proof of negligence, a definition of unfair contracts, rules for e-commerce and mediation, and easier access to forums for complainants.
