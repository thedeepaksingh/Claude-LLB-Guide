## 5. How to use the bank

1. **Learn the framework first.** Read the [digest](cases-and-statutes-digest.md) Part 3 (topic to cases) and write each leading case with its year, facts in one line and holding from memory.
2. **Section A (compulsory, 20 marks).** Read the 135 four-mark answers at least twice. They are all 75 words or fewer, so each takes four to five minutes to write. Practise the definitions that keep returning: tort, *damnum sine injuria*, nuisance, *res ipsa loquitur*, strict liability, libel and slander, innuendo, deficiency in service.
3. **Section B (any two, 20 marks).** Learn at least twelve of the 60 ten-mark answers well; they are written to fit 200 words. Practise one **problem** (B-31) in IRAC form.
4. **Section C (any three, 60 marks).** Prepare at least ten of the 25 twenty-mark answers: C-02 (*damnum sine injuria*), C-19 (defamation), C-13 and C-15 (negligence and nervous shock), C-12 (joint tortfeasors), C-10 and C-11 (vicarious and State liability), C-22, C-23 and C-24 (consumer), C-04 (*Rylands*) and C-07 (*volenti*). Write each in 30–35 minutes with the headings, the leading cases and one Indian case.
5. **Test yourself.** Sit the [three mock papers](mock-exam-1.md) and mark with the [checklist](answer-review-checklist.md).
6. **The day before.** Do the currency check in the checklist (especially the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 limits, BNS section numbers and doctors under the Act) and re-read the Section A answers.

- **A guarantee about what will be asked.** The frequency table shows past patterns only.
- **Hindi-medium wording.** The papers are bilingual; the answers are in English.
