## How to use this pack

**Three-pass plan**
1. **Understand (pass 1):** read each unit once, in order. Torts is a paper of cases, so read the facts of each leading case.
2. **Memorise (pass 2):** use the quick-revision boxes and the one-page sheet below; write each rule, its leading case and one Indian case from memory.
3. **Practise (pass 3):** write the Section C questions under timed conditions, compare with the model answers in the [question bank](question-bank/README.md), then sit the three [mock papers](question-bank/mock-exam-1.md) and mark yourself with the [review checklist](question-bank/answer-review-checklist.md).

**Answer-writing template for a theory answer (Section C; shorten it to 200 words for Section B, 75 for Section A)**

| Step | What to write | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | The meaning or definition; the leading authority (Salmond, Winfield, the statute) | 2–3 lines |
| 2. Essentials | The numbered elements of the tort or defence | 4–8 lines |
| 3. Cases | For each essential, the leading case with two lines of facts and the holding; at least one Indian case | the bulk |
| 4. Exceptions, defences, remedies | What the defendant can say; damages, injunction, limitation | 4–6 lines |
| 5. Criticism and developments | One or two points; the modern position in India | 3–5 lines |
| 6. Conclusion | One or two lines | 2 lines |

**Template for a problem question (IRAC)**

| Step | What to write | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Facts | The relevant facts only, with the parties named A and B | 1 |
| Issue | The legal question, in one sentence | 1 |
| Rule | The tort, its essentials, the leading case | 3 |
| Application | Each essential applied to **these** facts; the defence or the contrary view | 4 |
| Conclusion | A clear answer ("A is liable; damages reduced for contributory negligence") | 1 |

**Habits that earn marks:** give the case name *and* year; state the facts in two lines; use the Latin maxim and then translate it; write the section number for every statute (BNS s. 356, CPA 2019 s. 2(42)); end with a short conclusion. In Section A, stop at 75 words.

## One-page revision sheet: rules and statutes

| Topic | The rule in one line |
|---|---|
| Tort | A civil wrong, other than a mere breach of contract or trust, remedied by an action for unliquidated damages (Salmond). A duty fixed by law and owed to persons generally (Winfield) |
| Tort and crime | Tort: private wrong, damages to the victim, no fixed list of wrongs. Crime: public wrong, punishment by the State, offences defined in the BNS |
| Tort and contract | The duty in tort is fixed by law and owed to all; in contract it arises from agreement and is owed to the parties |
| *Damnum sine injuria* | Loss without a legal wrong gives no action (*Gloucester Grammar School*, *Mogul*, *Bradford v. Pickles*) |
| *Injuria sine damno* | A legal wrong without loss is actionable without proof of damage (*Ashby v. White*) |
| Strict liability | *Rylands v. Fletcher*: a dangerous thing, brought for the defendant's own purposes, a non-natural use, escape from his land |
| Absolute liability | *M.C. Mehta* (1987): an enterprise engaged in a hazardous activity is absolutely liable, with **no** *Rylands* exceptions; compensation is related to its size |
| *Volenti* | Knowing the risk is not consenting to it (*Smith v. Baker*); consent must be free and to the risk of injury |
| Necessity, inevitable accident, Act of God | Justified harm to avoid a greater harm; an accident that care could not avoid; an extraordinary natural force that no foresight could guard against |
| Vicarious liability | The master is liable for the servant's torts in the course of employment; the tests are control and organisation; not for an independent contractor (with exceptions) |
| State liability | Sovereign function: immunity (*Kasturilal*); non-sovereign: liability (*P. & O.*, *Vidhyawati*); public-law compensation for breach of fundamental rights (*Rudul Sah*, *Nilabati Behera*) |
| Joint tortfeasors | Concerted action, agency or breach of a joint duty; each liable for the whole damage; a release of one releases all, a covenant not to sue does not |
| Negligence | Duty of care + breach + damage caused + not too remote. *Donoghue* (neighbour), *Caparo* (three-stage), *Wagon Mound* (reasonable foresight) |
| *Res ipsa loquitur* | The thing speaks for itself: raises an inference of negligence; only the evidential burden shifts (*Byrne v. Boadle*, *Henderson v. Jenkins*) |
| Contributory negligence | Reduces damages; not a complete defence (*Butterfield v. Forrester*; apportionment) |
| Medical negligence | *Bolam* test, *Bolitho* gloss; criminal liability needs gross negligence (*Jacob Mathew*; BNS s. 106(1)) |
| Nuisance | Unreasonable interference with the use and enjoyment of land (private) or with the public (public); locality, duration, sensitivity, malice (*Sturges*, *St Helen's*) |
| Trespass | Direct, intentional interference with person, land or goods, actionable without proof of damage |
| Defamation | A statement that lowers the plaintiff in the estimation of right-thinking people, referring to him, published to a third person; libel and slander; defences: justification, fair comment, absolute and qualified privilege |
| Consumer (CPA 2019, s. 2(7)) | Buys goods or hires services for a consideration; not for resale or a commercial purpose (self-employment for a livelihood is not commercial) |
| Service (s. 2(42)) | Service of any description made available to users, for a consideration; free service and a contract of personal service are excluded |
| Forum and limits | District Commission up to ₹50 lakh; State Commission above ₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore; National Commission above ₹2 crore (by the consideration paid); limitation two years (s. 69); appeals 45 / 30 / 30 days with 50% deposit |

| Statute | Key provision |
|---|---|
| Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 | s. 356 defamation (simple imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both, or community service); s. 106(1) causing death by negligence (5 years; 2 for a registered medical practitioner) |
| Limitation Act, 1963 | Arts. 72–78: one year for statutory acts, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution, libel, slander, loss of service and inducing breach of contract |
| Consumer Protection Act, 2019 | s. 2(5) complainant, 2(7) consumer, 2(11) deficiency, 2(42) service, 2(46) unfair contract, 2(47) unfair trade practice; Commissions (District s. 28); CCPA (from 24 July 2020); product liability (Chapter VI) |
| Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (2019 amendments in force from 1 April 2022) | No-fault compensation (₹5 lakh death, ₹2.5 lakh grievous hurt); the section numbers need checking |
