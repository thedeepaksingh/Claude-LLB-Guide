# Paper III — Glossary of Latin Maxims and Terms

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-29 (IST) · **Branch:** `paper/03-law-of-torts`
> Use these terms (and their spellings) in answers. Unit numbers show where each is developed. The meanings are standard; the cases named are in the [digest](question-bank/cases-and-statutes-digest.md).

## Latin maxims

| Term | Meaning | Where it matters | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Tortum* | Twisted, wrong (the root of "tort") | Meaning of tort | 1 |
| *Ubi jus ibi remedium* | Where there is a right, there is a remedy | The basis of the law of torts | 1 |
| *Injuria sine damno* | Violation of a legal right without actual loss: actionable | *Ashby v. White* | 1 |
| *Damnum sine injuria* | Loss without violation of a legal right: not actionable | *Gloucester Grammar School*; *Mogul*; *Bradford v. Pickles* | 1 |
| *Actio personalis moritur cum persona* | A personal action dies with the person | Death of a party; Indian Succession Act, 1925, s. 306; Fatal Accidents Act, 1855 | 1 |
| *Sic utere tuo ut alienum non laedas* | Use your own property so as not to harm another's | Nuisance; *Rylands v. Fletcher* | 1, 4B |
| *Volenti non fit injuria* | To a willing person no injury is done | Consent as a defence; *Smith v. Baker* | 2 |
| *Vis major* / *damnum fatale* | Superior force / loss by fate: Act of God | *Nichols v. Marsland* | 2 |
| *Salus populi suprema lex* | The welfare of the people is the highest law | Public necessity | 2 |
| *Qui facit per alium facit per se* | He who acts through another acts himself | Vicarious liability | 3 |
| *Respondeat superior* | Let the master answer | Vicarious liability of the employer | 3 |
| *Delegatus non potest delegare* | A delegate cannot delegate | The non-delegable duty of an employer | 3 |
| The King can do no wrong | The Crown is not liable in tort (the old rule) | State liability; Art. 300 | 3 |
| *Res ipsa loquitur* | The thing speaks for itself | Inference of negligence; *Byrne v. Boadle* | 4A |
| *Novus actus interveniens* | A new intervening act | Breaks the chain of causation | 4A |
| *Causa sine qua non* | A cause without which the harm would not have occurred | "But for" test; *Barnett v. Chelsea* | 4A |
| *Ex turpi causa non oritur actio* | No action arises from a base cause | Illegality as a bar | 2, 4A |
| *Trespass ab initio* | Trespass from the beginning | Abuse of a lawful entry | 4B |
| *Per se* / *per quod* | In itself / by reason of which: slander actionable without, or only with, special damage | Defamation | 4C |
| *Caveat emptor* | Let the buyer beware | The old rule of sale, displaced by consumer law | 5 |

## English terms

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Tortfeasor | A person who commits a tort | 1 |
| Joint tortfeasors | Persons liable for the same damage through concerted action, agency or a joint duty | 3 |
| Unliquidated damages | Damages not fixed in advance; assessed by the court | 1 |
| Nominal damages | A small sum awarded for a violation of a right without loss | 1 |
| Exemplary (punitive) damages | Damages awarded to punish and deter | 1, 4C, 5 |
| Strict liability | Liability without proof of fault, with exceptions (*Rylands v. Fletcher*) | 1 |
| Absolute liability | Liability for a hazardous activity with no exceptions (*M.C. Mehta*) | 1 |
| Vicarious liability | Liability for another's tort | 3 |
| Contract of service / contract for services | Employment under control / work by an independent contractor | 3, 5 |
| Sovereign function | An act that only the State can do; formerly immune | 3 |
| Public-law compensation | Compensation under Arts. 32 and 226 for breach of fundamental rights | 1, 3 |
| Duty of care | The legal obligation to take reasonable care towards a person who may be harmed | 4A |
| Neighbour principle | A duty is owed to persons so closely affected that one ought to have them in contemplation (*Donoghue v. Stevenson*) | 4A |
| Reasonable man | The ordinary prudent person whose conduct is the standard of care | 4A |
| Remoteness | The limit on liability for consequences; reasonable foreseeability (*Wagon Mound*) | 4A |
| Eggshell-skull rule | The defendant takes the plaintiff as he finds him | 4A |
| Contributory negligence | The plaintiff's own lack of care, which reduces his damages | 2, 4A |
| Nervous shock (psychiatric injury) | Recognised mental illness caused by what the plaintiff saw or heard | 4A |
| Private nuisance | Unlawful interference with the use or enjoyment of land | 4B |
| Public nuisance | An act that affects the public at large; also a crime | 4B |
| Abatement | The injured party's self-help to end a nuisance | 4B |
| Trespass | Direct and intentional interference with person, land or goods | 4B |
| False imprisonment | Total restraint of a person without lawful justification | 4B |
| Malicious prosecution | Prosecution without reasonable cause and with malice, ending in the plaintiff's favour | 4B |
| Deceit | A false statement made knowingly or recklessly, relied on, causing loss (*Derry v. Peek*) | 4B |
| Libel / slander | Defamation in permanent form / in transient form | 4C |
| Innuendo | A meaning beyond the ordinary one, known to those who read or hear it | 4C |
| Justification | Truth, as a defence in civil defamation | 4C |
| Fair comment | An honest opinion on a matter of public interest | 4C |
| Absolute / qualified privilege | Protection regardless of malice / lost by malice | 4C |
| Consumer | One who buys goods or hires services for a consideration, not for resale or a commercial purpose | 5 |
| Complainant | A person entitled to file a complaint (s. 2(5)) | 5 |
| Deficiency in service | Any fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the performance of a service (s. 2(11)) | 5 |
| Unfair trade practice | An unfair or deceptive method to promote the sale or supply of goods or services (s. 2(47)) | 5 |
| Unfair contract | A one-sided contract that significantly changes the consumer's rights (s. 2(46)) | 5 |
| Product liability | The liability of a manufacturer, service provider or seller for harm from a defective product (s. 2(34)) | 5 |
| CCPA | The Central Consumer Protection Authority, set up from 24 July 2020 | 5 |
