**LL.B. (Semester I) · Jurisprudence – I (Legal Theory) (K-1001)**  
**Time: 3 hours · Maximum marks: 100**

*Note: Answer all the questions in Section A. In Section B answer any two of the three questions. In Section C answer any three of the five questions.*

### Section A (very short answer) · 5 × 4 = 20 marks
*Attempt all questions. Each answer must not exceed 75 words.*

1. Explain Austin's classification of laws.  *(4)*
2. What is meant by *Volksgeist*?  *(4)*
3. What are 'jural postulates'? Give two examples.  *(4)*
4. Write a short note on Jerome Frank.  *(4)*
5. What is meant by 'base' and 'superstructure' in the Marxist theory of law?  *(4)*

### Section B (short answer) · 2 × 10 = 20 marks
*Attempt any two questions. Each answer must not exceed 200 words.*

6. Explain Stammler's theory of natural law with a variable content.  *(10)*
7. Explain Hart's distinction between primary and secondary rules.  *(10)*
8. Describe Maine's stages of legal development.  *(10)*

### Section C (long answer) · 3 × 20 = 60 marks
*Attempt any three questions. The answer is required in detail.*

9. Critically examine Austin's command theory of law. How far does it explain the Indian legal system?  *(20)*
10. Discuss the revival of natural law in the twentieth century, with reference to Stammler, Kohler and later writers.  *(20)*
11. 'The movement of progressive societies has hitherto been a movement from status to contract.' Examine the statement and show how far it applies in India.  *(20)*
12. Explain Roscoe Pound's theory of social engineering. How far has it influenced Indian law?  *(20)*
13. Write a detailed essay on the Realist movement in jurisprudence.  *(20)*

## Time plan (180 minutes)

| Part | Time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Read the paper and choose | 10 min | Count the thinkers, works and cases you can name for each question before you choose. |
| Section A (5 answers) | 25 min | About 5 minutes each: definition, two points, one thinker or case. |
| Section B (2 answers) | 35 min | About 17 minutes each: 150–200 words, with the thinker, the work and one criticism. |
| Section C (3 answers) | 95 min | About 30 minutes each: introduction, five or six headings, thinkers and cases, a short conclusion. |
| Final check | 15 min | Question numbers, names and years, word limits in A and B. |

## Choice strategy
- **Section B and C:** choose the questions where you can name the thinker, the book and one criticism. A question you half-know costs more marks than one you know well.
- **Do not exceed the word limits in A and B.** An answer far above the limit wastes time; a good A answer is 60–75 words.
- **Section C is on a compulsory-detail basis:** write the headings in the order the question asks. Add one Indian case (see the [digest](jurists-and-cases-digest.md)) and a critical assessment to earn the last marks.
