## How to use this pack

**Three-pass plan**
1. **Understand (pass 1):** read each unit once, in order, focusing on the background and the diagrams.
2. **Memorise (pass 2):** use the *quick-revision boxes* and the one-page sheet below; write each thinker's name, work, year, central idea and one criticism from memory.
3. **Practise (pass 3):** write the Section C questions under timed conditions, then compare with the model answers in the [question bank](question-bank/README.md); sit the [mock papers](question-bank/mock-exam-1.md).

**Answer-writing template for a 20-mark answer (Section C; shorten it to 200 words for Section B)**

| Step | What to write | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | One or two lines placing the topic and naming the thinker/work | 2–3 lines |
| 2. Definition / core idea | Exact definition or proposition | 3–5 lines |
| 3. Body | 4–6 numbered points; **diagram or table** where possible | the bulk |
| 4. Cases / illustrations | At least 2–3 relevant cases, preferably one Indian | 4–6 lines |
| 5. Criticism | 4–5 points | 5–8 lines |
| 6. Conclusion | Balanced one-liner or two | 2–3 lines |

**Habits that earn marks:** name the thinker *and* the book *and* the year; draw one diagram; use headings; write one Indian case; end with a short conclusion; underline key terms.

## One-page revision sheet: thinkers at a glance

| Thinker | Dates | Key work | Core idea (one line) | Main criticism (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ulpian** | d. 223 | Digest 1.1.10.2 | Jurisprudence = knowledge of things divine and human; science of the just and unjust | Too wide |
| **Aquinas** | 1225–74 | *Summa Theologiae* | Four laws: eternal, divine, natural, human; unjust law is perversion of law | Theological; vague |
| **Grotius** | 1583–1645 | *De Jure Belli ac Pacis* (1625) | Natural law valid even without God | Content uncertain |
| **Locke** | 1632–1704 | *Second Treatise* (1689) | Natural rights to life, liberty, property; government by consent | Abstract |
| **Stammler** | 1856–1938 | *Theory of Just Law* (1902) | Natural law with variable content; community of free-willing men | Too formal |
| **Kohler** | 1849–1919 | *Philosophy of Law* (1909) | Law serves civilisation; jurisprudence of civilisation | Vague "civilisation" |
| **Fuller** | 1902–78 | *Morality of Law* (1964) | Eight desiderata: inner morality of law | Procedural only |
| **Bentham** | 1748–1832 | *Intro. to Principles of Morals and Legislation* (1789) | Utility; expository v. censorial jurisprudence | Natural rights "nonsense upon stilts" |
| **Austin** | 1790–1859 | *Province of Jurisprudence Determined* (1832) | Law = command of sovereign + sanction | Custom; international law; power-conferring rules |
| **Kelsen** | 1881–1973 | *Pure Theory of Law* (1934/1960) | Hierarchy of norms; **Grundnorm** | Fiction; too formal; *Dosso* |
| **Hart** | 1907–92 | *Concept of Law* (1961) | Primary + secondary rules; rule of recognition | Dworkin: ignores principles |
| **Savigny** | 1779–1861 | *Of the Vocation of Our Age* (1814) | *Volksgeist*; law grows like language; against codification | Vague; undervalues legislation |
| **Maine** | 1822–88 | *Ancient Law* (1861) | Stages of law; **status → contract** | Narrow evidence; reversal to status |
| **Jhering** | 1818–92 | *Law as a Means to an End* | Law is a means to a social end | Utilitarian |
| **Ehrlich** | 1862–1922 | *Fundamental Principles of the Sociology of Law* (1913) | Living law in society | Hard to identify |
| **Pound** | 1870–1964 | *Scope and Purpose of Sociological Jurisprudence* (1911–12) | Social engineering; interests | No scale of values |
| **Duguit** | 1859–1928 | *Transformations of Public Law* (1913) | Social solidarity; property as social function | Vague; endangers liberty |
| **Holmes** | 1841–1935 | *Path of the Law* (1897) | Law = prediction of what courts will do; bad man | Circular; Hart's internal view |
| **Gray** | 1839–1915 | *Nature and Sources of the Law* (1909) | Law = rules courts lay down; statute is a source | Ignores legislation |
| **Llewellyn** | 1893–1962 | *Bramble Bush* (1930); *Cheyenne Way* (1941) | Law is what officials do; law-jobs | Overstates uncertainty |
| **Frank** | 1889–1957 | *Law and the Modern Mind* (1930) | Fact-scepticism; myth of certainty | Speculative psychology |
| **Marx / Engels** | 1818–83 / 1820–95 | *Manifesto* (1848); *Critique of Political Economy* (1859) | Base and superstructure; law as class instrument; withering away | Determinism; failed prediction |
