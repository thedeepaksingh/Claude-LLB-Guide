## C. Thinker statements: how to quote safely

Use **short quotations only**, with the author's name. If your textbook words a sentence differently, follow your textbook. The statements below are standard and widely quoted; the quotation marks show the shortest form to use.

| Thinker | Statement (shortest safe form) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ulpian | Jurisprudence is knowledge of things divine and human, the science of the just and the unjust | Digest 1.1.10.2 |
| Austin | "The existence of law is one thing; its merit and demerit another." | *Province of Jurisprudence Determined* |
| Bentham | Natural rights are "nonsense upon stilts" | *Anarchical Fallacies* |
| Hobbes | Life in nature is "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short" | *Leviathan* |
| Holmes | "The life of the law has not been logic: it has been experience." | *The Common Law* (1881) |
| Holmes | Law is a prophecy of what courts will do (the "prophecies" passage) | *The Path of the Law* (1897) |
| Holmes | The Constitution "does not enact Mr. Herbert Spencer's Social Statics" | *Lochner* (dissent) |
| Maine | The movement of progressive societies has been "from Status to Contract" | *Ancient Law*, ch. V |
| Marx and Engels | "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles." | *Communist Manifesto* |
| Marx and Engels | Jurisprudence is "the will of your class made into a law for all" | *Communist Manifesto* |
| Hart | Union of primary and secondary rules is "the key to the science of jurisprudence" | *Concept of Law* |
| E.P. Thompson | Rule of law is "an unqualified human good" | *Whigs and Hunters* (1975) |
| Marbury | It is "emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is" | *Marbury v. Madison* |

**Paraphrase (do not quote verbatim):** Ehrlich's "living law" passage; Savigny's growth-of-law passage (use your textbook's translation); Llewellyn's "what officials do" passage; Pound's definition of interests.
