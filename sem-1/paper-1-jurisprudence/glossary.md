# Paper I — Glossary of Key Terms

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-27 (IST) · **Branch:** `paper/01-jurisprudence`
> Use these terms (and their spellings) in answers. Unit numbers show where each is developed.

## Latin and Roman terms

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| *Jurisprudentia* | Knowledge of or skill in law (*juris* + *prudentia*) | 1 |
| *Jus civile* | Law of one particular state (Roman citizens) | 2 |
| *Jus gentium* | Law common to all peoples | 2 |
| *Jus naturale* / *lex naturalis* | Natural law | 2 |
| *Lex aeterna* | Eternal law (Aquinas) | 2 |
| *Lex divina* | Divine (revealed) law | 2 |
| *Lex humana* | Human (positive) law | 2 |
| *Lex iniusta non est lex* | An unjust law is not law | 2 |
| *Nemo judex in causa sua* | No one may be judge in his own cause | 2 |
| *Audi alteram partem* | Hear the other side | 2 |
| *Etiamsi daremus* | "Even if we were to suppose" (Grotius: natural law holds even without God) | 2 |
| *Patria potestas* | The Roman father's power over his family | 4 |
| *Damnum sine injuria* / *injuria sine damno* | Loss without legal wrong / legal wrong without loss (torts; appears in later papers) | — |

## English jurisprudential terms

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive law** | Law laid down by human authority | 3 |
| **Positivism** | The view that law's existence is a matter of social fact, not moral merit | 3 |
| **Command** | Austin: wish of a determinate superior, backed by threat of evil | 3 |
| **Sanction** | Evil threatened for disobedience | 3 |
| **Sovereign** | Determinate superior habitually obeyed, not obeying another | 3 |
| **Separation thesis** | Law and morals are conceptually distinct | 3 |
| **Primary rules** | Rules imposing duties (Hart) | 3 |
| **Secondary rules** | Rules of recognition, change and adjudication (Hart) | 3 |
| **Rule of recognition** | Ultimate criterion of validity, existing as officials' practice | 3 |
| **Internal point of view** | Accepting a rule as a standard of conduct | 3 |
| **Open texture / penumbra** | Uncertainty at the edge of a rule's meaning | 3 |
| **Efficacy** | Actual obedience to a legal order (Kelsen) | 3 |
| **Themistes** | Divinely inspired judgments of early kings (Maine) | 4 |
| **Legal fiction** | Assumption of a falsehood as true, not open to challenge | 4 |
| **Equity** | Body of principles claiming higher justice beside civil law | 4 |
| **Status / contract** | Position fixed by birth / rights from free agreement | 4 |
| **Custom** | Long-observed rule acquiring force of law | 4 |
| **Social engineering** | Pound: adjusting competing interests with least friction | 5 |
| **Interest** | Claim, demand or desire a person or group seeks to satisfy | 5 |
| **Jural postulates** | Assumptions of civilised society that law should secure | 5 |
| **Law in action** | Actual working of law, v. *law in books* | 5, 6 |
| **Social solidarity** | Interdependence of people (Durkheim, Duguit) | 5 |
| **Formalism / mechanical jurisprudence** | Deciding cases by logic from rules alone | 6 |
| **Rule-scepticism** | Doubt that rules determine decisions | 6 |
| **Fact-scepticism** | Doubt about the reliability of fact-finding (Frank) | 6 |
| **Paper rules / real rules** | Rules in books / rules actually applied (Llewellyn) | 6 |
| **Law-jobs** | Basic functions law performs in a group (Llewellyn) | 6 |
| **Dialectical materialism** | Change through conflict of opposites, driven by material conditions | 7 |
| **Historical materialism** | History driven by modes of production | 7 |
| **Base / superstructure** | Economic structure / law, State, ideology built upon it | 7 |
| **Relative autonomy** | Superstructure can influence the base | 7 |
| **Bourgeoisie / proletariat** | Owners / wage-labourers | 7 |
| **Withering away** | Disappearance of State and law in communism | 7 |
| **Coase theorem** | With zero transaction costs, bargaining yields efficiency whatever the initial legal rule | 7 |
