## 2. Bigamy: BNS s. 82 (IPC ss. 494, 495)

### 2.1 The text ‡

**s. 82(1).** "Whoever, having a husband or wife living, **marries in any case in which such marriage is void by reason of its taking place during the life of such husband or wife**, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to **seven years**, and shall also be liable to fine."

**Exception.** This sub-section does not extend to:
- any person whose **marriage with such husband or wife has been declared void by a court of competent jurisdiction**; nor
- any person who contracts a marriage during the life of a former husband or wife, if such husband or wife, at the time of the subsequent marriage, shall have been **continually absent from such person for the space of seven years, and shall not have been heard of by such person as being alive within that time**, provided the person contracting such subsequent marriage shall, **before such marriage takes place, inform the person with whom the marriage is contracted of the real state of facts so far as the same are within his or her knowledge**.

**s. 82(2) (IPC 495).** Whoever commits the offence under sub-section (1) **having concealed from the person with whom the subsequent marriage is contracted the fact of the former marriage**, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to **ten years**, and shall also be liable to fine.

(The wording follows the page reproducing the Act ‡ as far as I could read it; the Exception is summarised from the same page and from the IPC text.)

### 2.2 Essentials

1. The accused **has a husband or wife living** at the time of the second marriage (a **valid** first marriage subsisting).
2. The accused **marries again** (the second marriage is **solemnised**; see §2.3).
3. The second marriage is **void by reason of its taking place during the life** of the first spouse (under the personal law applicable, usually Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, s. 5(i) and s. 11 †).
4. The case does not fall within the **Exceptions**.
5. For **s. 82(2)**: the accused **concealed** the first marriage from the second spouse.

### 2.3 "Solemnised": the second marriage must be proved

- ***Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v. State of Maharashtra*** AIR 1965 SC 1564 †. The Court held that, for the purposes of s. 494, a marriage must be **solemnised**, that is, celebrated with proper ceremonies and in due form. A marriage under Hindu law is not solemnised unless the **essential ceremonies** (for example the *saptapadi* or the *datta homa*, as the custom requires) are performed; mere proof of a form of marriage or admission is not enough. Since the second marriage was not proved to have been performed with the essential ceremonies, the conviction was set aside.
- ***Kanwal Ram v. Himachal Pradesh Administration*** AIR 1966 SC 614 †: the same rule; the prosecution must prove that the essential ceremonies of the second marriage were performed. A mere statement by a witness that a marriage took place is not enough.
- ***Priya Bala Ghosh v. Suresh Chandra Ghosh*** (1971) 1 SCC 864 †: on proof of the first marriage and its subsistence.

### 2.4 Bigamy and conversion

- ***Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India*** (1995) 3 SCC 635 †. Hindu husbands, married under Hindu law, converted to Islam in order to marry again without dissolving their first marriage. The Supreme Court held that the **first Hindu marriage does not dissolve** by the husband's conversion; it can only be dissolved under the Hindu Marriage Act; the second marriage is therefore **void**, and the husband is guilty of **bigamy** under s. 494. The Court said that conversion for the purpose of contracting a second marriage is an abuse of religion, and it called on the Government to enact a **Uniform Civil Code** under Article 44.
- ***Lily Thomas v. Union of India*** (2000) 6 SCC 224 †: the Court clarified that its earlier decision did not infringe the freedom of religion, and the husband who converts and marries again remains liable for bigamy.
- **Personal laws.** A **Muslim man** may lawfully have up to four wives, so a second marriage by him is not void and not bigamy; a fifth marriage is void (on his personal law) and is bigamy (position as I understand it †). A **Christian** and a **Parsi** are bound by monogamy by statute (the Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872; the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936, s. 4 and s. 5 †). The Special Marriage Act, 1954, s. 44 punishes bigamy by those who marry under it †. The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, s. 17 makes a second marriage during the first spouse's life **void** and **punishable under ss. 494 and 495 IPC** (now the BNS s. 82) †. *State of Bombay v. Narasu Appa Mali* AIR 1952 Bom 84 upheld a State law prohibiting Hindu bigamy against a challenge that it discriminated against Hindus †.

### 2.5 Other points

- **Consent of the first spouse is no defence**: the first marriage is a valid marriage whether the first spouse agrees to the second or not †.
- **Void first marriage**: if the first marriage was itself void (for example because the accused was already married to someone else), there is no bigamy as to that marriage.
- **Cognizance** and **compoundability** of the offence are matters of procedure under the BNSS (the old Cr.P.C. ss. 198 and 320 treated it as an offence only a person aggrieved could complain of) †.
- **Not a continuing offence**: the offence is complete when the second marriage is solemnised.

### 2.6 Illustrations

1. **A, a Hindu with a living wife, secretly marries B in a temple with all ceremonies, telling B he is unmarried.** Bigamy with concealment: s. 82(2), up to 10 years.
2. **A's wife left him eight years ago and has not been heard of since; A tells C of this and marries her.** Within the Exception (absence for seven years, not heard of, disclosure).
3. **A, a Hindu, converts to Islam and marries B while his first wife is alive and undivorced.** Bigamy (*Sarla Mudgal*).
4. **A is prosecuted for bigamy but the prosecution only shows that A and B lived together as husband and wife; no ceremony is proved.** The second marriage is not proved to be solemnised: acquittal (*Bhaurao Lokhande*). A may be liable under other provisions (s. 81 or s. 83), or under the Domestic Violence Act, etc. †

## 3. Adultery: IPC s. 497 (struck down; omitted from the BNS)

### 3.1 The old law (IPC s. 497)

"Whoever has sexual intercourse with a person who is and whom he knows or has reason to believe to be the wife of another man, without the consent or connivance of that man, such sexual intercourse not amounting to the offence of rape, is guilty of the offence of adultery, and shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to five years, or with fine, or with both. In such case the wife shall not be punishable as an abettor." (Text from memory †.)

**Features.** (i) Only a **man** could be the offender (the "adulterer"); (ii) the **wife** was never punishable, even as an abettor; (iii) **consent or connivance of the husband** removed the offence; (iv) only the **husband** could complain (Cr.P.C. s. 198(2)); (v) a **married woman** could not complain about her husband's adultery.

### 3.2 Earlier decisions upholding s. 497

- ***Yusuf Abdul Aziz v. State of Bombay*** AIR 1954 SC 321: s. 497 does not violate Art. 14 or 15, because Art. 15(3) permits special provisions for women †.
- ***Sowmithri Vishnu v. Union of India*** (1985) Supp SCC 137: the Court rejected a challenge to s. 497 (the argument that the section should be gender-neutral was a matter for the legislature) †.
- ***V. Revathi v. Union of India*** (1988) 2 SCC 72: upheld s. 497 and Cr.P.C. s. 198(2) †.

### 3.3 *Joseph Shine v. Union of India* (2018)

***Joseph Shine v. Union of India*** (2019) 3 SCC 39 (decided on 27 September 2018; Constitution Bench, five judges, unanimous in result, with four opinions) †.

- **Facts and issue.** Joseph Shine, an Indian residing in Italy, filed a public interest petition challenging the constitutional validity of s. 497 IPC and s. 198(2) Cr.P.C.
- **Held:** s. 497 IPC and s. 198(2) Cr.P.C. are **unconstitutional** and struck down, for these reasons: (i) the section treats a woman as the **property of her husband** (the consent or connivance of the husband removes the offence), which offends **dignity** under Art. 21; (ii) it is **discriminatory** between men and women (**Art. 14**) and rests on gender stereotypes (**Art. 15**): only the man is punished and the wife is a victim; (iii) it denies a woman **autonomy and sexual privacy** (Art. 21); (iv) it punishes a private moral wrong in a way not justified by any public interest; (v) Art. 15(3) cannot be used to justify a provision that perpetuates stereotypes. *Yusuf Abdul Aziz*, *Sowmithri Vishnu* and *Revathi* were overruled.
- **Adultery remains a ground of divorce** under personal laws (for example Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, s. 13(1)(i)) and can be relevant in civil proceedings, but it is **no longer a crime** (the Court said that adultery is not a crime but may be a civil wrong). †

### 3.4 The BNS and adultery

- The BNS does **not** contain an offence of adultery ‡. The omission follows *Joseph Shine*.
- The **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs**, in its report on the Bill in November 2023, recommended restoring adultery as a **gender-neutral** offence, to protect the institution of marriage; the Government did not accept it, and the BNS as enacted is silent on adultery (from memory †).
- **Arguments for criminalising adultery (gender-neutral):** protects the sanctity of marriage; deters; the State has an interest in the institution of marriage; other countries criminalise it. **Against:** a matter of private morality and personal autonomy; the criminal law is a blunt instrument; it was used to harass; the civil remedy of divorce suffices; *Joseph Shine*.

## 4. Other marriage offences (BNS ss. 81, 83, 84)

1. **s. 81 (IPC 493) †.** Cohabitation caused by a man **deceitfully inducing a belief of lawful marriage**: a man who by deceit causes a woman who is not lawfully married to him to believe that she is lawfully married to him and to cohabit with him in that belief: up to 10 years and fine.
2. **s. 83 (IPC 496) †.** Whoever **dishonestly or with a fraudulent intention goes through the ceremony of being married**, knowing that he is not thereby lawfully married: up to 7 years and fine.
3. **s. 84 (IPC 498) †.** Enticing, taking away or detaining with criminal intent a **married woman**: the IPC offence protected the husband's "right" to his wife and has been criticised on the reasoning of *Joseph Shine*; the BNS keeps a section under this heading (I did not check whether the text was changed).
4. **s. 69 ‡ (heading).** Sexual intercourse by **deceitful means**, including a false promise of marriage (new): see *Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v. State of Maharashtra* (2019) 9 SCC 608, which drew the line between a **false promise** (no intention to marry from the start: consent vitiated) and a **breach of a promise** (a genuine promise later broken: not rape) †.
5. **s. 85, 86 ‡: cruelty by husband or relatives** (3 years): Unit 4A-2.
6. **The marital-rape exception.** Exception 2 to s. 63 (rape) says that sexual intercourse by a man with his own wife, the wife not being under 18, is not rape (IPC s. 375, Exception 2, read down in *Independent Thought v. Union of India* (2017) 10 SCC 800 so that the age is 18). Challenges to the exception on the ground that it violates Arts. 14, 15 and 21 are pending in the Supreme Court as far as I know; I did not check the current stage †. This lies outside the syllabus text but touches "marriage".

## 5. Criticism

1. **Bigamy.** The law is a patchwork of personal laws: a Hindu, Christian or Parsi man who marries a second wife is punished, a Muslim man generally is not; the Supreme Court has repeatedly asked for a Uniform Civil Code (*Sarla Mudgal*; *John Vallamattom v. Union of India* (2003) 6 SCC 611 †) while the legislature has not acted (Article 44).
2. **Proof of solemnisation.** The strict requirement of proof of ceremonies (*Bhaurao Lokhande*) lets many bigamists escape.
3. **Adultery.** Whether the State should punish consensual adultery at all, and whether a gender-neutral offence would be consistent with *Joseph Shine*.
4. **Section 84.** Still rooted in the idea of a married woman as the husband's concern.
5. **The conversion rule** raises Article 25 (freedom of religion) issues; the Court said in *Sarla Mudgal* and *Lily Thomas* that the rule only prevents an abuse.

## 6. Current developments (to 29 October 2026)

1. BNS s. 82 re-enacts ss. 494–495 IPC in the same terms; the punishments are the same (7 and 10 years) ‡.
2. The BNS has no adultery offence ‡.
3. A new offence of sexual intercourse by deceitful means, including a false promise of marriage (s. 69) ‡.
4. I did not find a Supreme Court decision since 1 July 2024 on s. 82. **Not checked** exhaustively.

## 8. Quick-revision box

- **Bigamy (BNS s. 82):** a living spouse + marrying again + second marriage void + solemnised; 7 years; with concealment 10 years. Exceptions: marriage declared void by a court; absence for 7 years and disclosure. *Bhaurao Lokhande* (ceremonies); *Sarla Mudgal* (conversion).
- **Adultery (IPC 497):** man only; wife not punishable; husband's consent removes it; struck down in *Joseph Shine* (2018); **not in the BNS**; Standing Committee suggested a gender-neutral version (Nov 2023); remains a ground for divorce.
- Related: ss. 81, 83, 84; cruelty ss. 85–86; false promise s. 69.
