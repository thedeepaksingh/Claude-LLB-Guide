## Latin maxims

| Maxim | Meaning | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| ***Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea*** | The act does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty | The basis of *mens rea* (Coke; *Fowler v. Padget*, 1798). The Code puts the mental element in each definition |
| ***Actus reus*** | The guilty act: the physical element of an offence (an act, an omission, or a state of affairs), with its consequence | Unit 1A |
| ***Mens rea*** | The guilty mind: intention, knowledge, rashness or negligence, dishonesty, fraud | Unit 1A; excluded in strict liability |
| ***Ignorantia juris non excusat*** | Ignorance of the law is no excuse | A mistake of law is no defence; only a mistake of fact in good faith excuses (BNS ss. 14, 17 †) |
| ***Ignorantia facti excusat*** | Ignorance (mistake) of fact excuses | Mistake of fact; *Tolson* (1889) |
| ***Doli incapax*** | Incapable of crime (by reason of age) | A child under seven (BNS s. 20; IPC s. 82) |
| ***Actus me invito factus non est meus actus*** | An act done by me against my will is not my act | Compulsion and involuntary acts (BNS s. 32 for threats) |
| ***De minimis non curat lex*** | The law does not concern itself with trifles | Acts causing slight harm (BNS s. 33; IPC s. 95) |
| ***Nullum crimen sine lege, nulla poena sine lege*** | No crime and no punishment without a law | Art. 20(1) of the Constitution: no ex post facto criminal law |
| ***Ex post facto*** | After the fact: a law made after the act | Art. 20(1); the BNS applies only to offences on or after 1 July 2024 |
| ***Autrefois acquit / autrefois convict*** | Previously acquitted / previously convicted | Double jeopardy (Art. 20(2); BNSS provisions †) |
| ***Volenti non fit injuria*** | To a willing person no injury is done | Consent (BNS ss. 25–31; IPC ss. 87–93) |
| ***Necessitas non habet legem*** | Necessity knows no law | Necessity (BNS s. 19; IPC s. 81); no defence to murder (*Dudley and Stephens*) |
| ***Qui facit per alium facit per se*** | He who acts through another acts himself | An innocent agent; abetment |
| ***Locus poenitentiae*** | A place for repentance: the point up to which a person may withdraw | Preparation and attempt |
| ***Cogitationis poenam nemo patitur*** | No one is punished for mere thought | Intention, the first stage of a crime |
| ***Mala in se / mala prohibita*** | Wrong in themselves / wrong because prohibited | Mens rea and strict-liability offences |
| ***Corpus delicti*** | The body of the offence: the fact that a crime has been committed | Proof of the offence |
| ***Bona fide / mala fide*** | In good faith / in bad faith | "Good faith" (BNS s. 2(11); IPC s. 52): due care and attention |
| ***Animus furandi*** | The intention to steal | Theft: dishonest intention at the time of moving the property |
| ***Causa causans / causa sine qua non*** | The operating cause / a cause without which the event would not have happened | Causation: death by negligence needs the proximate cause (*Kurban Hussein*) |
| ***Prima facie*** | On first appearance | Presumptions, e.g. dowry death |
| ***Ratio decidendi / obiter dictum*** | The reason for the decision / a remark in passing | Reading case law |
| ***Modus operandi*** | The manner of operation | Proof of identity and intention |

## English and Indian-law terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Offence** | A thing made punishable by the Sanhita or by any special or local law (BNS s. 2(24); IPC s. 40) ‡ |
| **Crime** | A legally prohibited act or omission, with a guilty mind, causing injury, punishable by the State. The Code does not define it |
| **Injury** | Any harm illegally caused to any person in body, mind, reputation or property (BNS s. 2(14); IPC s. 44) ‡ |
| **Dishonestly; fraudulently** | With the intention of causing wrongful gain to one person or wrongful loss to another (BNS s. 2(7)); with intent to defraud (s. 2(9)) ‡ |
| **Voluntarily** | By means that the person intended to employ, or knew or had reason to believe to be likely to cause the effect (BNS s. 2(33); IPC s. 39) ‡ |
| **Strict liability** | Liability without proof of *mens rea*, imposed by statute for social-welfare offences (*M.H. George*; *Hobbs*) |
| **Constructive liability** | Liability for an act done by another, because of a shared intention (BNS s. 3(5); IPC s. 34) or common object (BNS s. 190; IPC s. 149) |
| **Abettor; abetment** | One who instigates, conspires or aids; the offence of doing so (BNS s. 45; IPC s. 107) † |
| **Principal offender; accomplice; accessory** | The one who commits the offence; one who takes part in it; one who helps before or after (the Code uses "abettor") |
| **Attempt; preparation** | An act towards an offence that falls short of completion; the arranging of the means (BNS s. 62; IPC s. 511) † |
| **Culpable homicide; murder** | Causing death with the intention or knowledge that death is likely (BNS s. 100); culpable homicide that satisfies s. 101 and falls within no Exception ‡ |
| **Hurt; grievous hurt** | Bodily pain, disease or infirmity (BNS s. 114); one of the eight kinds in s. 116 ‡ |
| **Force; criminal force; assault** | Motion caused to a person or thing (BNS s. 128); force used without consent to commit an offence or cause injury, fear or annoyance (s. 129); a gesture or preparation causing apprehension of criminal force (s. 130) ‡ |
| **Wrongful restraint; wrongful confinement** | Obstructing a person from going in a direction in which he has a right to go (BNS s. 126); restraining so as to prevent him from going beyond limits (s. 127) ‡ |
| **Kidnapping; abduction** | Taking or enticing a child or a person of unsound mind out of lawful keeping, or conveying a person beyond India (BNS s. 137); compelling or inducing a person by force or deceit to go from a place (s. 138) ‡ |
| **Theft; extortion; robbery; dacoity** | Dishonest moving of movable property (s. 303); obtaining delivery by putting in fear of injury (s. 308); theft or extortion with hurt, restraint or fear (s. 309); robbery by five or more (s. 310) ‡ |
| **Criminal misappropriation; criminal breach of trust** | Dishonest conversion of movable property (BNS s. 314 †); dishonest misappropriation of property that has been entrusted (s. 316) ‡ |
| **Cheating; mischief; trespass** | Deceiving and dishonestly inducing delivery or an act (BNS s. 318); destroying or damaging property (s. 324); entry on property to commit an offence or annoy (s. 329) ‡ |
| **Unlawful assembly; riot; affray** | Five or more with a common object listed in the Code (BNS s. 189); the use of force or violence by it (s. 191); fighting in a public place disturbing the peace (s. 194) ‡ |
| **Cognisable offence** | One for which the police may arrest without a warrant and investigate without a magistrate's order (BNSS †) |
| **Bailable offence** | One in which bail can be claimed as of right (BNSS †) |
| **Compoundable offence** | One that the victim may settle with the accused, with or without the court's permission (BNSS †) |
| **Legal insanity** | Incapacity, by reason of unsoundness of mind, to know the nature of the act or that it is wrong or contrary to law (BNS s. 22; IPC s. 84) ‡ |
| **Right of private defence** | The right of every person to defend his body and property, and those of others, against an offence (BNS ss. 34–44; IPC ss. 96–106) ‡ |
| **Rarest of rare** | The test for the death sentence: life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed (*Bachan Singh*, 1980) |
| **Dowry death** | The death of a woman within seven years of marriage, other than in normal circumstances, after cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before (BNS s. 80; IPC s. 304B) ‡ |
| **Bigamy** | Marrying again during the life of a husband or wife, the marriage being void (BNS s. 82; IPC s. 494) ‡ |
