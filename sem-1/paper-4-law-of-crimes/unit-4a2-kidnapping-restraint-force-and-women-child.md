## 2. Wrongful restraint (BNS s. 126; IPC ss. 339–341)

### 2.1 The text

"Whoever voluntarily **obstructs** any person so as to **prevent** that person from **proceeding in any direction in which that person has a right to proceed**, is said wrongfully to restrain that person." (Wording from the IPC s. 339, repeated in s. 126(1) of the BNS ‡.) **Exception:** the obstruction of a private way over land or water, which a person in good faith believes he has a lawful right to obstruct, is not an offence under this section. **Illustration:** A obstructs a path along which Z has a right to pass, A not believing in good faith that he has a right to stop the path. Z is prevented from passing. A wrongfully restrains Z.

**Punishment (s. 126(2)) ‡:** simple imprisonment up to **one month**, or fine up to **₹5,000**, or both. (IPC s. 341: one month, or ₹500, or both.)

### 2.2 Essentials

1. **Voluntary obstruction** (intention or knowledge that the obstruction is likely to prevent the person).
2. The obstruction **prevents** a person from proceeding in a direction in which he **has a right to proceed**. It is enough that he is prevented from going **in one direction**, though he is free to go in another.
3. The obstruction must be a **real** prevention. Mere inconvenience or difficulty in proceeding is not enough.
4. The person has a **right** to proceed (a trespasser on private land has no right to proceed there).
5. **Not wrongful** if done in lawful exercise of a right (arrest, quarantine, a parent restraining a child, a private way where the accused honestly claims a right).

## 3. Wrongful confinement (BNS s. 127; IPC s. 340)

### 3.1 The text

"Whoever **wrongfully restrains** any person in such a manner as to **prevent that person from proceeding beyond certain circumscribing limits**, is said wrongfully to confine that person." (IPC s. 340; BNS s. 127(1) ‡.) **Illustrations (IPC):** (a) A causes Z to go within a walled space, and locks Z in. Z is thus prevented from proceeding in any direction beyond the circumscribing line of wall. A wrongfully confines Z. (b) A places men with firearms at the outlets of a building, and tells Z that they will fire at Z if Z attempts to leave the building. A wrongfully confines Z.

### 3.2 Punishment ‡

| Sub-section | Case | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| 127(2) | Wrongful confinement | Up to **1 year**, or fine up to ₹5,000, or both |
| 127(3) | Confined for **three days or more** | Up to **3 years**, or fine up to ₹10,000, or both |
| 127(4) | Confined for **ten days or more** | Up to **5 years** and fine not less than ₹10,000 |
| 127(5) onwards † | (IPC ss. 345–348): knowing that a **writ for liberation** has been issued; in **secret**; to **extort property** or compel an illegal act; to **extort a confession** | Increasing punishments (the IPC added two to three years; check the BNS terms) |

### 3.3 Essentials

1. **Wrongful restraint**, plus
2. **Total** prevention of movement: the person is kept within **limits** from which he cannot go in **any** direction. The limits may be small (a room) or large (an island, a town), but they must be **circumscribing**; a person who may leave by any route is not confined.
3. **Wrongfully**: without lawful authority.
4. **Means:** physical force, locking, threats, show of force, or fear (a guard with a gun).

*Bird v. Jones* (1845) 7 QB 742: a part of a public bridge was fenced off for a regatta. The plaintiff, who wished to go along the bridge, was stopped but was free to go back. It was held that there was no imprisonment, since he could go in the other direction; the case is the classic illustration of the difference between partial obstruction (restraint) and total confinement †.

## 4. Wrongful restraint and wrongful confinement compared

| Point | Wrongful restraint (s. 126) | Wrongful confinement (s. 127) |
|---|---|---|
| Relation | **Genus** | **Species**: confinement is an aggravated form of restraint |
| Prevention | Prevents a person from proceeding in **some direction**; other directions are free | Prevents from proceeding in **all directions**, beyond **circumscribing limits** |
| Limits | None | Circumscribing limits are essential |
| Nature of obstruction | Partial | Complete |
| Punishment | Up to **1 month**, or ₹5,000, or both | Up to **1 year**, or ₹5,000, or both; higher if three days or more, ten days or more |
| Aggravated forms | None | Many (writ, secret, to extort, confession) |
| Example | A blocks the road so Z's car cannot go ahead; Z can go back | A locks Z in a room |
| Conversion | Restraint becomes confinement when escape in every direction is cut off | – |

Every confinement involves a restraint; not every restraint is a confinement.

## 5. Force, criminal force and assault (BNS ss. 128–131; IPC ss. 349–352)

### 5.1 Force (s. 128; IPC 349)

A person is said to use **force** to another if he causes **motion, change of motion, or cessation of motion** to that other, or causes to any substance such motion, change of motion or cessation of motion as brings that substance into contact with any part of that other's body, or with anything that other is wearing or carrying, or with anything so situated that such contact affects that other's **sense of feeling**; provided that the person causing the motion, etc. does so by (i) his own **bodily power**; (ii) **disposing any substance** in such a manner that the motion occurs without any further act on his part or on the part of any other person; or (iii) **inducing any animal** to move, to change its motion or to cease to move. (Paraphrased from the IPC text, which the BNS repeats †.)

### 5.2 Criminal force (s. 129; IPC 350)

Whoever **intentionally uses force** to any person, **without that person's consent**, in order to the committing of any offence, or intending by the use of such force to cause, or knowing it to be likely that by the use of such force he will cause, **injury, fear or annoyance** to the person to whom the force is used, is said to use **criminal force** to that other. (Text from IPC s. 350 †.)

**Illustrations (IPC, paraphrased):** (a) Z is sitting in a moored boat. A unfastens the moorings, and the boat drifts down the stream: A has used force to Z by disposing substances so that the motion is produced, and, if without Z's consent and intending or knowing annoyance, criminal force. (b) Z is riding in a chariot; A lashes the horses, quickening their pace: A has used force by inducing the animals to change their motion. (c) Z is riding in a palanquin; A, intending to rob Z, seizes the pole and stops it: A has caused cessation of motion by his own bodily power and used criminal force.

### 5.3 Assault (s. 130; IPC 351)

Whoever makes any **gesture, or any preparation**, intending or knowing it to be likely that such gesture or preparation will cause any person **present** to **apprehend** that he who makes that gesture or preparation is **about to use criminal force** to that person, is said to commit an **assault**.

**Explanation.** **Mere words** do not amount to an assault. But the words which a person uses may give to his gestures or preparation such a meaning as may make those gestures or preparations amount to an assault. (Text from IPC s. 351 †.)

**Illustrations (IPC, paraphrased):** (a) A shakes his fist at Z, intending to cause Z to believe that A is about to strike him: assault. (b) A begins to unloose the muzzle of a ferocious dog, intending Z to believe he is about to set the dog on him: assault. (c) A takes up a stick, saying to Z, "I will give you a beating": the words alone, or the gesture alone, may not be assault, but the gesture explained by the words may be.

### 5.4 Punishment (s. 131; IPC 352) ‡

Whoever assaults or uses criminal force to any person otherwise than on **grave and sudden provocation** given by that person: imprisonment up to **three months**, or fine up to **₹1,000**, or both. (Explanation: grave and sudden provocation is not a mitigating circumstance if it is sought or voluntarily provoked as an excuse, or given by anything done in obedience to law, or by a public servant in lawful exercise of power, or in lawful exercise of private defence. The text is from IPC s. 352 †.)

### 5.5 Aggravated forms (from the IPC order †)

| BNS † | IPC | Offence |
|---|---|---|
| s. 132 | 353 | Assault or criminal force to **deter a public servant** from discharge of his duty |
| s. 74 | 354 | Assault or criminal force to a **woman** with intent to **outrage her modesty** |
| s. 76 | 354B | Assault or criminal force to a woman with intent to **disrobe** |
| s. 133 | 355 | Assault or criminal force with intent to **dishonour** a person, otherwise than on grave provocation |
| s. 134 | 356 | Assault or criminal force in attempt to commit **theft** of property carried by a person |
| s. 135 | 357 | Assault or criminal force in attempt wrongfully to **confine** a person |
| s. 136 | 358 | Assault or criminal force on **grave and sudden provocation** |

### 5.6 Differences

| Point | Force | Criminal force | Assault |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Causing motion, change of motion or cessation of motion | **Intentional** force **without consent**, with an offence, or intending (or knowing) injury, fear or annoyance | A **gesture or preparation** that causes **apprehension** of criminal force |
| Contact | Needed | Needed | **Not needed** |
| Offence by itself? | No | Punishable under s. 131 | Punishable under s. 131 |
| Mental element | None | Intention or knowledge | Intention or knowledge |
| Words | – | – | Words alone are not enough; words may explain a gesture |
| Victim must be present | – | – | **Present**, and must apprehend |

**Assault is the threat; criminal force is the application.** An assault can occur without any force (shaking a fist); criminal force can occur without any earlier assault (a push from behind).

**Assault and hurt.** Hurt requires bodily pain, disease or infirmity; criminal force need not cause pain (spitting at a man is criminal force). **Assault and the English tort or crime.** English cases: *Stephens v. Myers* (1830) 4 C & P 349 (a threat with a clenched fist across a table was an assault only if the person threatening had the present ability to carry it out) †; *R v. Ireland* [1998] AC 147 (silent telephone calls may amount to assault) †.

## 6. Kidnapping (BNS s. 137; IPC ss. 359–361) and abduction (BNS s. 138; IPC s. 362)

### 6.1 Kidnapping: two kinds ‡

BNS s. 137(1): kidnapping is of two kinds: **kidnapping from India** and **kidnapping from lawful guardianship**.

1. **Kidnapping from India** (s. 137(1)(a); IPC 360): whoever **conveys any person beyond the limits of India without the consent** of that person, or of some person legally authorised to consent on behalf of that person, is said to kidnap that person from India.
2. **Kidnapping from lawful guardianship** (s. 137(1)(b); IPC 361): whoever **takes or entices any child or any person of unsound mind**, out of the keeping of the **lawful guardian** of such child or person of unsound mind, **without the consent of such guardian**, is said to kidnap such child or person from lawful guardianship.
   - **Explanation:** "lawful guardian" includes any person lawfully entrusted with the care or custody of such child or other person.
   - **Exception:** the clause does not extend to the act of any person who in good faith believes himself to be the **father of an illegitimate child**, or who in good faith believes himself to be entitled to the lawful custody of such child, unless such act is committed for an immoral or unlawful purpose.

**Punishment (s. 137(2)) ‡:** imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to **seven years**, and fine.

**"Child" and the age.** The IPC required a male under **sixteen** or a female under **eighteen**. The BNS speaks of "any **child**", and "child" means a person under **eighteen** (BNS s. 2(3) ‡); so, for both sexes, the age is now **eighteen** (my reading of the two provisions; I did not find a case on the point under the BNS).

### 6.2 Essentials of kidnapping from lawful guardianship

1. **Taking or enticing.** *Taking* means causing the child to go, whether by force or by persuasion, and the accused must have played an **active part**; *enticing* means inducing the child by **exciting hope or desire** (*State of Haryana v. Raja Ram* (1973) 1 SCC 544 †). The consent of the **child is immaterial**.
2. **A child (under 18) or a person of unsound mind.**
3. **Out of the keeping of the lawful guardian**: the guardian must have had **keeping** (care and custody; not necessarily physical presence).
4. **Without the guardian's consent.**

**Cases.**

- ***S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras*** AIR 1965 SC 942 †. A girl of sixteen, who was living with her father, left his house on her own and met the accused, and went with her to register their marriage (facts from memory). The Supreme Court held that it is necessary to distinguish between **taking** and **allowing a minor to accompany**: where the girl leaves her guardian's custody of her own accord and the accused takes no active part in her leaving, there is no "taking"; kidnapping needs some **active participation** by the accused in the girl's leaving, by inducement or force; the accused was acquitted.
- ***State of Haryana v. Raja Ram*** (1973) 1 SCC 544 †: the object of the section is the protection of minors and persons of unsound mind and the **rights of guardians**; the Court said the words "takes or entices" are wider than a mere "allowing to accompany" and that an act of persuasion that overcomes the child's reluctance, or that **exerts influence** that brings about her leaving, is enough; a girl's consent is no defence.
- ***Thakorlal D. Vadgama v. State of Gujarat*** (1973) 2 SCC 413 †: the accused who induced a minor girl to leave her parents' custody, took her away and later married her was guilty; the Court said that enticement is an act of **inducement**, and held that the consent of the minor girl is immaterial.

### 6.3 Abduction: s. 138 ‡

"Whoever **by force compels**, or **by any deceitful means induces**, any person to **go from any place**, is said to **abduct** that person."

1. **Any person**, of any age (a man, woman, child or adult).
2. By **force** or **deceitful means** (not simple persuasion). If the person goes of his own free will, there is no abduction.
3. To **go from any place**: the movement is essential.
4. **Abduction is not an offence by itself.** The BNS provides **no punishment** in s. 138 for abduction as such. It is punishable only **when done with a particular intent**, as in ss. 87 (a woman, to compel her marriage), 140 (to murder; for ransom; to subject to grievous hurt or slavery or unnatural lust; secretly and wrongfully to confine) and similar provisions ‡ (s. 87 and s. 140(1)–(2) read; s. 140's other sub-sections †).
5. **Continuing offence.** Abduction is regarded as a **continuing** offence, since it continues so long as the abducted person is detained; kidnapping from lawful guardianship is **not** continuing, being complete when the child is taken (the standard textbook view, from Ratanlal & Dhirajlal †).

### 6.4 Kidnapping and abduction compared

| Point | Kidnapping | Abduction |
|---|---|---|
| Section | BNS s. 137 (IPC 359–361) | BNS s. 138 (IPC 362) |
| Victim | From India: any person. From guardianship: a **child (under 18)** or a **person of unsound mind** | **Any person**, of any age |
| Means | **Taking or enticing** (no force necessary) | **Force** or **deceitful means** |
| Consent | The **guardian's consent** is the test; the **child's consent is immaterial** | **Free consent** of the person removes the offence, since there is no force or deceit |
| Guardian | Taking must be out of the **lawful guardian's keeping** | Guardianship is not relevant |
| Intention | Not necessary | Intent is essential **to make it punishable** |
| Punishable by itself? | **Yes**: up to 7 years and fine (s. 137(2)) | **No**: only with a specified intention |
| Nature | Not continuing | Continuing |
| Place | From India or from guardianship | From any place |
| Overlap | A child induced by deceit may be both kidnapped and abducted | – |

**Cases of overlap.** If A, by deceit, induces a 15-year-old girl to leave her father's house, A has *kidnapped* her (enticing out of the keeping of the guardian) and *abducted* her (by deceitful means). If A induces an adult woman by force to go from her house, A has abducted her; there is no kidnapping, since she is not a child.

### 6.5 Aggravated forms (s. 139, 140, 141, 142) †

- **s. 139 (IPC 363A).** Kidnapping or maiming a **child for the purposes of begging** †.
- **s. 140(1) ‡ (IPC 364).** Kidnapping or abducting a person **in order that he may be murdered** or disposed of so as to be put in danger of being murdered: **imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment up to ten years**, and fine.
- **s. 140(2) ‡ (IPC 364A).** Kidnapping or abduction for **ransom** (threatening to cause death or hurt, or causing death or hurt, in order to compel the Government, a foreign State or any person to do or abstain from doing an act, or to pay a ransom): **death or imprisonment for life**, and fine. The Supreme Court upheld s. 364A in *Vikram Singh v. Union of India* (2015) 9 SCC 502 †.
- **s. 140 (other sub-sections) †:** kidnapping or abducting with intent secretly and wrongfully to confine (IPC 365); to subject to grievous hurt, slavery or the unnatural lust of any person (IPC 367).
- **s. 87 ‡ (heading) (IPC 366).** Kidnapping, abducting or inducing a **woman to compel her marriage**, etc. †
- **s. 141 † (IPC 366B).** Importation of a girl or boy from a foreign country for illicit intercourse.
- **s. 142 † (IPC 368).** Wrongfully concealing or keeping in confinement a kidnapped or abducted person.
- **s. 143 ‡ (IPC 370).** **Trafficking of person**: whoever, for the purpose of exploitation, recruits, transports, harbours, transfers or receives a person by threats, force, coercion, abduction, fraud, deception, abuse of power or inducement, commits trafficking; exploitation includes prostitution or sexual exploitation, slavery, servitude, forced removal of organs †. Punishment: RI of **seven to ten years** and fine (143(2)); higher tiers for trafficking of more than one person, of children, and for public servants ‡ (tiers beyond 143(2) not checked). See also Article 23 of the Constitution and the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956 †.

## 7. Dowry death (BNS s. 80; IPC s. 304B), cruelty (BNS ss. 85–86; IPC 498A) and stalking (BNS s. 78; IPC 354D)

### 7.1 Dowry death: s. 80 ‡

**s. 80(1).** Where the **death of a woman is caused by any burns or bodily injury or occurs otherwise than under normal circumstances** within **seven years of her marriage**, and it is shown that **soon before her death** she was **subjected to cruelty or harassment by her husband or any relative of her husband for, or in connection with, any demand for dowry**, such death shall be called "**dowry death**", and such husband or relative shall be deemed to have caused her death.

**Explanation.** "Dowry" has the same meaning as in section 2 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.

**s. 80(2) ‡.** Punishment: imprisonment for a term **not less than seven years**, which may extend to **imprisonment for life**.

**Essentials.**

1. The **death of a woman**, caused by **burns or bodily injury**, or otherwise than under **normal circumstances** (suicide, poisoning, hanging, drowning all qualify).
2. Within **seven years of marriage**.
3. **Soon before** her death she was subjected to **cruelty or harassment**.
4. By her **husband or a relative of the husband**.
5. In connection with a **demand for dowry**.

**Presumption.** Section 113B of the Evidence Act, 1872 (BSA, 2023, corresponding section †): when the question is whether a person has committed the dowry death of a woman and it is shown that soon before her death she was subjected by that person to cruelty or harassment for, or in connection with, any demand for dowry, the **court shall presume** that such person had caused the dowry death. The presumption is **mandatory** ("shall"), and it shifts the burden on the accused to rebut it.

**Cases.**

- ***Kans Raj v. State of Punjab*** (2000) 5 SCC 207 †: the expression "**soon before**" has no fixed time limit; there must be a **proximate and live link** between the cruelty or harassment and the death; the Court examined the conduct of the husband's family on the evidence (details from memory).
- ***Satbir Singh v. State of Haryana*** (2021) 6 SCC 1 †: the Supreme Court discussed the meaning of "soon before", the burden on the accused under s. 113B, and the way courts should frame charges under s. 304B along with an alternative charge under s. 302 IPC; it said that in dowry death cases the court should examine the entire evidence of cruelty and the circumstances of the death, and that the presumption operates once the foundational facts are proved.

**Dowry death compared with murder and abetment of suicide**

| Point | Dowry death (s. 80) | Murder (s. 101/103) | Abetment of suicide (s. 108) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Victim | A married woman | Any person | Any person |
| Cause of death | Burns, bodily injury or other than normal | Intentional or knowing killing | Suicide |
| Time | Within **7 years** of marriage | None | None |
| Presumption | Yes (BSA s. 118 †) | No | Presumption of abetment of suicide: Evidence Act s. 113A (BSA s. 117 †) within 7 years of marriage |
| Mental element | Cruelty/harassment for dowry | Intention/knowledge | Instigation, aid |
| Minimum punishment | **7 years** | Life (or death) | None; up to 10 years |

**BNS and IPC compared (Dec 2024 C-question).** The definition, the explanation and the punishment are **substantially the same**; the IPC's s. 304B is now **s. 80** (in Chapter V, Of Offences Against Woman and Child); the presumption now sits in the BSA 2023 (the Evidence Act's s. 113B) †. There is no change in the requirement that the death be "soon before" the cruelty and within seven years of the marriage.

### 7.2 Cruelty by husband or relatives: ss. 85–86 (IPC 498A) ‡

**s. 85.** Whoever, being the **husband or the relative of the husband** of a woman, subjects such woman to **cruelty**, shall be punished with imprisonment up to **three years** and fine. **s. 86** defines cruelty: (a) any wilful conduct likely to drive the woman to commit suicide or to cause grave injury or danger to her life, limb or health (mental or physical); or (b) harassment of the woman where it is with a view to coercing her or any person related to her to meet any unlawful demand for any property or valuable security, or is on account of failure by her or any person related to her to meet such demand †.

Supreme Court: *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar* (2014) 8 SCC 273 (no automatic arrest in s. 498A cases; police to follow the checklist under s. 41 Cr.P.C.) †. *Rajesh Sharma v. State of U.P.* (2017) 10 SCC 472 (family welfare committees, later modified in *Social Action Forum for Manav Adhikar v. Union of India* (2018) 10 SCC 443) †.

### 7.3 Stalking: s. 78 ‡ (IPC 354D)

**s. 78(1).** A **man** commits stalking who:

1. **follows a woman** and **contacts, or attempts to contact** such woman to foster personal interaction **repeatedly**, despite a **clear indication of disinterest** by such woman; or
2. **monitors the use by a woman of the internet, email or any other form of electronic communication**.

**Provisos.** The conduct is not stalking if the man proves that: (i) it was pursued for the purpose of **preventing or detecting crime**, by a person entrusted with that responsibility by the State; (ii) it was pursued **under any law** or to comply with any condition or requirement imposed by any person under any law; or (iii) in the particular circumstances such conduct was **reasonable and justified**.

**s. 78(2) ‡.** Punishment: on **first conviction**, imprisonment of either description up to **three years**, and fine; on **second or subsequent conviction**, up to **five years**, and fine.

**Notes.** (i) Inserted into the IPC as s. 354D by the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013, after the Nirbhaya case and the J.S. Verma Committee report †. (ii) **Gender-specific**: only a man can be the offender and only a woman the victim. (iii) **Cyberstalking** is covered by clause (ii). (iv) The words "repeatedly" and "clear indication of disinterest" are the key. (v) Critics say the section leaves out stalking of men and by women, and that "foster personal interaction" is vague.

## 9. Illustrations and problems

1. **A parks his lorry across a lane so that Z cannot drive forward; Z can reverse and leave by another road.** Wrongful restraint (s. 126), not confinement.
2. **A locks Z in a warehouse for twelve hours.** Wrongful confinement (s. 127(2)); if for ten days or more, s. 127(4).
3. **A, a man of 25, persuades B, a 17-year-old girl who lives with her parents, to leave home and marry him, without their consent.** Kidnapping from lawful guardianship (s. 137(1)(b)): the girl's consent is immaterial. If she left on her own and A took no part in her leaving, the *Varadarajan* rule may apply and A is not guilty.
4. **A, by promising a job, induces C, an adult woman, to go from Delhi to Mumbai, intending to sell her for prostitution.** Abduction (s. 138) by deceitful means, and trafficking (s. 143); abduction alone is not punishable but the specified intent makes it so.
5. **A raises his stick and shouts "I will beat you" at Z across a room; Z believes he is about to be hit.** Assault (s. 130); punished by s. 131.
6. **A, a husband, and his mother demand a car from the wife's parents, harass the wife, and she is found dead of burns in the fifth year of marriage.** Dowry death (s. 80) with the presumption; also s. 85.

## 10. Criticism

1. **Overlap and age.** The BNS raises the age of male victims of kidnapping from 16 to 18, which removes a distinction that had no clear basis; however, the offence still treats the **child's consent** as irrelevant, which can lead to prosecution of consensual elopements.
2. **Abduction.** An act with no punishment of its own is a drafting oddity; the intent-based punishments make the structure complicated.
3. **Dowry death.** Misuse allegations, courts' call for care in applying the presumption; and the opposite concern: the low conviction rate. The Supreme Court in *Satbir Singh* (2021) stressed the careful framing of charges.
4. **Section 85 (cruelty).** A news report that came up in my search said the Supreme Court asked the Centre to consider changes to the BNS provision on cruelty against a woman; I did not read the order, so the point is **not checked** †.
5. **Stalking.** Gender-specific.
6. **Assault.** The rule that mere words are not assault is criticised in an era of threats by phone and online (*R v. Ireland*).

## 11. Current developments (to 29 October 2026)

1. BNS ss. 126–131 re-enact the IPC provisions with **raised fines** (restraint ₹5,000 against ₹500; confinement ₹5,000 against ₹1,000; assault ₹1,000 against ₹500; IPC figures from memory †) ‡.
2. s. 137: "child" for both sexes under 18 ‡; s. 143 trafficking tiers ‡; s. 80 and s. 78 as in the IPC ‡.
3. New offences: s. 95 (hiring a child to commit an offence), s. 69 (sexual intercourse by deceitful means) ‡.
4. I did not find a Supreme Court decision since 1 July 2024 changing the law on kidnapping, abduction, restraint or dowry death under the BNS. **Not checked** exhaustively.

## 13. Quick-revision box

- **Restraint (s. 126):** obstruction in some direction; ≤ 1 month. **Confinement (s. 127):** total, within circumscribing limits; ≤ 1 year (3 days: 3 years; 10 days: 5 years).
- **Force (s. 128) → criminal force (s. 129) → assault (s. 130, gesture or preparation, apprehension; words alone no); punishment s. 131: ≤ 3 months / ₹1,000.**
- **Kidnapping (s. 137):** from India · from guardianship (child under 18 / unsound mind; taking or enticing; without guardian's consent); ≤ 7 years. **Abduction (s. 138):** any person; force or deceit; no punishment by itself. *Varadarajan; Raja Ram; Thakorlal Vadgama*.
- **Dowry death (s. 80):** woman's death, abnormal, within 7 years, soon before cruelty/harassment for dowry; 7 years to life; presumption. *Kans Raj; Satbir Singh*.
- **Stalking (s. 78):** man follows/contacts repeatedly, or monitors electronic communication; 3 years / 5 years.
- **Children (ss. 93–99):** exposure, concealment, hiring, procuration, kidnapping under 10, selling, buying.
