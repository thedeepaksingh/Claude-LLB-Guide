## B-34 · Define culpable homicide. State its essentials

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Set at 20 marks in Dec 2025 Q11, Dec 2024 Q10, Dec 2023 Q10 and Dec 2022 Q13 (C-12). Related: A-68.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 3 · essentials 3 · Explanations 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Definition (BNS s. 100; IPC s. 299) ‡.** Whoever causes death by doing an act (1) with the **intention of causing death**, or (2) with the **intention of causing such bodily injury as is likely to cause death**, or (3) with the **knowledge that he is likely by such act to cause death**, commits culpable homicide.

**Essentials.** (i) A **human being is dead**; (ii) death is **caused by an act of the accused**, not necessarily the sole or immediate cause; (iii) one of the **three mental states**.

**Explanations** ‡
1. Injury to a person suffering from a **disorder, disease or infirmity** that accelerates his death is causing death.
2. Death is caused though **proper remedies and skilful treatment** might have prevented it.
3. Causing the death of a **child in the womb** is not homicide; killing a living child of which any part has been brought forth may be.

*Illustration.* A covers a pit with turf intending or knowing death is likely; Z falls in and dies: culpable homicide.

**Transferred malice (s. 102) †.** Killing someone other than the intended victim has the same character.

**Conclusion.** Culpable homicide is the **genus**; if s. 101 applies it is murder, otherwise s. 105.

## B-35 · Define murder. Explain its four limbs

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-12. Related: A-69.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** four limbs 5 · *Virsa Singh* 3 · illustrations and punishment 2

### Answer

**BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300) ‡.** Except in the cases excepted, culpable homicide is **murder** if the act is done:
1. with the **intention of causing death**;
2. with the intention of causing such bodily injury as the offender **knows to be likely to cause the death** of the person harmed;
3. with the intention of causing bodily injury to any person, and the injury intended is **sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death**;
4. with knowledge that the act is **so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death**, or such injury, and **without any excuse** for incurring the risk.

**Third limb: *Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab*** (1958) †. The prosecution must prove (i) a bodily injury; (ii) its nature; (iii) an **intention to inflict that particular injury**, not an accidental one; (iv) that the injury is **sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death**. Intention to kill is not needed.

*Illustration* †. Firing a loaded cannon into a crowd, without excuse, and killing one is murder (fourth limb).

**Exceptions.** Five Exceptions reduce it to culpable homicide not amounting to murder (s. 105).

**Punishment (s. 103(1)) ‡.** Death or imprisonment for life, and fine.

## B-36 · Distinguish between culpable homicide and murder

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Set at 20 marks in Dec 2025 Q11, Dec 2024 Q10, Dec 2023 Q10 and Dec 2022 Q13 (C-12). Related: A-70.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** genus and species 2 · table 5 · cases 2 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Relation.** Culpable homicide (BNS s. 100; IPC s. 299) is the **genus**; murder (s. 101; IPC s. 300) is the **species**, culpable homicide with the extra features of s. 101 (*Reg. v. Govinda*, 1876 †).

| Point | Culpable homicide | Murder |
|---|---|---|
| Intention to kill | Yes | Yes (1st limb) |
| Injury intended | Likely to cause death | Known to be likely to cause *that* victim's death (2nd), or sufficient in the ordinary course of nature (3rd) |
| Knowledge | That the act is **likely** to cause death | That the act is so imminently dangerous that it **must in all probability** cause death (4th) |
| Probability of death | Lower | Higher |
| Exceptions | – | Five Exceptions bring it back to culpable homicide |
| Punishment | s. 105: life, or 5 to 10 years (intention); up to 10 years (knowledge) | s. 103: death or life, and fine |

**Correspondence.** Intention to kill = 1st limb; intention to cause injury likely to cause death = 2nd and 3rd limbs; knowledge = 4th limb.

**Case.** The difference lies in the **degree of probability** of death (*State of A.P. v. R. Punnayya*, 1977 †).

**Conclusion.** All murder is culpable homicide; not all culpable homicide is murder.

## B-37 · Explain grave and sudden provocation as an exception to murder

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Part of the 20-mark answer C-13. Related: A-71.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** Exception 1 and conditions 4 · provisos 2 · cases 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Exception 1 to BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300) ‡.** Culpable homicide is not murder if the offender, **deprived of the power of self-control by grave and sudden provocation**, causes the death of the person who gave the provocation, or of any other person by mistake or accident.

**Conditions.**
1. **Provocation**: an act or words (words and gestures may suffice).
2. **Grave**: enough to deprive a **reasonable person** of self-control (*Mancini v. DPP*, 1942 †), and the accused must in fact have lost it.
3. **Sudden**: no time for **cooling**.
4. The death is of the provoker, or of another by mistake or accident.

**Provisos.** The provocation must not be (a) **sought or voluntarily provoked** by the offender as an excuse; (b) given by anything done in **obedience to law**, or by a public servant in lawful exercise of power; (c) given by anything done in the lawful exercise of **private defence** †.

**Case.** *K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra* (1962) †: Commander Nanavati, told by his wife of her affair, took a revolver and shot Ahuja at his flat. The Supreme Court held that there was time to cool and convicted him of murder.

**Effect.** Punishment under s. 105, not s. 103.

## B-38 · Explain sudden fight and the other Exceptions to murder

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-13. Related: A-72, A-73, A-74.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** Exception 4 conditions 4 · other Exceptions 4 · conclusion 2

### Answer

The five Exceptions to BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300) reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder, punished under s. 105 ‡.

**Exception 4: sudden fight.** The act is committed **without premeditation**, in a **sudden fight**, in the **heat of passion** upon a **sudden quarrel**, and the offender has **not taken undue advantage** or acted in a **cruel or unusual manner**. It is immaterial which party gave the provocation or made the first assault. All conditions must be met; killing an unarmed man with a deadly weapon, or striking one who has fallen, is undue advantage (*Surinder Kumar v. Union Territory, Chandigarh*, 1989 †).

**Other Exceptions** ‡
- **Exception 2: excess of private defence.** Good-faith exercise of the right, exceeding the power given by law, without premeditation and without intending more harm than necessary.
- **Exception 3: public servant.** A public servant, acting for the advancement of public justice, exceeds his powers in the good-faith belief that the act is lawful and necessary, without ill-will.
- **Exception 5: consent.** The person killed is **over eighteen** and suffers death or takes the risk of death with his own consent.

**Exception 1** is provocation (B-37).

**Conclusion.** Each Exception turns on the absence of cruelty or premeditation.

*Word count: 200*

## B-39 · Explain causing death by negligence. What is the offence of hit-and-run?

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-14. Related: A-79, A-80.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** s. 106(1) 3 · rashness and negligence 2 · medical negligence 2 · s. 106(2) 2 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Section 106(1) (IPC s. 304A) ‡.** Whoever causes death by a **rash or negligent act** not amounting to culpable homicide: up to **five years** and fine; up to **two years** and fine if a registered medical practitioner causes it while performing a medical procedure.

**Rashness** is a conscious taking of an unjustifiable risk, hoping that harm will not follow; **negligence** is failure to take the care a reasonable person would, without foresight of harm (*Empress of India v. Idu Beg*, 1881 †). The negligence must be **gross** and the **proximate cause** of death (*Kurban Hussein Rangwalla v. State of Maharashtra*, 1965 †).

**Medical negligence.** A doctor is criminally liable only for **gross** negligence or recklessness, not for an error of judgment (*Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*, 2005) †.

**Section 106(2) ‡: hit-and-run (new).** Causing death by **rash and negligent driving** not amounting to culpable homicide and **escaping without reporting** to a police officer or Magistrate soon after: up to **ten years** and fine. I found no notification bringing this sub-section into force, so check before relying on it †.

**Difference from culpable homicide.** No intention or knowledge of the likelihood of death.

## B-40 · Distinguish hurt from grievous hurt. When is attempt to murder made out?

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-14. Related: A-81 to A-84.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** table 5 · attempt to murder 4 · conclusion 1

### Answer

| Point | Hurt | Grievous hurt |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Bodily **pain, disease or infirmity** (BNS s. 114; IPC s. 319) | **Eight kinds only** (s. 116; IPC s. 320) |
| The kinds | One definition | Emasculation; permanent loss of sight or hearing; privation of a member or joint; permanent impairing of its powers; permanent disfiguration of head or face; fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth; hurt endangering life, or causing severe pain or inability to follow ordinary pursuits for **fifteen days** |
| Voluntary causing | Up to **one year**, or ₹10,000, or both (s. 115) | Up to **seven years** and fine (s. 117) |

If the hurt causes **permanent disability**, rigorous imprisonment of at least **ten years**, extendable to life (s. 117(3)) ‡.

**Attempt to murder (s. 109; IPC s. 307) ‡.** Whoever does an act with such **intention or knowledge, and in such circumstances, that if death were caused he would be guilty of murder**: up to **ten years** and fine; **life** if hurt is caused. **Hurt need not be caused**: the act itself suffices (*Om Prakash v. State of Punjab*, 1961; *State of Maharashtra v. Balram Bama Patil*, 1983 †).

**Conclusion.** The intention, not the injury, decides attempt to murder.

*Word count: 191*
