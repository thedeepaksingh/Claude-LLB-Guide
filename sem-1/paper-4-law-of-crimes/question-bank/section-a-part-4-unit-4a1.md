**A-68 · Culpable homicide** · *Related: B-34, C-12*

BNS s. 100 (IPC s. 299): whoever causes death by doing an act with the intention of causing death, or with the intention of causing such bodily injury as is likely to cause death, or with the knowledge that he is likely by such act to cause death, commits culpable homicide ‡. If it falls within s. 101 it is murder; otherwise s. 105 applies.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-69"></a>
**A-69 · Murder** · *Related: B-35, C-12*

BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300): culpable homicide is murder, except in the excepted cases, if the act is done with intention to cause death; or an injury known to be likely to cause that person's death; or an injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death; or with knowledge that the act is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death, without excuse ‡.

*Word count: 70*

<a id="a-70"></a>
**A-70 · Culpable homicide and murder** · *Related: B-36, C-12*

Culpable homicide is the genus, murder the species. Murder needs a higher degree of probability: an injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death, or an act that must in all probability cause death, whereas culpable homicide needs only that death be likely (*State of A.P. v. R. Punnayya*, 1977 †). Five Exceptions reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-71"></a>
**A-71 · Grave and sudden provocation** · *Related: B-37, C-13*

Exception 1 to BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300): culpable homicide is not murder if the offender, deprived of self-control by grave and sudden provocation, causes the death of the person who gave it, or of another by mistake or accident ‡. The provocation must not be sought, nor given by acts done in obedience to law or in private defence. See *K.M. Nanavati* (1962) †.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-72"></a>
**A-72 · Sudden fight** · *Related: B-38, C-13*

Exception 4 to BNS s. 101: culpable homicide is not murder if committed without premeditation in a sudden fight, in the heat of passion upon a sudden quarrel, and the offender has not taken undue advantage or acted in a cruel or unusual manner ‡. It is immaterial which party offers the provocation or commits the first assault.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-73"></a>
**A-73 · Private defence and public servant Exceptions to murder** · *Related: B-38, C-13*

Exception 2: death caused in good-faith exercise of private defence, exceeding the power given by law, without premeditation and without intending more harm than necessary. Exception 3: a public servant, acting for the advancement of public justice, exceeds his powers in good faith and without ill-will, causing death. Both reduce murder to culpable homicide (BNS s. 101) ‡.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-74"></a>
**A-74 · Consent as an Exception to murder** · *Related: B-38, C-13*

Exception 5 to BNS s. 101: culpable homicide is not murder when the person whose death is caused, being above eighteen, suffers death or takes the risk of death with his own consent ‡. Example: Z, over eighteen, agrees to be killed and A kills him: culpable homicide. A person under eighteen cannot give this consent.

*Word count: 55*

<a id="a-75"></a>
**A-75 · Punishment for murder** · *Related: B-12, B-35, C-12*

BNS s. 103(1) (IPC s. 302): whoever commits murder is punished with death or imprisonment for life, and is liable to fine ‡. Death is awarded only in the rarest of rare cases (*Bachan Singh*, 1980 †). Section 103(2) is new: murder by a group of five or more on grounds such as race, caste or community carries death, life, or at least seven years.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-76"></a>
**A-76 · Murder by a life-convict** · *Related: B-12, C-12*

BNS s. 104 (IPC s. 303): whoever, being under sentence of imprisonment for life, commits murder is punished with death, or with imprisonment for life meaning the remainder of his natural life ‡. The IPC made death mandatory; the Supreme Court struck that down in *Mithu v. State of Punjab* (1983) †. The BNS gives the court a choice.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-77"></a>
**A-77 · Mob lynching** · *Related: B-18, C-12*

BNS s. 103(2): when a group of five or more persons acting in concert commits murder on the ground of race, caste or community, sex, place of birth, language, personal belief or any other similar ground, each member is punished with death or imprisonment for life, or imprisonment of not less than seven years, and fine ‡. It is new; the IPC had no such provision.

*Word count: 65*

<a id="a-78"></a>
**A-78 · Punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder** · *Related: B-36, C-12, C-13*

BNS s. 105 (IPC s. 304) ‡: where the act is done with intention to cause death or such bodily injury as is likely to cause death: imprisonment for life, or for not less than five years and up to ten years, and fine. Where it is done with knowledge only: imprisonment up to ten years, with fine. The five-year minimum is new.

*Word count: 62*

<a id="a-79"></a>
**A-79 · Death by negligence** · *Related: B-39, C-14*

BNS s. 106(1) (IPC s. 304A): whoever causes death by a rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide is punished with imprisonment up to five years and fine; up to two years if done by a registered medical practitioner in a medical procedure ‡. The negligence must be gross and the proximate cause of death (*Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab*, 2005 †).

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-80"></a>
**A-80 · Hit and run** · *Related: B-39, C-14*

BNS s. 106(2): whoever causes death by rash and negligent driving not amounting to culpable homicide, and escapes without reporting to a police officer or Magistrate soon after, is punished with imprisonment up to ten years and fine ‡. It is new. I found no notification bringing it into force, so check before relying on it †.

*Word count: 55*

<a id="a-81"></a>
**A-81 · Attempt to murder** · *Related: B-40, C-14*

BNS s. 109 (IPC s. 307): whoever does any act with such intention or knowledge, and in such circumstances, that if he by that act caused death he would be guilty of murder, is punished with imprisonment up to ten years and fine; if hurt is caused, life imprisonment may be imposed ‡. Hurt need not be caused (*Om Prakash v. State of Punjab*, 1961 †).

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-82"></a>
**A-82 · Hurt** · *Related: B-40, C-14*

BNS s. 114 (IPC s. 319): whoever causes bodily pain, disease or infirmity to any person is said to cause hurt ‡. Mental distress alone is not hurt. Voluntarily causing hurt (s. 115(1)) needs intention or knowledge that hurt is likely; punishment up to one year, or fine up to ₹10,000, or both (s. 115(2)) ‡.

*Word count: 54*

<a id="a-83"></a>
**A-83 · Grievous hurt** · *Asked: Dec 2022 Q1* · *Related: B-40, C-14*

BNS s. 116 (IPC s. 320) designates eight kinds of hurt as grievous ‡: emasculation; permanent loss of sight of either eye; permanent loss of hearing of either ear; privation of any member or joint; permanent impairing of its powers; permanent disfiguration of the head or face; fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth; any hurt endangering life or causing severe pain or inability to follow ordinary pursuits for fifteen days.

*Word count: 71*

<a id="a-84"></a>
**A-84 · Hurt and grievous hurt** · *Related: B-40, C-14*

Hurt is bodily pain, disease or infirmity (BNS s. 114); grievous hurt is one of eight listed kinds of serious hurt (s. 116). Voluntarily causing hurt: up to one year or ₹10,000 (s. 115); voluntarily causing grievous hurt: up to seven years and fine (s. 117(2)) ‡. Hurt is compoundable; grievous hurt only in some cases, with the court's permission †.

*Word count: 59*

<a id="a-85"></a>
**A-85 · Acid attack** · *Related: B-40, C-14*

BNS s. 124(1) (IPC s. 326A): causing permanent or partial damage or deformity, burns, maiming, disfigurement, disability or grievous hurt by throwing or administering acid: imprisonment of not less than ten years, up to life (the remainder of natural life), with fine for the victim's medical expenses. Attempt: five to seven years and fine (s. 124(2)) ‡. See *Laxmi v. Union of India* (2014) †.

*Word count: 63*
