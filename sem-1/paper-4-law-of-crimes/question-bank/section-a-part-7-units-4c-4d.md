**A-120 · Sedition (IPC s. 124A)** · *Related: B-55, C-23*

IPC s. 124A: whoever by words, signs or visible representation brings or attempts to bring into hatred or contempt, or excites or attempts to excite disaffection towards, the Government established by law in India commits sedition; life imprisonment, or up to three years, or fine †. *Kedar Nath* (1962) limited it to words that incite violence or disorder. It is not in the BNS: s. 152 replaces it.

*Word count: 67*

<a id="a-121"></a>
**A-121 · BNS section 152** · *Related: B-58, C-23*

BNS s. 152: whoever, purposely or knowingly, by words, signs, visible representation, electronic communication or use of financial means, excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities, encourages separatist feelings, or endangers the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India, is punished with life imprisonment, or up to seven years, and fine ‡. Lawful criticism of Government measures is excepted.

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-122"></a>
**A-122 · Waging war against the Government** · *Related: B-55, C-23*

BNS s. 147 (IPC s. 121): whoever wages war against the Government of India, or attempts to wage such war, or abets the waging of such war, is punished with death or imprisonment for life, and fine ‡. It needs an organised use of force against the Government, not a mere riot (*State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu*, 2005; *Kasab*, 2012 †).

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-123"></a>
**A-123 · Unlawful assembly** · *Related: B-56, C-24*

BNS s. 189 (IPC s. 141): an assembly of five or more persons is unlawful if their common object is one of the five listed in s. 189(1). An assembly lawful when it met may become unlawful. The common object is inferred from conduct, arms and language (*Masalti v. State of U.P.*, 1965 †). A member is punished with imprisonment up to six months, or fine, or both (s. 189(2)) ‡.

*Word count: 69*

<a id="a-124"></a>
**A-124 · Lawful and unlawful gatherings** · *Related: B-56, C-24*

A lawful assembly has a lawful object and is peaceful and unarmed; Article 19(1)(b) protects it, subject to reasonable restrictions. An unlawful assembly has five or more members whose common object is one of those in BNS s. 189(1), pursued by criminal force or show of it; it may be dispersed. A lawful gathering becomes unlawful if its object changes or it defies a dispersal order.

*Word count: 66*

<a id="a-125"></a>
**A-125 · Rioting** · *Related: B-57, C-24*

BNS s. 191(1) (IPC s. 146): whenever force or violence is used by an unlawful assembly, or by any member of it, in prosecution of the common object, every member is guilty of rioting ‡. Punishment: up to two years, or fine, or both; if armed with a deadly weapon, up to five years, or fine, or both (s. 191(2), (3)) ‡.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-126"></a>
**A-126 · Affray** · *Related: B-57, C-24*

BNS s. 194 (IPC s. 159): when two or more persons, by fighting in a public place, disturb the public peace, they commit an affray †. No common object or assembly is needed. Punishment: imprisonment up to one month, or fine up to ₹1,000, or both ‡. A fight in private, or one that does not disturb the public peace, is not an affray.

*Word count: 62*

<a id="a-127"></a>
**A-127 · Riot and affray** · *Related: B-57, C-24*

Rioting needs five or more persons forming an unlawful assembly, a common object, and force or violence used in prosecuting it; place is immaterial. Affray needs two or more persons fighting in a public place and disturbing the public peace; no common object is needed. Riot: up to two years (five if armed). Affray: up to one month (BNS ss. 191, 194) ‡.

*Word count: 62*

<a id="a-128"></a>
**A-128 · Punishment for membership of an unlawful assembly** · *Related: B-56, C-24*

BNS s. 189 (IPC ss. 143–145) ‡: being a member of an unlawful assembly: up to six months, or fine, or both; joining or continuing after being commanded to disperse: up to two years, or fine, or both; being a member while armed with a deadly weapon: up to two years, or fine, or both. Hiring persons for an unlawful assembly is punished as being a member.

*Word count: 66*

<a id="a-129"></a>
**A-129 · Promoting enmity between groups** · *Related: C-24*

BNS s. 196 (IPC s. 153A): whoever by words, signs or visible representation promotes, or attempts to promote, disharmony or enmity between groups on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, caste or community, or does acts prejudicial to the maintenance of harmony, is punished with imprisonment up to three years, or fine, or both; up to five years if committed in a place of worship ‡ (grounds †).

*Word count: 69*

<a id="a-130"></a>
**A-130 · Bigamy** · *Asked: Dec 2022 Q4* · *Related: B-59, C-25*

BNS s. 82(1) (IPC s. 494): whoever, having a husband or wife living, marries in any case in which the marriage is void by reason of its taking place during the life of that husband or wife commits bigamy; punishment: up to seven years and fine ‡. The second marriage must be solemnised with the essential ceremonies (*Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v. State of Maharashtra*, 1965 †).

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-131"></a>
**A-131 · Exceptions to bigamy** · *Related: B-59, C-25*

Section 82(1) does not apply to a person whose marriage with the former spouse has been declared void by a court of competent jurisdiction, or whose former spouse has been continually absent for seven years and not heard of as alive, provided he informs the person he is marrying of the facts before the marriage ‡. The first spouse's consent is no defence †.

*Word count: 62*

<a id="a-132"></a>
**A-132 · Adultery (IPC s. 497)** · *Related: B-60, C-25*

IPC s. 497: a man who had sexual intercourse with the wife of another man, without that man's consent or connivance, was guilty of adultery (up to five years, or fine, or both); the wife was not punishable, and only the husband could complain †. The Supreme Court struck s. 497 down in *Joseph Shine v. Union of India* (2018) †. The BNS has no adultery offence ‡.

*Word count: 65*

<a id="a-133"></a>
**A-133 · *Joseph Shine v. Union of India*** · *Related: B-60, C-25*

On 27 September 2018 a Constitution Bench struck down IPC s. 497 and Cr.P.C. s. 198(2) as unconstitutional †. The section treated the wife as the husband's property, offended dignity (Art. 21), and discriminated on grounds of sex (Arts. 14, 15). Adultery remains a ground of divorce, but is not a crime.

*Word count: 51*

<a id="a-134"></a>
**A-134 · Concealing a former marriage when marrying again** · *Related: B-59, C-25*

BNS s. 82(2) (IPC s. 495): whoever commits bigamy having concealed from the person with whom the subsequent marriage is contracted the fact of the former marriage is punished with imprisonment up to ten years, and fine ‡. Example: a Hindu with a living wife secretly marries B with all ceremonies, telling her he is unmarried.

*Word count: 55*

<a id="a-135"></a>
**A-135 · Sexual intercourse by deceitful means** · *Related: B-14, C-25*

BNS s. 69 (new) ‡: sexual intercourse with a woman by deceitful means, including a false promise of marriage, not amounting to rape, is punishable with imprisonment up to ten years and fine †. The Supreme Court distinguishes a false promise made with no intention of marrying from the breach of a genuine promise (*Pramod Suryabhan Pawar v. State of Maharashtra*, 2019) †.

*Word count: 60*
