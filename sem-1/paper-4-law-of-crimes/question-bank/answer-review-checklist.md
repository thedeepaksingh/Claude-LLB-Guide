# Paper IV · Answer-Review Checklist (use after every mock and every practice answer)

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-29 (IST) · **Law stated as on:** 2026-10-29 · **Branch:** `paper/04-law-of-crimes`
> **What this is.** A checklist for marking your own answers against the [model answers](README.md) and for finding what to fix before the exam. **The mark splits are mine.** The university publishes no marking scheme, and I could not find one (see the [gaps document](../../../docs/01-gaps-and-open-questions.md) and the [source-verification log](source-verification-log.md)). Treat them as a guide to what a careful examiner would look for.

---

## 1. Before you review: set the answer beside the question

1. Did you **answer the question that was asked**? Compare your first paragraph with the verb in the question (*define, explain, discuss, distinguish, write a note, state*).
2. Did you keep to the **exam limit**? Section A ≤ 75 words, Section B ≤ 200 words, Section C: no limit printed ("answer is required in detail"), but the models run about 800–1,500 words; 700–1,000 handwritten words (my estimate) with the same headings and the leading cases fits a 30–35 minute answer.
3. Did you write the **right number of answers**? A: all five. B: two of three. C: three of five. An extra answer in B or C does not earn extra marks.
4. Did you give the **BNS section first and the IPC section in brackets**? The December 2022 and 2023 papers use IPC language and the December 2024 and 2025 papers use BNS language, so write both: for example, "BNS s. 101 (IPC s. 300)".
5. Did you answer **every part** of a two-part question? Most Section C questions have two or three parts ("when does culpable homicide amount to murder *and* distinguish them"; "define sedition *and* its position under the BNS"; "kidnapping *and* abduction *and* their difference"). Stopping after the first half loses half the marks.
6. If it is a **problem** (as in December 2023 Q7, the box), did you use **IRAC**: facts in a line, issue, rule with a section, application to *these* facts, conclusion? See section 5.

## 2. Section A (4 marks): tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Definition or meaning in the first sentence, with the section number | 1 |
| Two or three main points (the essentials, not a list of ten) | 2 |
| One case, punishment or example | 1 |
| ≤ 75 words | required |

**Typical losses:** starting with a long introduction; the section number missing or wrong; the punishment omitted when the question is "state the offence"; mixing up theft with extortion, kidnapping with abduction, hurt with grievous hurt; writing 120 words and running out of time.

## 3. Section B (10 marks): tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| One-line introduction that answers the question (section number, code) | 1 |
| The rule or ingredients stated correctly | 3 |
| Leading case(s) with year and a one-line fact each, or an illustration from the Code | 3 |
| A distinction, an exception, a punishment or a current point (BNS change) | 2 |
| One-line conclusion | 1 |
| ≤ 200 words | required |

For a **"distinguish"** question, use a table of five or six points (basis, meaning, section, essentials, punishment, example) and one line on the overlap; this is the quickest way to cover the points and stay within 200 words.

## 4. Section C (20 marks): tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Introduction: definition, section (BNS and IPC), background | 3 |
| Five or six headings in a logical order (definition → essentials → punishment → exceptions or defences → distinctions) | 5 |
| Leading cases named correctly, with facts or holding (at least four) | 5 |
| Comparison with the IPC, or the change made by the BNS | 2 |
| Criticism, a current development or a balanced view | 3 |
| Conclusion that answers the question set | 2 |

**Typical losses:** a good definition with no cases; cases listed without facts or holding; the BNS ignored (or the IPC ignored); the second part of the question left out; for culpable homicide and murder, no discussion of the *four* limbs and the *five* Exceptions; for private defence, the restrictions (s. 37, IPC s. 99) left out; for sedition, no mention that IPC s. 124A is not in the BNS and that s. 152 replaced it.

## 5. Problem questions (IRAC)

| Step | What to write | Marks (mine, for a 10-mark problem) |
|---|---|---|
| Facts and parties | The relevant facts, in two lines, with the parties named A and B | 1 |
| Issue | The legal question, in one sentence | 1 |
| Rule | The offence, its section (BNS and IPC) and the essentials | 3 |
| Application | Apply each essential to **these** facts; deal with the alternative if the answer turns on A's intention | 4 |
| Conclusion | A clear answer ("A is guilty of house-trespass by night and of theft, or in the alternative of attempt to commit theft") | 1 |

**December 2023 Q7** (the box): the answer turns on what A intended to take. If he meant to take the box itself, theft was complete when he moved it (BNS s. 303, Expl. 3), and finding it empty is no defence; if he meant to take only the contents, the offence is an attempt (s. 62; IPC s. 511). Add house-trespass by night and lurking house-trespass or house-breaking (ss. 329–331). See [B-49](section-b-part-6-unit-4b.md#b-49) and [C-19](section-c-part-6-unit-4b.md#c-19).

### 6.2 Common confusions to avoid

These are standard slips, not errors that I found in my drafts.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| Culpable homicide and murder are different offences with different elements | Culpable homicide is the **genus** and murder the **species**; murder needs a higher degree of probability (*Punnayya*, 1977 †). Five Exceptions reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder |
| Common intention (s. 3(5); IPC s. 34) needs five persons | It needs **two or more** persons with a prior meeting of minds. **Five or more** are needed for an unlawful assembly and common object (s. 190; IPC s. 149) |
| Abduction is an offence by itself | It is punishable only with a specified intent (murder, ransom, marriage, wrongful confinement, etc.): BNS ss. 87, 140 ‡ |
| Kidnapping from lawful guardianship requires force | It requires **taking or enticing**, a child (under eighteen: s. 2(3)), a lawful guardian, and no consent of the guardian; the child's consent is immaterial |
| Theft requires carrying the thing away | Any **moving** is enough (BNS s. 303, Expl. 3) |
| Theft and extortion are the same because both take property | Theft: **without consent**, movable property, no fear. Extortion: consent **obtained by putting in fear of injury**, any property or valuable security |
| Criminal misappropriation and criminal breach of trust are the same | In breach of trust the property is **entrusted**; in misappropriation it comes innocently (found, etc.) and dishonesty arises later |
| Robbery and dacoity differ in the force used | They differ in **number**: dacoity is robbery by five or more persons conjointly |
| *Mens rea* is needed for every offence | Not for strict-liability offences (*Nathulal*; *M.H. George* †); the Code also uses knowledge and negligence |
| Medical insanity is a defence | Only **legal** insanity (BNS s. 22; IPC s. 84): the accused did not know the nature of the act or that it was wrong or contrary to law |
| Voluntary intoxication is a defence | Only where it is **involuntary** and produces incapacity (BNS ss. 23–24) †; voluntary intoxication is no defence, but may bear on intention |
| The right of private defence extends to causing death in every case | Only in the **seven** cases of BNS s. 38 (IPC s. 100), within the restrictions of s. 37 |
| Life imprisonment means fourteen years | It means imprisonment for the **remainder of natural life** unless remitted or commuted (*Gopal Vinayak Godse*; *Swamy Shraddananda*; *V. Sriharan* †) |
| Sedition is an offence under the BNS | The word is gone. **BNS s. 152** (acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India) replaces IPC s. 124A; the challenge to both is undecided on the last date I could check |
| Adultery is still an offence | IPC s. 497 was struck down in *Joseph Shine* (2018) †; the BNS has no adultery provision ‡ |
| IPC sections are the current law | The BNS, 2023 applies to offences committed on or after **1 July 2024**; the IPC applies to earlier offences. Write both |
