## B-55 · Explain the offence of sedition. What did the Supreme Court decide in *Kedar Nath*?

**Asked as:** Dec 2022 Q6 ("sedition"). Set at 20 marks in Dec 2023 Q11 (C-23). Related: A-120.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** section and essentials 4 · *Kedar Nath* 3 · present position 3

### Answer

**IPC s. 124A †.** Whoever by **words, spoken or written, signs or visible representation**, **brings or attempts to bring into hatred or contempt, or excites or attempts to excite disaffection towards, the Government established by law in India**: imprisonment for life, or up to three years, with fine, or fine alone. Explanation 1: "disaffection" includes **disloyalty and all feelings of enmity**; Explanations 2 and 3: lawful criticism of measures or administrative action is not an offence. The section was inserted in **1870**, and was used against Tilak and Gandhi †.

***Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar*** (1962, Constitution Bench) †. Section 124A is **valid** under Art. 19(2) if read narrowly: it punishes only words that have a **tendency or intention to create disorder or incite violence**. Strong criticism of the Government, without that, is not sedition. *Balwant Singh v. State of Punjab* (1995): casual slogans, with no call to violence, are not sedition †.

**Present position.** On **11 May 2022** (*S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India*) the Supreme Court put s. 124A **in abeyance**: no fresh FIRs while the Union re-examined it ‡. The BNS has no sedition section; **s. 152** replaces it (B-58). Validity undecided at 22 May 2026 ‡.

## B-56 · What is an unlawful assembly? Distinguish lawful from unlawful gatherings

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q6 ("unlawful assembly; lawful and unlawful gatherings"). Set at 20 marks in Dec 2025 Q10 (C-24). Related: A-123, A-124.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 3 · essentials 2 · table 4 · punishment 1

### Answer

**BNS s. 189(1) (IPC s. 141) ‡.** An assembly of **five or more** persons is an **unlawful assembly** if their **common object** is: (a) to overawe by criminal force, or show of it, the Government, Parliament, a Legislature or a public servant; (b) to resist the execution of any law or legal process; (c) to commit mischief, criminal trespass or other offence; (d) by criminal force, or show of it, to take possession of property or to enforce a right; (e) by criminal force, or show of it, to compel a person to do what he is not bound to do. An assembly lawful at the start may become unlawful.

**Essentials.** Five or more persons; a common object, inferred from conduct, arms and language (*Masalti v. State of U.P.*, 1965 †).

| Point | Lawful | Unlawful |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Any | Five or more |
| Object | Lawful: a meeting, a peaceful protest | One of the five listed |
| Means | Peaceful, without arms | Criminal force, or show of it |
| Constitution | Protected by **Art. 19(1)(b)** | Not protected; Art. 19(3) |
| Police | Regulation only | May be dispersed (BNSS ss. 148–150 †) |

**Punishment (s. 189(2)) ‡.** Member: up to **6 months**, or fine, or both; armed: up to **2 years**.

## B-57 · Distinguish between rioting and affray

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q13(a) in Section C ("rioting v. affray", C-24). Not yet set at 10 marks. Related: A-125 to A-127.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** two definitions 4 · table 5 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Rioting (BNS s. 191(1); IPC s. 146).** Whenever **force or violence** is used by an unlawful assembly, or by any member of it, **in prosecution of its common object**, every member is guilty of rioting ‡.
**Affray (s. 194; IPC s. 159).** When **two or more persons**, by **fighting in a public place**, **disturb the public peace** †.

| Point | Rioting | Affray |
|---|---|---|
| Number | **Five or more** (an unlawful assembly) | **Two or more** |
| Common object | **Required** | Not required |
| Force or violence | Used in prosecution of the object | A fight |
| Place | Anywhere | **Public place** |
| Disturbance of the peace | Not stated | **Essential** |
| Punishment | Up to **2 years**, or fine, or both; **armed**: up to **5 years** (s. 191(2), (3)) | Up to **1 month**, or ₹1,000, or both ‡ |
| Seriousness | Aggravated | Minor |

**Relationship.** Every riot is an unlawful assembly; not every unlawful assembly is a riot. Members of a riot are also liable under s. 190 for offences committed in prosecution of the common object.

*Example.* Eight villagers beat a surveyor with lathis to stop him: riot. Two men fight in a market: affray.

**Conclusion.** Rioting is an offence of a group with a common object; affray is a fight in public by two or more.

## B-58 · Explain section 152 of the BNS. Is it sedition by another name?

**Asked as:** not yet set. Sedition was set in Dec 2022 Q6 (B-55) and Dec 2023 Q11 (C-23). Related: A-121.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** text 3 · comparison 4 · status 3

### Answer

**BNS s. 152, "Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India" ‡.** Whoever, **purposely or knowingly**, by words, signs, visible representation, **electronic communication** or **use of financial means**, **excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities**, encourages feelings of **separatist activities**, or **endangers the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India**, or commits any such act: imprisonment for **life, or up to seven years**, and fine. **Explanation:** comments expressing disapprobation of the Government's measures or administrative action, to obtain alteration by lawful means, are not an offence.

| Point | IPC s. 124A | BNS s. 152 |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Hatred, contempt, disaffection towards the Government | Secession, armed rebellion, subversion, separatism |
| Mental element | Not stated | Purposely or knowingly |
| Means | Words, signs | Also electronic and financial means |
| Punishment | Life; or up to 3 years; or fine | Life, or up to 7 years, and fine |

**Arguments.** *For:* the word "sedition" is gone and the object is the country, not the Government. *Against:* the substance remains in wider, undefined words ("subversive activities", "separatist feelings") †.

**Status.** In **August 2025** the Supreme Court issued notice on a challenge to s. 152 and tagged it with the s. 124A batch ‡. I found no judgment as of 22 May 2026.

## B-59 · What is bigamy? State its essentials and exceptions

**Asked as:** Dec 2022 Q4 set "bigamy" at 4 marks (A-130). 20-mark form: C-25. Related: A-131, A-134.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** section 3 · essentials 3 · exceptions 2 · cases 2

### Answer

**BNS s. 82(1) (IPC s. 494) ‡.** Whoever, having a husband or wife living, **marries in any case in which the marriage is void by reason of its taking place during the life of that husband or wife**: imprisonment up to **seven years** and fine. **S. 82(2) (IPC s. 495):** where the former marriage was **concealed** from the person with whom the second marriage is contracted: up to **ten years** and fine.

**Essentials.** (1) A **valid first marriage subsisting**; (2) the accused **marries again**, and the second marriage is **solemnised**; (3) it is void because the spouse is alive; (4) no exception applies.

**Exceptions** ‡. (i) The first marriage has been **declared void by a court**; (ii) the first spouse has been **continually absent for seven years** and not heard of as alive, provided the accused **informs** the second spouse of the facts before marrying.

**Cases** †
- *Bhaurao Shankar Lokhande v. State of Maharashtra* (1965): the second marriage must be proved to be **solemnised**, with the essential ceremonies.
- *Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India* (1995): a Hindu husband's **conversion to Islam** does not dissolve the first marriage; a second marriage is bigamy.

**Note.** The consent of the first spouse is no defence †.

## B-60 · Discuss adultery. Why is it not an offence under the BNS?

**Asked as:** not yet set. Related: A-132, A-133.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** old law 3 · *Joseph Shine* 4 · BNS position 3

### Answer

**IPC s. 497 †.** A man who had sexual intercourse with a woman he knew or had reason to believe was **the wife of another man, without that man's consent or connivance**, committed adultery: up to **five years**, or fine, or both. Features: only a **man** could be the offender; the **wife was never punishable**; the husband's **consent or connivance** removed the offence; only the **husband** could complain (Cr.P.C. s. 198(2)).

**Earlier decisions.** The Supreme Court upheld the section in *Yusuf Abdul Aziz* (1954), *Sowmithri Vishnu* (1985) and *V. Revathi* (1988) †.

***Joseph Shine v. Union of India*** (27 September 2018, Constitution Bench) †. Section 497 and Cr.P.C. s. 198(2) were **struck down** as unconstitutional: the section treated the wife as the **husband's property**, offended **dignity** (Art. 21), **discriminated** on grounds of sex (Arts. 14, 15) and rested on gender stereotypes. Adultery remains a **ground of divorce** but is not a crime.

**BNS.** The Sanhita has **no adultery offence** ‡. The Parliamentary Standing Committee (November 2023) recommended a **gender-neutral** offence; Parliament did not adopt it †.

**Arguments.** *For:* protects marriage. *Against:* private morality; civil remedies suffice.

*Word count: 181*
