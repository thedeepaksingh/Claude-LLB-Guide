<a id="a-28"></a>
**A-28 · Constructive liability** · *Related: B-18, C-05*

Liability for an offence not wholly committed by the accused himself, arising because he acted with others. Examples: common intention (BNS s. 3(5); IPC s. 34) and the common object of an unlawful assembly (s. 190; IPC s. 149) ‡. Each participant is liable as if he had done the whole act. It exists because in group crime the fatal blow is hard to attribute.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-29"></a>
**A-29 · Common intention** · *Related: B-15, C-05*

BNS s. 3(5) (IPC s. 34): when a criminal act is done by several persons in furtherance of the common intention of all, each is liable for that act as if he had done it alone ‡. It creates no offence. It requires a prior meeting of minds, which may arise on the spot, and participation (*Mahbub Shah v. King-Emperor*, 1945 †).

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-30"></a>
**A-30 · Similar intention and common intention** · *Related: B-15, B-17*

Common intention means a pre-arranged plan, a meeting of minds, so that the act is done in furtherance of the intention of all. Similar intention means that each person independently intends the same result, without concert. Similar intention does not attract s. 3(5); each is liable only for his own act (*Mahbub Shah v. King-Emperor*, 1945 †).

*Word count: 56*

<a id="a-31"></a>
**A-31 · Common object** · *Related: B-16, B-17, C-05*

The purpose shared by the members of an unlawful assembly, being one of the five listed in BNS s. 189(1). Section 190 (IPC s. 149) makes every member guilty of an offence committed by any member in prosecution of that object, or such as the members knew to be likely to be committed in prosecution of it ‡. Five or more persons are needed.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-32"></a>
**A-32 · Unlawful assembly** · *Related: B-16, B-56*

An assembly of five or more persons whose common object is: to overawe the Government or a public servant by criminal force; to resist the execution of law or legal process; to commit mischief, criminal trespass or other offence; to take possession of property or enforce a right by criminal force; or to compel a person to do what he is not bound to do (BNS s. 189(1); IPC s. 141) ‡.

*Word count: 71*

<a id="a-33"></a>
**A-33 · "Knew to be likely" limb of section 190** · *Related: B-16*

The second limb of BNS s. 190 (IPC s. 149) makes members liable for an offence outside the common object if they knew it was likely to be committed in prosecution of that object. Knowledge is inferred from the nature of the assembly, the weapons carried and the behaviour at or before the scene (*Mizaji v. State of U.P.*, 1959 †).

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-34"></a>
**A-34 · Participation: "they also serve who only stand and wait"** · *Related: B-15*

Under BNS s. 3(5) (IPC s. 34) a person who takes part in the common plan is liable even if he does not himself do the criminal act. Standing guard outside while the others act may be participation: "they also serve who only stand and wait" (*Barendra Kumar Ghosh v. King-Emperor*, 1925 †). Mere presence, without participation, is not enough †.

*Word count: 59*

<a id="a-35"></a>
**A-35 · Abetment** · *Related: B-19, C-06*

BNS s. 45 (IPC s. 107): a person abets the doing of a thing who instigates any person to do it; or engages with others in a conspiracy for its doing, if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance; or intentionally aids its doing by any act or illegal omission †. The offence abetted need not be committed (s. 46, Explanation 2).

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-36"></a>
**A-36 · Abettor** · *Asked: Dec 2024 Q4* · *Related: B-19, B-20, C-06*

BNS s. 46 (IPC s. 108): an abettor is a person who abets the commission of an offence, or the commission of an act which would be an offence if done by a person capable by law of committing an offence with the same intention or knowledge as the abettor †. The act abetted need not be committed, and the person abetted need not be capable of committing an offence.

*Word count: 69*

<a id="a-37"></a>
**A-37 · Instigation** · *Related: B-19, C-06*

To instigate is to goad, urge, provoke, incite or encourage a person to do an act. It needs a positive act, by words or conduct. Wilful misrepresentation, or concealment of a material fact one is bound to disclose, is instigation (Explanation 1 to s. 45). An angry remark made without intention is not instigation (*Ramesh Kumar v. State of Chhattisgarh*, 2001 †).

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-38"></a>
**A-38 · Abetment by aid** · *Related: B-19, C-06*

A person abets by aid who intentionally aids the doing of a thing, by an act or illegal omission, before or at the time of the offence (BNS s. 45, "thirdly"; Explanation 2) †. The aid must actually facilitate the offence and must be intentional. Giving a lift to a person ignorant of his purpose is not abetment. Example: supplying a weapon knowing it will be used.

*Word count: 66*

<a id="a-39"></a>
**A-39 · Abetment by conspiracy** · *Related: B-19, B-23, C-06*

A person abets by conspiracy who engages with one or more persons in a conspiracy for the doing of a thing, if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of the conspiracy and in order to the doing of that thing (BNS s. 45, "secondly") †. Without the act there is no abetment, though there may be criminal conspiracy under s. 61.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-40"></a>
**A-40 · Abetment of an abetment** · *Related: B-20, C-06*

Abetment of an abetment is itself an offence (Explanation 4 to BNS s. 46; IPC s. 108) †. Example: A instigates B to instigate C to murder Z; if C commits the murder, A is liable as an abettor. It is not necessary that the abettor concert the offence with the person abetted (Explanation 5) †.

*Word count: 54*

<a id="a-41"></a>
**A-41 · Abetment when the offence is not committed** · *Related: B-20, C-06*

If the offence abetted is not committed, the abettor is punished under BNS s. 55 (IPC s. 115) where the offence carries death or life imprisonment: up to seven years and fine, up to fourteen if harm results; or under s. 56 (IPC s. 116) where it carries imprisonment: up to one-fourth of the longest term, or fine, or both †.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-42"></a>
**A-42 · Abettor present at the offence** · *Related: B-18, B-20, C-05*

BNS s. 54 (IPC s. 114) †: whenever an act for which an abettor would be liable is committed in his presence, he is deemed to have committed that act. It is a rule of constructive liability. Example: A instigates B to beat C and is present when B beats C to death; A is treated as having committed the offence.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-43"></a>
**A-43 · Abetment of suicide** · *Related: B-21, C-06*

BNS s. 108 (IPC s. 306): if a person commits suicide, whoever abets it is punished with imprisonment up to ten years and fine ‡. Section 107 (IPC s. 305) covers the suicide of a child or a person of unsound mind: death, life, or up to ten years ‡. Instigation must be a positive act with intention (*Ramesh Kumar*, 2001; *Sanju*, 2002 †).

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-44"></a>
**A-44 · Criminal conspiracy** · *Related: B-22, C-07*

BNS s. 61(1) (IPC s. 120A): when two or more persons agree to do, or cause to be done, an illegal act, or a legal act by illegal means, the agreement is a criminal conspiracy ‡. Where the object is an offence the agreement alone suffices. It is usually proved by inference from circumstances (*Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration)*, 1988 †).

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-45"></a>
**A-45 · Overt act in conspiracy** · *Related: B-22, B-23, C-07*

By the proviso to BNS s. 61(1) (IPC s. 120A), no agreement except an agreement to commit an offence is a criminal conspiracy unless some act besides the agreement is done in pursuance of it †. Where the object is an offence, no overt act is needed. Abetment by conspiracy, in contrast, always needs an act or illegal omission.

*Word count: 58*
