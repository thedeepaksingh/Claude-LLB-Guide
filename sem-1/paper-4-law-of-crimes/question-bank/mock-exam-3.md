**LL.B. (Semester I) · Law of Crimes – I (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 / Indian Penal Code, 1860) (K-1004)**  
**Time: 3 hours · Maximum marks: 100**

*Note: Answer all the questions in Section A. In Section B answer any two of the three questions. In Section C answer any three of the five questions.*

### Section A (very short answer) · 5 × 4 = 20 marks
*Attempt all questions. Each answer must not exceed 75 words.*

1. State the theories of punishment.  *(4)*
2. What is the punishment for abetment where the offence abetted is not committed?  *(4)*
3. How far is consent a defence in criminal law?  *(4)*
4. Who is a 'child' under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita?  *(4)*
5. What is the punishment for murder by a person under sentence of imprisonment for life?  *(4)*

### Section B (short answer) · 2 × 10 = 20 marks
*Attempt any two questions. Each answer must not exceed 200 words.*

6. Discuss the doctrine of 'rarest of rare' in the award of the death sentence.  *(10)*
7. Distinguish between section 34 and section 149 of the Indian Penal Code.  *(10)*
8. Explain dowry death. What is the presumption?  *(10)*

### Section C (long answer) · 3 × 20 = 60 marks
*Attempt any three questions. The answer is required in detail.*

9. Explain the stages of a crime. When does preparation become attempt? How is each stage punished?  *(20)*
10. What is criminal conspiracy? Discuss its essentials, proof and punishment, and distinguish it from abetment.  *(20)*
11. Explain the general exceptions of mistake of fact, accident, necessity, infancy, consent and compulsion.  *(20)*
12. Discuss the offences against children under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.  *(20)*
13. Explain unlawful assembly under the BNS. Distinguish lawful from unlawful gatherings, and rioting from affray.  *(20)*

## Choice strategy
- **Section B and C:** choose the questions where you can state the section and its essentials. A question you half-know costs more marks than one you know well.
- **Problem questions** (as in December 2023 Q7): state the facts in a line, the issue, the section, apply it to the facts, and give a conclusion (IRAC). Offer the alternative if the answer turns on the accused's intention.
- **Two codes.** The December 2022 and 2023 papers refer to the IPC and the December 2024 and 2025 papers to the BNS. Write the **BNS section first and the IPC section in brackets**, so that either form of the question is answered.
- **Do not exceed the word limits in A and B.** A good A answer is 55–75 words.
- Add one **case** and one **criticism or recent development** to earn the last marks in Section C (see the [digest](cases-and-statutes-digest.md)).
