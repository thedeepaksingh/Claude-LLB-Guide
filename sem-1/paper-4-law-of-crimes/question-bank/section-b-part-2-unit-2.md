## B-19 · Define abetment. What are its essentials?

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Related 4-mark question: Dec 2024 Q4 ("abettor", A-36). 20-mark form: C-06.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 2 · three modes 4 · essentials 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Definition.** BNS s. 45 (IPC s. 107) †: a person **abets** the doing of a thing who

1. **instigates** any person to do it;
2. **engages with one or more others in a conspiracy** to do it, if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of it; or
3. **intentionally aids**, by an act or illegal omission, the doing of it.

**Instigation** means to goad, urge, provoke or encourage; it needs a **positive act**. Wilful misrepresentation or concealment of a material fact is instigation (Expl. 1). An angry remark without intention is not (*Ramesh Kumar v. State of Chhattisgarh*, 2001 †).
**Aid** must be intentional; it may be given before or at the time of the offence (Expl. 2).

**Essentials.** (i) A specific offence, or an act that would be an offence, is abetted; (ii) one of the three modes is proved; (iii) the abettor has a **guilty mind**: he intends the offence; (iv) the offence **need not be committed** (s. 46, Expl. 2).

*Illustration.* A urges B to kill C; B refuses. A is guilty of abetting murder.

**Conclusion.** Abetment is an inchoate offence: the instigation is punished whether or not the offence follows.

## B-20 · Who is an abettor? How is an abettor punished?

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q4 set "abettor" at 4 marks (A-36). This is its 10-mark form. 20-mark form: C-06.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** abettor and Explanations 4 · punishment table 5 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Abettor** (BNS s. 46; IPC s. 108): one who abets (i) the commission of an offence, or (ii) an act which would be an offence if done by a person capable by law of committing an offence with the same intention or knowledge as the abettor.

**Explanations** †: (1) abetting an illegal omission is an offence even if the abettor is not bound to do the act; (2) it is **not necessary that the act abetted be committed**; (3) the person abetted need not be capable of committing an offence; (4) **abetment of an abetment** is an offence; (5) the abettor need not concert the offence with the person abetted.

**Punishment** (section numbers †)

| Case | Section (IPC) | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| Act abetted is committed | s. 49 (109) | Same as for the offence |
| Offence not committed: death or life | s. 55 (115) | Up to 7 years and fine |
| Offence not committed: imprisonment | s. 56 (116) | Up to one-fourth of the longest term |
| Different act, a probable consequence | s. 51 (111) | As if abetted directly |
| Abettor present at the offence | s. 54 (114) | Deemed to have committed it |

**Conclusion.** Punishment is that of the principal if the offence follows, lighter if not.

## B-21 · Discuss abetment of suicide

**Asked as:** not yet set. 20-mark form: C-06. Related: A-43.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** provisions 3 · ingredients 2 · cases 4 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Provisions.** **BNS s. 108 (IPC s. 306):** if a person commits suicide, whoever abets it shall be punished with imprisonment up to **ten years** and fine ‡. **S. 107 (IPC s. 305):** abetment of the suicide of a **child or a person of unsound mind** (and similar persons): death, life imprisonment, or up to ten years, and fine ‡.

**Attempt to commit suicide** (IPC s. 309) is not carried into the BNS, except an attempt made to compel or restrain a public servant (s. 226) †. The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, s. 115, presumes severe stress †.

**Ingredients.** (1) Suicide is actually committed; (2) the accused abetted it by instigation, conspiracy or aid; (3) he had **intention**; (4) his act was close in time and a cause of the suicide.

**Cases** †
- *Gian Kaur v. State of Punjab* (1996): the right to life does not include the right to die; s. 306 is valid.
- *Ramesh Kumar* (2001): instigation needs a positive act.
- *Sanju v. State of M.P.* (2002): "go and die", said in a quarrel, is not instigation.
- *Amalendu Pal v. State of W.B.* (2010): harassment alone is not enough.

**Conclusion.** Quarrels and marital discord are not abetment without intention and a proximate act.

## B-22 · What is criminal conspiracy? State its essentials and punishment

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-07. Related: A-44, A-45.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 3 · essentials 3 · proof 2 · punishment 2

### Answer

**Definition.** BNS s. 61(1) (IPC s. 120A): when two or more persons agree to do, or cause to be done, (a) an **illegal act**, or (b) a **legal act by illegal means**, the agreement is a **criminal conspiracy** ‡. **Proviso †:** unless the agreement is to commit an **offence**, some **act besides the agreement** must be done in pursuance of it. Chapter V-A was inserted in 1913 †.

**Essentials.** (1) An **agreement** between **two or more** persons; (2) to do an illegal act or a legal act by illegal means; (3) where the object is an **offence**, the agreement alone suffices; (4) a guilty mind: knowledge of the agreement and intention to further it.

**Proof.** The agreement is secret, so it is **inferred from circumstances** and conduct before, during and after the offence (*Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration)*, 1988 †, the Indira Gandhi assassination case). Acts of one conspirator in reference to the common design are relevant against all (Evidence Act s. 10; BSA s. 8 †).

**Punishment** (s. 61(2); IPC s. 120B) ‡: conspiracy to commit an offence punishable with death, life or rigorous imprisonment of **two years or more**: as an abettor; any other conspiracy: up to **six months**, or fine, or both.

## B-23 · Distinguish between criminal conspiracy and abetment by conspiracy

**Asked as:** not yet set. 20-mark form: C-07. Related: A-39, A-44.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** meaning of each 3 · table 5 · conclusion 2

### Answer

**Criminal conspiracy** (BNS s. 61; IPC s. 120A): an agreement of two or more to commit an illegal act or a legal act by illegal means. **Abetment by conspiracy** (s. 45, "secondly"; IPC s. 107): engaging with others in a conspiracy to do a thing, **if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of it**.

| Point | Criminal conspiracy | Abetment by conspiracy |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | A separate offence | One of three modes of abetment |
| Act beyond the agreement | **Not needed** if the object is an offence | An **act or illegal omission in pursuance** is essential |
| Persons | Two or more | One abettor and a person who does the act |
| Punishment | As for an abettor (offence of two years or more) or up to six months (s. 61(2)) | Under ss. 49, 55, 56 †, according to whether the offence is committed |
| Offence need be committed | No | Not for ss. 55–56 †; yes for s. 49 |

**Overlap.** The same facts may disclose both; the Code punishes conspiracy because abetment by conspiracy needed an act, which was a gap before 1913 †.

**Conclusion.** Conspiracy punishes the agreement; abetment by conspiracy punishes the agreement plus an act in pursuance of it.

## B-24 · Explain attempt to commit an offence under BNS s. 62 (IPC s. 511)

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Set at 20 marks in Dec 2023 Q12 (C-02). Related: A-18, A-19.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** section 3 · essentials 3 · cases 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Section 62 (IPC s. 511).** Whoever attempts an offence punishable with life imprisonment or imprisonment, and does any act towards it, shall, **where no express provision is made**, be punished with up to **one-half** of the life term or the longest term, or with the fine provided, or both ‡.

**Essentials.** (1) Intention to commit a specific offence; (2) an act **beyond preparation**, done towards the commission; (3) failure or interruption from a cause other than the offender's choice.

**Special sections prevail:** attempt to murder (s. 109; IPC s. 307), attempt to commit culpable homicide (s. 110; IPC s. 308), attempt to rob (s. 309(5)) ‡.

**Impossible attempts.** Attempting to pick an empty pocket, or to steal from a box with no jewels, is an attempt (illustrations to IPC s. 511 †).

**Cases.**
- *Abhayanand Mishra v. State of Bihar* (1961): applying for an examination on forged documents was attempt to cheat.
- *Malkiat Singh v. State of Punjab* (1991): paddy carried towards the border, stopped before leaving the State: preparation †.
- *Om Prakash v. State of Punjab* (1961): attempt to murder does not need hurt †.

**Conclusion.** The test is directness of the act, not the stage reached.

*Word count: 192*
