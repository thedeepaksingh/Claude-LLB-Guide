## B-48 · Define theft. State its ingredients

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q6 ("theft: ingredients"). Set at 20 marks in Dec 2024 Q9 and Dec 2022 Q12 (C-19). Related: A-102, A-14.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 2 · five ingredients 5 · cases 2 · punishment 1

### Answer

**BNS s. 303(1) (IPC s. 378) ‡.** Whoever, **intending to take dishonestly any movable property out of the possession of any person without that person's consent, moves that property in order to such taking**, commits theft.

**Ingredients**
1. **Dishonest intention**: intention to cause wrongful gain to one person or wrongful loss to another (BNS s. 2(7), 2(36), 2(37)); it must exist **when the property is moved**.
2. **Movable property** (s. 2(21)). Things attached to the earth are not, until **severed** (Expl. 1).
3. **Out of the possession of another.** A servant's custody is the master's possession. Property in nobody's possession (a ring lying on the road) cannot be stolen.
4. **Without consent**, express or implied (Expl. 5).
5. **Moving**: any moving suffices (Expls. 3, 4).

**Cases** †. *K.N. Mehra v. State of Rajasthan* (1957): dishonest intention shown when the aircraft was moved, though later abandoned. *Pyare Lal Bhargava v. State of Rajasthan* (1963): **temporary removal** for a dishonest purpose is theft.

**Punishment (s. 303(2)) ‡.** Up to **3 years**, or fine, or both; a later conviction: rigorous imprisonment of **1 to 5 years** and fine. A first offender who returns property worth under **₹5,000** may get **community service**.

## B-49 · A, intending to commit theft, enters B's house at night and removes a heavy box to the courtyard, where he opens it. Finding nothing worth taking, he leaves it there and goes away. Did A commit any offence?

**Asked as:** Dec 2023 Q7 (problem). Treated in C-19. Related: A-18, A-115 to A-117.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** issue and rule 3 · theft or attempt 3 · trespass 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Issue.** What offences, if any, has A committed?

**Law.** *Theft* (BNS s. 303(1)): dishonestly moving movable property out of another's possession without consent; **any moving suffices** (Expl. 3; the bullock illustration). *Attempt* (s. 62): an act towards the offence that falls short of completion; an empty box is still an attempt (illustration to IPC s. 511) †.

**Application.**
1. *Theft or attempt.* The box was in B's possession; A moved it without consent, intending to take dishonestly. **If he intended to take the box itself**, theft was **complete on moving**; that he found nothing and left it is immaterial (*K.N. Mehra*, 1957 †). **If he intended to take only the contents** and the box was empty, he is guilty of **attempt to commit theft**. The two are alternatives.
2. *Trespass.* A entered B's house at night to commit theft: **house-trespass**, and **lurking house-trespass or house-breaking by night** if he concealed his entry or entered in one of the listed ways (s. 330). Punishment: up to **five years** and fine; up to **fourteen years** if theft was intended (s. 331(4)) ‡.

**Conclusion.** A is guilty of house-trespass by night to commit theft, and of theft or attempt to commit theft.

## B-50 · Define extortion. When does extortion become robbery and robbery become dacoity?

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q7 ("extortion; when dacoity"). Related: C-20, A-105 to A-108.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** extortion 4 · robbery 2 · dacoity 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**Extortion (BNS s. 308(1); IPC s. 383) ‡.** Whoever **intentionally puts any person in fear of any injury** to that person or another, and thereby **dishonestly induces** the person to **deliver** any property, valuable security, or anything signed or sealed which may be converted into a valuable security.
*Essentials.* Intentional fear of injury; dishonest inducement; **delivery** by the person put in fear (without it, only attempt: s. 308(3)).
*Punishment (s. 308(2)) ‡.* Up to **7 years**, or fine, or both; higher if the fear is of death, grievous hurt or a grave accusation.

**Robbery (s. 309(3); IPC s. 390).** Extortion is robbery if the offender is **in the presence** of the person put in fear and puts him in fear of **instant death, instant hurt or instant wrongful restraint**, and thereby induces him **then and there** to deliver the thing ‡. Punishment: rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years** (14 on a highway at night).

**Dacoity (s. 310(1); IPC s. 391).** When **five or more** persons conjointly commit or attempt a robbery, **including those present and aiding**, each commits dacoity. Punishment: life, or rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years**, and fine (s. 310(2)); if one commits murder, every member (s. 310(3)) ‡.

## B-51 · Distinguish between theft, robbery and dacoity

**Asked as:** Dec 2022 Q12 asked "theft; when is it robbery" at 20 marks (C-19, C-20). Not yet set at 10 marks. Related: A-106 to A-108.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** table 7 · relationship 2 · conclusion 1

### Answer

| Point | Theft (s. 303) | Robbery (s. 309) | Dacoity (s. 310) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Dishonest moving of movable property without consent | **Theft or extortion plus** violence, or fear of **instant** violence | **Robbery by five or more** |
| Force or fear | Not needed | Voluntarily causing, or attempting, death, hurt or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death, hurt or restraint | The same, by a gang |
| Presence of victim | Not needed | Needed | Needed |
| Number | One or more | One or more | **Five or more**, including those present and aiding |
| Punishment | Up to **3 years**, or fine, or both | Rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years** (14 on a highway at night); hurt: life or up to 10 years | Life, or rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years**; murder: **death**, life or at least 10 years, for every member |

All ‡ (IPC ss. 378–379, 390–392, 395–396).

**Relationship.** In all robbery there is either **theft or extortion** (s. 309(1)). Every dacoity is a robbery.

*Illustration.* A takes Z's watch while Z sleeps: theft. A snatches it and strikes Z to get away: robbery. Five men do this together: dacoity.

**Conclusion.** The three form a ladder: theft, with violence, becomes robbery; robbery by five or more is dacoity.

## B-52 · Distinguish criminal breach of trust from dishonest misappropriation of property

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q5 set "theft v. misappropriation" at 4 marks (A-104). 20-mark form: C-21. Related: A-109 to A-111.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** two definitions 4 · table 4 · illustrations and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Dishonest misappropriation (BNS s. 314; IPC s. 403) †.** Whoever dishonestly misappropriates, or converts to his own use, any movable property. Possession is obtained **innocently** (finding, delivery by mistake). Punishment: six months to two years and fine †.

**Criminal breach of trust (s. 316(1); IPC s. 405) ‡.** Whoever, being **entrusted with property or with any dominion over it**, dishonestly misappropriates or converts it to his own use, or dishonestly uses or disposes of it in violation of a direction of law or a legal contract about the trust, or wilfully suffers another to do so.

| Point | Misappropriation | Criminal breach of trust |
|---|---|---|
| Possession | Obtained innocently | Obtained by **entrustment** |
| Entrustment | None | **Essential** |
| Dishonesty arises | After possession | After entrustment |
| Punishment | 6 months to 2 years and fine † | Up to **5 years**; clerk, servant or carrier: up to **7 years**; public servant, banker, agent: **life or up to 10 years** ‡ |

*Illustrations.* A finds a ring on the road and keeps it: misappropriation. Z leaves a plate with a warehouse-keeper A, who sells it: breach of trust.

**Conclusion.** Breach of trust is misappropriation after entrustment, and it is punished more heavily.

## B-53 · Define cheating. How does it differ from breach of contract?

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q3 ("cheating") and Dec 2025 Q4 ("cheating by personation") at 4 marks (A-112, A-113). 20-mark form: C-21.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** definition 3 · essentials 3 · punishment and personation 2 · breach of contract 2

### Answer

**BNS s. 318(1) (IPC s. 415) ‡.** Whoever, by **deceiving any person**, **fraudulently or dishonestly induces** him to **deliver any property**, or to **consent that any person shall retain any property**, or **intentionally induces him to do or omit to do anything** he would not do or omit if not deceived, and which causes or is likely to cause **damage or harm to him in body, mind, reputation or property**, cheats. **Explanation:** dishonest concealment of facts is deception.

**Essentials.** (1) Deception; (2) fraudulent or dishonest inducement; (3) delivery, retention or an act or omission; (4) damage or harm, actual or likely; (5) the dishonest intention exists **at the time of the inducement**.

**Punishment (s. 318) ‡.** Up to **3 years**, or fine, or both; up to **5 years** where the cheat was bound to protect the victim's interest; up to **7 years** and fine where property is dishonestly induced to be delivered.

**Personation (s. 319; IPC s. 416) ‡.** Cheating by pretending to be another, real or imaginary: up to **5 years**.

**Breach of contract.** Failure to keep a promise is not cheating unless the **dishonest intention existed when the promise was made** (*Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v. State of Bihar*, 2000 †).

## B-54 · Define mischief. Explain criminal trespass, house-trespass and house-breaking

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q3 set "mischief" at 4 marks (A-114). 20-mark form: C-22. Related: A-115 to A-117.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** mischief 4 · trespass 4 · punishment 2

### Answer

**Mischief (BNS s. 324(1); IPC s. 425) ‡.** Intending or knowing it likely to cause **wrongful loss or damage** to the public or any person, **destroying property or changing it or its situation** so as to destroy or diminish its value or utility. The offender's own property may be the subject (Expl. 2).
*Punishment (s. 324).* Up to **6 months**; loss of **₹20,000 or more**: up to 2 years; **₹1 lakh or more**: up to 5 years ‡.

**Criminal trespass (s. 329(1); IPC s. 441) †.** Entering property in another's possession with intent to commit an offence or to **intimidate, insult or annoy**, or unlawfully remaining there with that intent.
**House-trespass (s. 329(2); IPC s. 442) †.** Criminal trespass in a building used as a **dwelling**, a place of worship or a place for the custody of property.
**Lurking house-trespass (s. 330; IPC s. 443) †.** House-trespass after **taking precautions to conceal** it.
**House-breaking (s. 330; IPC s. 445) †.** House-trespass by one of six modes of entry (for example scaling a wall or opening a lock).

**Punishment (s. 331) ‡.** Up to **2 years**; by night **3**; to commit theft **10**; by night, to commit theft **14**.
