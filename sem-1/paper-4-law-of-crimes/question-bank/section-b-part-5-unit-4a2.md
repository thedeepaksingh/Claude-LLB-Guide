## B-41 · Distinguish between wrongful restraint and wrongful confinement

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q8 ("restraint v confinement"). Set in Section C as Q13(b) in Dec 2023 (C-16). Related: A-86, A-87.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** two definitions 3 · table 5 · illustrations and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Wrongful restraint (BNS s. 126; IPC s. 339):** voluntarily obstructing any person so as to prevent him from proceeding in **any direction in which he has a right to proceed** ‡.
**Wrongful confinement (s. 127; IPC s. 340):** wrongfully restraining a person so as to prevent him from proceeding **beyond certain circumscribing limits** ‡.

| Point | Restraint | Confinement |
|---|---|---|
| Relation | **Genus** | **Species** |
| Prevention | In **some** direction; others are open | In **all** directions, within limits |
| Limits | None | Circumscribing limits essential |
| Obstruction | Partial | Complete |
| Punishment | Up to **1 month**, or ₹5,000, or both (s. 126(2)) | Up to **1 year**, or ₹5,000, or both (s. 127(2)); **3 days or more**: up to 3 years; **10 days or more**: up to 5 years and fine of at least ₹10,000 ‡ |

*Illustrations.* A parks a lorry across a lane; Z can reverse and leave by another road: **restraint**. A locks Z in a room: **confinement**. In *Bird v. Jones* (1845) a person stopped from crossing a bridge, but free to go back, was not imprisoned †.

**Conclusion.** Every confinement involves a restraint, but not every restraint is a confinement.

## B-42 · Define force, criminal force and assault. How do they differ?

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-16. Related: A-88, A-89 (Dec 2022 Q2), A-90.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** three definitions 4 · table 4 · punishment and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Force (BNS s. 128; IPC s. 349).** A person uses force to another if he causes **motion, change of motion or cessation of motion** to that person, or to a substance in contact with him, or affecting his sense of feeling ‡.

**Criminal force (s. 129; IPC s. 350).** **Intentionally** using force to a person **without his consent**, to commit an offence, or intending, or knowing it likely, to cause **injury, fear or annoyance** †.

**Assault (s. 130; IPC s. 351).** Making any **gesture or preparation**, intending or knowing it likely to cause a person **present** to **apprehend** that criminal force is about to be used. **Mere words are not assault**, but words may give a gesture its meaning †.

| Point | Criminal force | Assault |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Application of force | Threat of force |
| Contact | Needed | Not needed |
| Apprehension | Not needed | Essential |
| Example | A push from behind; spitting at a man | Raising a stick, saying "I will beat you" |

**Punishment (s. 131; IPC s. 352) ‡.** Up to **three months**, or fine up to ₹1,000, or both, unless on grave and sudden provocation.

**Conclusion.** Assault is the threat; criminal force is its application.

## B-43 · Distinguish between kidnapping and abduction

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q8 and Dec 2023 Q8. Set at 20 marks in Dec 2025 Q12 (C-15). Related: A-91, A-92.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** two definitions 3 · table 5 · overlap and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Kidnapping (BNS s. 137; IPC ss. 359–361)** is of two kinds ‡: (a) **from India**: conveying a person beyond India without consent; (b) **from lawful guardianship**: **taking or enticing** a **child** (under 18) or a person of unsound mind out of the keeping of the lawful guardian without the guardian's consent.
**Abduction (s. 138; IPC s. 362)**: **by force compelling, or by deceitful means inducing**, a person to go from any place ‡.

| Point | Kidnapping | Abduction |
|---|---|---|
| Victim | Child or person of unsound mind (or any person, from India) | Any person |
| Means | Taking or enticing | Force or deceit |
| Consent | Guardian's consent counts; **child's is immaterial** | Free consent removes the offence |
| Punishable alone | **Yes**: up to 7 years and fine (s. 137(2)) | **No**: only with a specified intent |
| Nature | Not continuing | Continuing † |

**Overlap.** A girl of fifteen induced by deceit to leave her father's house is both kidnapped and abducted.

**Conclusion.** Kidnapping protects the guardian's right over the child; abduction protects personal liberty, and is punishable only where done for a purpose such as murder, ransom or marriage (ss. 87, 140) ‡.

## B-44 · Explain kidnapping from lawful guardianship. When is "taking" established?

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. 20-mark form: C-15. Related: A-93, A-94.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** text and essentials 4 · cases 4 · exception and punishment 2

### Answer

**BNS s. 137(1)(b) (IPC s. 361).** Whoever **takes or entices** a **child** or a **person of unsound mind** out of the keeping of the **lawful guardian**, without the guardian's consent, kidnaps him from lawful guardianship. The IPC required a male under sixteen or a female under eighteen; the BNS says "child", meaning a person under eighteen (s. 2(3)), which is my reading and not tested by a case †. **Exception:** a person who in good faith believes himself the father of an illegitimate child, or entitled to its custody, unless for an immoral or unlawful purpose ‡.

**Essentials.** (1) Taking or enticing; (2) a child or person of unsound mind; (3) out of the **keeping** of the lawful guardian; (4) without his consent. **The child's consent is immaterial.**

**Cases** †
- *S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras* (1965): "taking" differs from allowing a minor to accompany; where the girl leaves of her own accord and the accused takes **no active part**, there is no kidnapping.
- *State of Haryana v. Raja Ram* (1973): "takes or entices" covers persuasion or influence that brings about her leaving.
- *Thakorlal D. Vadgama v. State of Gujarat* (1973): enticement is **inducement**.

**Punishment (s. 137(2)) ‡.** Up to **seven years** and fine.

## B-45 · Explain dowry death. State its essentials and the presumption

**Asked as:** Dec 2025 Q2 set "dowry death" at 4 marks (A-95); Dec 2024 Q11 set "dowry death: BNS v IPC" at 20 marks (C-17). This is the 10-mark form.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** text 3 · essentials 3 · presumption and cases 3 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**BNS s. 80(1) (IPC s. 304B) ‡.** Where the death of a woman is caused by **burns or bodily injury**, or occurs **otherwise than under normal circumstances**, within **seven years of her marriage**, and it is shown that **soon before her death** she was subjected to **cruelty or harassment by her husband or any relative of her husband for, or in connection with, any demand for dowry**, such death is a "**dowry death**" and the husband or relative is **deemed to have caused it**. "Dowry" has the meaning in s. 2 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.

**Punishment (s. 80(2)) ‡.** Imprisonment of **not less than seven years**, up to **life**.

**Essentials.** (1) Death of a woman, in the circumstances above; (2) within seven years of marriage; (3) cruelty or harassment **soon before** death; (4) by the husband or a relative; (5) in connection with a **dowry demand**.

**Presumption.** The court **shall presume** dowry death once the foundational facts are shown (Evidence Act s. 113B; BSA s. 118 †).

**"Soon before"** has no fixed limit but needs a **proximate and live link** (*Kans Raj v. State of Punjab*, 2000 †).

**BNS and IPC.** Text and punishment are substantially the same ‡.

## B-46 · Explain stalking and cruelty by husband or relatives under the BNS

**Asked as:** Dec 2024 Q5 set "stalking" at 4 marks (A-97). Part of C-17 at 20 marks. Related: A-96.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** stalking 4 · cruelty 4 · cases and conclusion 2

### Answer

**Stalking (BNS s. 78; IPC s. 354D, inserted in 2013) ‡.** A **man** commits stalking who (1) **follows a woman** and contacts, or attempts to contact, her to foster personal interaction **repeatedly, despite a clear indication of disinterest**; or (2) **monitors her use of the internet, email or other electronic communication**.
*Provisos.* Not stalking if done to prevent or detect crime under State authority, under any law, or if reasonable and justified in the circumstances.
*Punishment (s. 78(2)).* First conviction: up to **three years** and fine; later conviction: up to **five years** and fine. It is **gender-specific** and covers cyberstalking †.

**Cruelty (BNS s. 85; IPC s. 498A) ‡.** A **husband or his relative** who subjects a woman to **cruelty**: up to **three years** and fine. **Section 86** defines cruelty as (a) wilful conduct likely to drive her to suicide or to cause grave injury to life, limb or health, or (b) harassment to coerce her or her relatives to meet an unlawful demand for property.

**Cases** †. *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar* (2014): no automatic arrest in s. 498A cases; police must follow the statutory checklist.

**Conclusion.** Both protect women, and both have been criticised for gender-specific wording and misuse.

## B-47 · Discuss the offences against a child under the BNS

**Asked as:** not yet set at 10 marks. Set in Section C as Q13(b) in Dec 2024 (C-18). Related: A-98 to A-101.
**Marks:** 10 · **Limit:** ≤200 words
**Rubric (mine):** child defined 2 · offences 5 · special laws 2 · conclusion 1

### Answer

**"Child"** means a person **under eighteen** (BNS s. 2(3)) ‡.

**Offences in the Code** (headings ‡; punishments for ss. 93–99 not checked †)
- **Exposure and abandonment** of a child under twelve by the parent or person in charge (s. 93; IPC s. 317).
- **Concealment of birth** by secretly disposing of the body (s. 94; IPC s. 318).
- **Hiring, employing or engaging a child to commit an offence** (s. 95; new).
- **Procuration of a child** (s. 96; IPC s. 366A).
- **Kidnapping or abducting a child under ten** to steal from the child (s. 97; IPC s. 369).
- **Selling or buying a child for prostitution** (ss. 98, 99; IPC ss. 372, 373).
- **Kidnapping from lawful guardianship** (s. 137(1)(b)); **abetment of suicide of a child** (s. 107); **trafficking**, with higher tiers for children (s. 143) ‡†.

**Special laws.** The **POCSO Act, 2012** (sexual offences); the **Juvenile Justice Act, 2015**; the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006; the Child Labour Act, 1986 as amended †.

**Constitution.** Articles 15(3), 21A, 23, 24, 39(e)–(f), 45 †.

**Case.** *Independent Thought v. Union of India* (2017) read down the exception to rape so that intercourse with a wife aged 15 to 18 is rape †.

*Word count: 190*
