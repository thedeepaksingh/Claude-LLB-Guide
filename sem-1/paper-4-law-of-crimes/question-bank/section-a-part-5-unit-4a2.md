**A-86 · Wrongful restraint** · *Related: B-41, C-16*

BNS s. 126 (IPC s. 339): whoever voluntarily obstructs any person so as to prevent him from proceeding in any direction in which he has a right to proceed is said wrongfully to restrain him ‡. Punishment: simple imprisonment up to one month, or fine up to ₹5,000, or both (s. 126(2)) ‡. Example: blocking a lane while the person is free to go back.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-87"></a>
**A-87 · Wrongful confinement** · *Related: B-41, C-16*

BNS s. 127 (IPC s. 340): whoever wrongfully restrains a person so as to prevent him from proceeding beyond certain circumscribing limits wrongfully confines him ‡. Punishment: up to one year, or fine up to ₹5,000, or both; three days or more: up to three years; ten days or more: up to five years and fine of at least ₹10,000 ‡.

*Word count: 59*

<a id="a-88"></a>
**A-88 · Force** · *Related: B-42, C-16*

BNS s. 128 (IPC s. 349): a person uses force to another if he causes motion, change of motion or cessation of motion to that person, or to a substance in contact with that person's body, clothing or sense of feeling ‡. Force is only a definition: it becomes an offence as criminal force (s. 129).

*Word count: 55*

<a id="a-89"></a>
**A-89 · Criminal force** · *Asked: Dec 2022 Q2* · *Related: B-42, C-16*

BNS s. 129 (IPC s. 350): whoever intentionally uses force to any person, without that person's consent, in order to commit any offence, or intending, or knowing it likely, to cause injury, fear or annoyance to that person, uses criminal force †. Example: pushing a man from behind. Punishment: up to three months, or fine up to ₹1,000, or both (s. 131) ‡.

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-90"></a>
**A-90 · Assault** · *Related: B-42, C-16*

BNS s. 130 (IPC s. 351): whoever makes any gesture or preparation, intending or knowing it likely to cause a person present to apprehend that criminal force is about to be used against him, commits assault †. Mere words are not assault, but words may give a gesture its meaning. Contact is not needed. Punishment as for criminal force (s. 131) ‡.

*Word count: 60*

<a id="a-91"></a>
**A-91 · Kidnapping** · *Related: B-43, C-15*

BNS s. 137 (IPC ss. 359–361): kidnapping is of two kinds ‡: from India (conveying a person beyond the limits of India without consent) and from lawful guardianship (taking or enticing a child, or a person of unsound mind, out of the keeping of the lawful guardian without the guardian's consent). Punishment: up to seven years and fine (s. 137(2)) ‡.

*Word count: 59*

<a id="a-92"></a>
**A-92 · Abduction** · *Related: B-43, C-15*

BNS s. 138 (IPC s. 362): whoever by force compels, or by any deceitful means induces, any person to go from any place is said to abduct that person ‡. The victim may be of any age. Abduction is not punishable by itself; it is punishable only when done with a specified intent, as to murder, for ransom or to compel marriage (ss. 140, 87) ‡.

*Word count: 64*

<a id="a-93"></a>
**A-93 · Kidnapping from lawful guardianship** · *Related: B-44, C-15*

BNS s. 137(1)(b) (IPC s. 361): taking or enticing a child, or a person of unsound mind, out of the keeping of the lawful guardian without his consent. The child's consent is immaterial. "Taking" means more than allowing a minor to accompany (*S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras*, 1965 †). The BNS says "child" for both sexes, meaning a person under eighteen (s. 2(3)) †.

*Word count: 63*

<a id="a-94"></a>
**A-94 · Kidnapping for ransom** · *Related: B-43, C-15*

BNS s. 140(2) (IPC s. 364A): whoever kidnaps or abducts a person, or keeps him in detention after kidnapping or abduction, and threatens to cause death or hurt, or causes death or hurt, to compel the Government, a foreign State or any person to do or abstain from any act, or to pay a ransom, is punished with death or imprisonment for life, and fine ‡.

*Word count: 65*

<a id="a-95"></a>
**A-95 · Dowry death** · *Asked: Dec 2025 Q2* · *Related: B-45, C-17*

BNS s. 80 (IPC s. 304B): where a woman's death is caused by burns or bodily injury, or occurs otherwise than in normal circumstances, within seven years of marriage, and soon before her death she was subjected to cruelty or harassment by her husband or his relatives for dowry, it is dowry death. Punishment: not less than seven years, up to life ‡. The court presumes it once the facts are shown †.

*Word count: 71*

<a id="a-96"></a>
**A-96 · Cruelty by husband or relatives** · *Related: B-46, C-17*

BNS s. 85 (IPC s. 498A): a husband or his relative who subjects a woman to cruelty is punished with imprisonment up to three years and fine ‡. Section 86 defines cruelty as wilful conduct likely to drive her to suicide or to cause grave injury to life, limb or health, or harassment to coerce her or her relatives to meet an unlawful demand for property ‡.

*Word count: 65*

<a id="a-97"></a>
**A-97 · Stalking** · *Asked: Dec 2024 Q5* · *Related: B-46, C-17*

BNS s. 78 (IPC s. 354D): a man who follows a woman and contacts, or attempts to contact her, to foster personal interaction repeatedly despite a clear indication of disinterest, or who monitors her use of the internet, email or other electronic communication, commits stalking. First conviction: up to three years and fine; later conviction: up to five years and fine ‡.

*Word count: 61*

<a id="a-98"></a>
**A-98 · Child (BNS s. 2(3))** · *Related: B-47, C-18*

BNS s. 2(3): "child" means any person below the age of eighteen years ‡. The IPC had no general definition and used different ages in different sections; for kidnapping it was sixteen for a male and eighteen for a female †. The uniform age of eighteen now applies to the offences against children under the Sanhita.

*Word count: 54*

<a id="a-99"></a>
**A-99 · Exposure and abandonment of a child** · *Related: B-47, C-18*

BNS s. 93 (IPC s. 317): the father or mother of a child under twelve, or a person having its care, who exposes or leaves it in any place with the intention of wholly abandoning it, commits the offence ‡ (heading). Under the IPC the punishment was up to seven years, or fine, or both; the BNS terms were not checked †. If the child dies, the offender may be tried for murder or culpable homicide.

*Word count: 74*

<a id="a-100"></a>
**A-100 · Hiring a child to commit an offence** · *Related: B-47, C-18*

BNS s. 95 is new: hiring, employing or engaging a child to commit an offence ‡ (heading). The punishment was not checked †. It targets adults who use children as instruments of crime. A child under seven is incapable of crime (s. 20), and a child under eighteen who offends is dealt with under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015.

*Word count: 57*

<a id="a-101"></a>
**A-101 · Selling or buying a child for prostitution** · *Related: B-47, C-18*

BNS s. 98 (IPC s. 372) punishes selling, letting to hire or otherwise disposing of a child with intent that he be employed for prostitution or illicit intercourse; s. 99 (IPC s. 373) punishes buying or obtaining possession of a child for that purpose ‡ (headings). Under the IPC each carried up to ten years and fine; the BNS terms were not checked †.

*Word count: 62*
