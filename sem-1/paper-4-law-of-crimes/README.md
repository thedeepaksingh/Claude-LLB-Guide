## How to use this pack

**Three-pass plan**
1. **Understand (pass 1):** read each unit once, in order. Criminal law is a paper of definitions: learn the words of each section, then the essentials in order.
2. **Memorise (pass 2):** use the quick-revision boxes and the one-page sheets below; write each section number (BNS and IPC), its essentials and one leading case from memory.
3. **Practise (pass 3):** write the Section C questions under timed conditions, compare with the model answers in the [question bank](question-bank/README.md), then sit the three [mock papers](question-bank/mock-exam-1.md) and mark yourself with the [review checklist](question-bank/answer-review-checklist.md).

**Answer-writing template for a theory answer (Section C; shorten it to 200 words for Section B, 75 for Section A)**

| Step | What to write | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | The definition, with the BNS section and the IPC section in brackets | 2–3 lines |
| 2. Essentials | The numbered ingredients of the offence, in the order of the section | 4–8 lines |
| 3. Cases and illustrations | For each essential that is disputed, the leading case with two lines of facts and the holding; one illustration from the Code | the bulk |
| 4. Exceptions and punishment | The exceptions or defences; the punishment | 4–6 lines |
| 5. Distinction, criticism, change | The difference from the nearest offence; what the BNS changed; one criticism | 3–5 lines |
| 6. Conclusion | One or two lines that answer the question | 2 lines |

**Template for a problem question (IRAC)**

| Step | What to write | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Facts | The relevant facts only, with the parties named A and B | 1 |
| Issue | The legal question, in one sentence | 1 |
| Rule | The offence, its section and its essentials | 3 |
| Application | Each essential applied to **these** facts; the alternative if the answer turns on A's intention | 4 |
| Conclusion | A clear answer ("A is guilty of house-trespass by night and of theft, or in the alternative of attempt to commit theft") | 1 |

**Habits that earn marks:** give the section number *and* the Code (BNS s. 101; IPC s. 300); give the case name *and* year; state the facts in two lines; use the Latin maxim and then translate it; distinguish the offence from its nearest neighbour (theft and extortion; kidnapping and abduction; culpable homicide and murder); end with a short conclusion. In Section A, stop at 75 words.

## One-page revision sheet: sections and rules

| Topic | The rule in one line (BNS first; IPC in brackets) |
|---|---|
| Crime and offence | The Code defines "offence" (s. 2(24); IPC s. 40) ‡: a thing made punishable. Crime = act or omission + guilty mind + injury, punishable by the State |
| Elements | Human being · *mens rea* · *actus reus* · injury (s. 2(14); IPC s. 44) ‡ |
| *Mens rea* | Intention, knowledge, rashness or negligence, dishonesty, fraud. Presumed unless excluded (*Sherras*; *Nathulal*); strict liability for social-welfare offences (*M.H. George*; *Hobbs*) |
| Stages | Intention (not punished) · preparation (not punished, except where the Code says, e.g. dacoity, s. 310(4)) · attempt (s. 62; IPC s. 511) † · accomplishment. Tests: *Abhayanand Mishra*, *Malkiat Singh*, *Mohd. Yakub* |
| Punishments | Death · life · imprisonment (rigorous or simple) · forfeiture of property † · fine · community service (s. 4; IPC s. 53) †. Death only in the rarest of rare cases (*Bachan Singh*); mandatory death struck down (*Mithu*) |
| Common intention (s. 3(5); IPC s. 34) † | Two or more persons; a prior meeting of minds; an act in furtherance; participation. Creates no offence |
| Common object (s. 190; IPC s. 149) ‡ | An unlawful assembly of five or more (s. 189); an offence in prosecution of the common object, or one the members **knew to be likely** |
| Abetment (s. 45; IPC s. 107) † | Instigation · conspiracy with an act or illegal omission · intentional aid. The offence need not be committed |
| Conspiracy (s. 61; IPC ss. 120A–120B) † | An agreement of two or more to do an illegal act; an overt act is needed unless the object is a serious offence |
| General exceptions (ss. 14–44; IPC ss. 76–106) ‡ | Mistake of fact in good faith excuses, mistake of law does not · accident · necessity (s. 19) · infancy (ss. 20–21) · insanity (s. 22: cognitive incapacity) · intoxication (ss. 23–24) · consent (ss. 25–31) · compulsion (s. 32) · trifles (s. 33) |
| Private defence (ss. 34–44) ‡ | Defence, not retaliation; no duty to retreat; restrictions (s. 37); death only in the seven cases of s. 38; property: s. 41; burden on the accused to a preponderance of probability |
| Culpable homicide and murder (ss. 100–101; IPC ss. 299–300) ‡ | Genus and species: four limbs of murder; five Exceptions (provocation, private defence, public servant, sudden fight, consent); *Virsa Singh*, *Punnayya*, *Govinda* |
| Punishment for murder (s. 103; s. 104; s. 105) ‡ | Death or life and fine; s. 103(2) group murder (new); s. 104 life-convict (not mandatory); s. 105 culpable homicide: life or 5–10 years |
| Death by negligence (s. 106(1)) ‡ | Up to five years (two for a doctor); gross negligence (*Jacob Mathew*). Hit and run, s. 106(2): not in force as far as I found † |
| Hurt, grievous hurt (ss. 114, 116) ‡ | Bodily pain, disease or infirmity; eight designated kinds. Attempt to murder, s. 109 |
| Restraint and confinement (ss. 126–127) ‡ | Obstruction in one direction; confinement within limits. Force (s. 128), criminal force (s. 129), assault (s. 130) |
| Kidnapping and abduction (ss. 137–138) ‡ | Kidnapping: from India or from lawful guardianship (a child under eighteen); abduction: force or deceit, any age, punishable only with a specified intent |
| Dowry death (s. 80), cruelty (ss. 85–86), stalking (s. 78) ‡ | Seven years to life, soon before death, within seven years; cruelty up to three years; stalking up to three years (first) |
| Theft (s. 303; IPC s. 378) ‡ | Dishonest moving of movable property out of possession without consent; any moving suffices; up to three years |
| Extortion, robbery, dacoity (ss. 308–310) ‡ | Fear of injury and delivery (up to seven years) · theft or extortion with hurt or fear (ten years; fourteen on a highway at night) · robbery by five or more (life or ten years) |
| Misappropriation, breach of trust, cheating (ss. 314, 316, 318) | Dishonest conversion of property innocently got † · entrusted property (up to five years) ‡ · deception and dishonest inducement (up to three years) ‡ |
| Mischief, trespass (ss. 324, 329–331) ‡ | Wrongful loss or damage by destroying or changing property (six months) · entry with intent · house-trespass and house-breaking |
| Sedition and s. 152 | IPC s. 124A is **not** in the BNS; s. 152 (acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India) replaced it ‡. *Kedar Nath* (1962). Validity undecided at 22 May 2026 |
| Unlawful assembly, riot, affray (ss. 189, 191, 194) ‡ | Five or more with a common object; force used in prosecuting it; two or more fighting in public |
| Bigamy (s. 82) ‡ | Second marriage void by reason of the first spouse's life: up to seven years; with concealment up to ten. Adultery (IPC s. 497) struck down in *Joseph Shine* (2018); not in the BNS |
