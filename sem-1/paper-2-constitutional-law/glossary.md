|---|---|---|

| **Ultra vires** | Beyond the powers conferred by law or the Constitution | Art. 13 (Unit 3) |
| **Intra vires** | Within powers | Unit 3 |
| **Ex post facto law** | A law that operates retrospectively to make an act criminal or increase its punishment | Art. 20(1) (Unit 7) |
| **Autrefois acquit / autrefois convict** | Previously acquitted / previously convicted: a plea barring a second trial | Art. 20(2) (Unit 7) |
| **Habeas corpus** | "You may have the body": writ against unlawful detention | Unit 12 |
| **Mandamus** | "We command": writ compelling a public duty | Unit 12 |
| **Certiorari** | "To be certified": writ quashing an order of an inferior body | Unit 12 |

| **Locus standi** | Standing: right to approach the court | PIL (Unit 12) |
| **Res judicata** | A matter already decided cannot be re-agitated | Art. 32 / *Daryao* (Unit 12) |
| **Stare decisis** | Stand by decided cases: doctrine of precedent | Unit 3 |

| **Mala fide** | In bad faith | Unit 4 |

## B. Constitutional doctrines and tests

| Term | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic structure doctrine** | Parliament may amend the Constitution but cannot alter its basic features (*Kesavananda*, 1973) | Unit 3 |
| **Doctrine of eclipse** | A pre-Constitution law inconsistent with FRs is not void but dormant; revives when the inconsistency is removed | Art. 13 (Unit 3) |
| **Doctrine of severability** | Only the offending part of a law is void if it can be separated | Art. 13 (Unit 3) |
| **Doctrine of waiver** | Whether a person can give up a Fundamental Right (generally not for rights in public interest) | Unit 3 |
| **Prospective overruling** | A decision operates only for the future | *Golaknath* (Unit 3) |
| **Pith and substance** | Courts look at the true nature of a law to see which legislative list it falls under | Unit 1 |
| **Colourable legislation** | A legislature doing indirectly what it cannot do directly | Unit 1 |
| **Harmonious construction** | Reading provisions together so that each gets effect | Units 10, 13 |
| **Reasonable classification** | Equality permits classification based on an intelligible differentia with a rational nexus to the object | Art. 14 (Unit 4) |
| **Intelligible differentia** | A clear basis distinguishing those grouped from those left out | Unit 4 |
| **Rational nexus** | A reasoned connection between the classification and the law's object | Unit 4 |
| **Manifest arbitrariness** | A law capricious, irrational or without adequate determining principle | *Shayara Bano* (Units 4, 10) |
| **New concept of equality** | Equality as non-arbitrariness (*Royappa*, *Maneka*) | Unit 4 |
| **Legitimate expectation** | A reasonable expectation created by a promise or practice, protected from arbitrary frustration | Unit 4 |
| **Promissory estoppel** | The Government can be held to a promise on which a person has relied | Unit 4 |
| **Creamy layer** | The advanced section of a backward class excluded from reservation | *Indra Sawhney* (Unit 5) |
| **Sub-classification** | Dividing a reserved category into groups for fair distribution of benefits | *Davinder Singh* (Unit 5) |
| **Golden triangle** | Arts. 14, 19 and 21 read together | *Maneka Gandhi* (Unit 8) |
| **Procedure established by law** | Art. 21 standard: a valid law with fair, just and reasonable procedure | Unit 8 |
| **Due process** | US standard of substantive and procedural fairness | Unit 8 |
| **Proportionality** | Legitimate aim, rational connection, necessity (least restrictive means), balance | Units 6, 8 |
| **Public order / law and order / security of the State** | Three concentric circles (*Lohia*) | Unit 6 |
| **Chilling effect** | Deterrence of lawful speech through vague or severe laws | *Shreya Singhal* (Unit 6) |
| **Prior restraint** | Restricting speech before publication | Unit 6 |
| **Community standards test** | Obscenity judged by contemporary community values (*Aveek Sarkar*) | Unit 6 |
| **Testimonial compulsion** | Compelling a person to give incriminating evidence from personal knowledge | Art. 20(3) (Unit 7) |
| **Double jeopardy** | Being prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence | Unit 7 |
| **Rarest of rare** | Death penalty only in the rarest cases (*Bachan Singh*) | Unit 8 |
| **Informational privacy** | Control over personal information | *Puttaswamy* (Unit 8) |
| **Absolute liability** | Enterprise engaged in hazardous activity liable without exceptions (*M.C. Mehta*) | Unit 8 |
| **Precautionary principle / polluter pays** | Environmental principles accepted in *Vellore Citizens* | Unit 8 |
| **Passive euthanasia / living will** | Withdrawal of life support / advance medical directive | *Common Cause* (Unit 8) |
| **Essential religious practice** | A practice integral to a religion, protected from State regulation | Unit 10 |
| **Religious denomination** | A group with a common faith, organisation and distinctive name | Art. 26 (Unit 10) |
| **Principled distance** | India's model of secularism: equal respect with power to regulate and reform | Unit 10 |
| **Minority character** | The identity of an institution as established for a minority | Art. 30 (Unit 11) |
| **Continuing mandamus** | The court retains the case and supervises compliance | Unit 12 |
| **Epistolary jurisdiction** | Treating a letter as a writ petition | Unit 12 |
| **Horizontal application** | Enforcing rights against private persons | Units 3, 9 |
| **Cooperative federalism** | Union and States working together | Unit 1 |
| **Quasi-federal** | Federal in form, unitary in spirit (*K.C. Wheare*) | Unit 1 |
| **Asymmetric federalism** | Different States have different constitutional arrangements (Arts. 370, 371–371J) | Unit 1 |
| **Constitutional morality** | Fidelity to constitutional values over social or religious morality | Units 8, 10 |
| **Non-justiciable** | Not enforceable in court | DPSP, FD (Units 13, 14) |

## C. Institutions and procedure

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Constitution Bench** | A bench of at least five Supreme Court judges to decide substantial questions of constitutional law (Art. 145(3)) |
| **Presidential Reference** | The President seeks the Supreme Court's opinion under Art. 143 |
| **Curative petition** | A last remedy after review is dismissed (*Rupa Ashok Hurra*) |
| **Ninth Schedule** | List of laws placed beyond challenge by Art. 31B, subject to *Coelho* |
| **Tenth Schedule** | Anti-defection law (52nd Amendment, 1985) |
| **Public interest litigation (PIL)** | Litigation for public rights by a public-spirited person |
| **Public-law compensation** | Compensation awarded by constitutional courts for violation of Fundamental Rights |
| **Advisory Board** | Body reviewing preventive detention beyond three months (Art. 22) |
| **Preventive detention** | Detention to prevent a future act, not to punish |
| **Unit of minority** | The State is the unit for determining a minority (*T.M.A. Pai*) |
