## 1. Article 15: prohibition of discrimination

### 1.1 Text
- **15(1):** The State shall not discriminate against **any citizen** on grounds **only** of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** or any of them.
- **15(2):** No citizen shall, on these grounds, be subject to any disability, liability, restriction or condition regarding (a) **access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment**; or (b) the **use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, places of public resort** maintained wholly or partly out of State funds or dedicated to the general public. *(Binds private persons too.)*
- **15(3):** Special provision for **women and children**.
- **15(4):** (First Amendment, 1951) Special provision for the advancement of **socially and educationally backward classes** (SEBCs) or **SCs and STs**.
- **15(5):** (93rd Amendment, 2005) Special provision for SEBCs, SCs, STs in **admission to educational institutions**, including **private unaided** institutions, other than minority educational institutions covered by Art. 30(1).
- **15(6):** (103rd Amendment, 2019) Special provision for **economically weaker sections** (EWS) other than those in 15(4)–(5), in admission to educational institutions (including private, except minority) up to **10%** in addition to existing reservations.

### 1.2 Key terms
- **"Only"**: discrimination must be on grounds **only** of the listed ones; if other factors also operate, Art. 15(1) is not breached. 
- **"Discriminate"** means to make an adverse distinction; **special provision** (15(3)–(6)) is not discrimination.
- **"Sex"** includes **gender identity** (*NALSA v. Union of India*, (2014) 5 SCC 438: recognition of transgender persons as a third gender; Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019).
- **"Place of birth"** differs from **residence** (Art. 16(2) lists both; residence requirements are for Parliament under Art. 16(3)).

### 1.3 Article 15(3): women and children

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Yusuf Abdul Aziz v. State of Bombay** | AIR 1954 SC 321 | s. 497 IPC punishing only the man for adultery was valid under Art. 15(3) |
| **Joseph Shine v. Union of India** | (2019) 3 SCC 39 | Overruled *Yusuf Abdul Aziz*; s. 497 IPC violated Arts. 14, 15, 21; Art. 15(3) cannot be used to perpetuate stereotypes |
| **Air India v. Nargesh Meerza** | (1981) 4 SCC 335 | The rule terminating an air hostess's service on her **first pregnancy** was struck down as arbitrary and violative of Art. 14; other service conditions challenged in the case were treated differently (read the report for which) |
| **C.B. Muthamma v. Union of India** | (1979) 4 SCC 260 | Rules of the Foreign Service discriminating against married women held unconstitutional |
| **Anuj Garg v. Hotel Association of India** | (2008) 3 SCC 1 | Punjab Excise Act s. 30, which barred women from employment in bars, struck down; "protective" discrimination rooted in stereotypes violates Arts. 14, 15 |
| **Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan** | (1997) 6 SCC 241 | Guidelines against sexual harassment; gender equality |
| **Githa Hariharan v. Reserve Bank of India** | (1999) 2 SCC 228 | "natural guardian" provision in the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act read down so that the mother can be guardian in appropriate cases |
| **Secretary, Ministry of Defence v. Babita Puniya** | (2020) 7 SCC 469 | Permanent commission for women officers in the Army; stereotypes on physical and social differences rejected |
| **Indian Young Lawyers Association v. State of Kerala (Sabarimala)** | (2019) 11 SCC 1 (28 Sept 2018; *verify the report*) | Exclusion of women of menstruating age from Sabarimala temple held to violate Arts. 14, 15, 25 (a nine-judge reference on wider questions is pending; see Unit 10) |

### 1.4 Article 15(4): backward classes
- ***State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan*** AIR 1951 SC 226: a Government Order allocating medical-college seats by community **violated Art. 15(1) and Art. 29(2)**. This prompted the **First Amendment, 1951**, which inserted **Art. 15(4)**.
- ***M.R. Balaji v. State of Mysore*** AIR 1963 SC 649: reservation of 68% of seats in medical and engineering colleges held excessive; **caste cannot be the sole test**; reservations should generally not exceed **50%**.
- ***Indra Sawhney v. Union of India*** 1992 Supp (3) SCC 217: see §2.
- ***Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. Union of India*** (2008) 6 SCC 1: the **93rd Amendment** (Art. 15(5)) and the Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006 upheld; the **creamy layer** must be excluded from OBC reservation; 27% OBC reservation valid.
- ***Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India*** (2014) 8 SCC 1: Art. 15(5) valid, but cannot apply to **minority** educational institutions.
- ***Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India*** (decided **7 Nov 2022**, five judges, **3:2**): the **103rd Amendment** (Arts. 15(6), 16(6)) upheld; economic criterion alone is a valid classification; the 10% EWS reservation, which excludes SCs, STs and OBCs, does not violate the basic structure or equality code. Dissent by Lalit C.J. and Bhat J. (exclusion of SC/ST/OBC among the poor; 50% ceiling breach).

### 1.5 Article 15(2) and private persons
Art. 15(2) is one of the few rights enforceable against private persons (shops, hotels, wells). It is complemented by the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and Art. 17.

## 2. Article 16: equality of opportunity in public employment

### 2.1 Text
- **16(1):** Equality of opportunity for **all citizens** in matters relating to employment or appointment to **any office under the State**.
- **16(2):** No citizen shall be ineligible for, or discriminated against in respect of, any employment or office under the State on grounds **only** of **religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence** or any of them.
- **16(3):** Parliament may prescribe **residence** as a requirement for certain posts in a State or UT (Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957; Art. 35(a)).
- **16(4):** Reservation of appointments or posts in favour of any **backward class** not adequately represented in State services.
- **16(4A):** (77th Amendment, 1995; extended by 85th, 2001) Reservation **in promotion** for SC/ST with **consequential seniority**.
- **16(4B):** (81st Amendment, 2000) **Carry-forward** of unfilled reserved vacancies; not counted towards the 50% ceiling.
- **16(5):** Offices of religious or denominational institutions may be reserved for persons professing that religion.
- **16(6):** (103rd Amendment, 2019) Reservation of up to **10% for EWS**.

### 2.2 Cases before *Indra Sawhney*
- ***General Manager, Southern Railway v. Rangachari*** AIR 1962 SC 36: Art. 16(4) includes reservation in promotion (later restricted).
- ***T. Devadasan v. Union of India*** AIR 1964 SC 179: "carry forward" rule that took reservation above 50% held invalid.
- ***State of Kerala v. N.M. Thomas*** (1976) 2 SCC 310: Art. 16(4) is **not an exception** to Art. 16(1) but an application of equality; preferential treatment of SC/ST in promotion examinations was valid.

### 2.3 *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India* (1992 Supp (3) SCC 217) — the "Mandal Commission case"
**Background:** the Mandal Commission (1980) recommended 27% reservation for OBCs; the Government implemented it in 1990 (Office Memorandum of 13 Aug 1990) and added a 10% economic reservation (1991). A **nine-judge bench** decided the case (majority 6:3 on most points).

| Issue | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Reservation for OBCs under Art. 16(4)** | Valid; "backward class" in Art. 16(4) may be identified primarily by **caste** along with other criteria; but caste is not the sole test |
| **Creamy layer** | The **socially advanced** section of backward classes must be **excluded** |
| **50% ceiling** | Total reservations should **not exceed 50%** save in **extraordinary situations** |
| **Reservation in promotion** | Not permissible under Art. 16(4) (this part was overridden by the **77th Amendment** inserting Art. 16(4A); the Court gave a five-year grace period) |
| **Economic criterion alone** | The 10% economic reservation (1991) was **struck down**: poverty alone cannot be the basis of backwardness *(the 103rd Amendment in 2019 introduced EWS reservation by constitutional amendment)* |
| **Art. 16(4) as a facet of equality** | Reaffirmed *N.M. Thomas* |
| **How the 50% rule is applied** | It is applied to the appointments made in a **particular year**; carrying forward unfilled reserved vacancies is permissible only so long as the limit is not breached in any year (the **81st Amendment**, Art. 16(4B), later took carried-forward vacancies out of the ceiling) |
| **Standing mechanism** | Direction for **permanent statutory bodies** to examine claims of inclusion/exclusion (National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993) |

### 2.4 Promotion, consequential seniority and data

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **R.K. Sabharwal v. State of Punjab** | (1995) 2 SCC 745 | Post-based roster valid; reservation is applied to **posts** (cadre strength), not vacancies |
| **Ajit Singh (II) v. State of Punjab** | (1999) 7 SCC 209 | "Catch-up" rule: promotion by reservation does not give seniority over earlier general candidates |
| **M. Nagaraj v. Union of India** | (2006) 8 SCC 212 | Upheld the 77th, 81st, 82nd and 85th Amendments; to provide reservation in promotion the State must show **quantifiable data** on (i) backwardness, (ii) inadequacy of representation, and (iii) overall efficiency (Art. 335) |
| **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta** | (2018) 10 SCC 396 | Data on **backwardness** of SC/ST need not be shown, but **inadequacy of representation** data is required; **creamy layer** principle applies to SC/ST in promotions |
| **B.K. Pavitra v. Union of India (II)** | (2019) 16 SCC 129 | Karnataka's consequential-seniority law upheld on the basis of data collected |
| **Mukesh Kumar v. State of Uttarakhand** | (2020) 3 SCC 1 | **No fundamental right** to reservation in promotion; Art. 16(4) and (4A) are enabling; the State is not bound to provide it; courts cannot direct data collection |
| **Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil v. Chief Minister, Maharashtra** | (2021) 8 SCC 1 | **Maratha** reservation (SEBC Act 2018) held unconstitutional; **50% ceiling** upheld (no "extraordinary circumstances"); *Indra Sawhney* not referred to a larger bench; interpretation of the 102nd Amendment (Art. 342A): only the President/Parliament can identify SEBCs *(the 105th Amendment, 2021, restored the States' power to maintain their own OBC lists)* |
| **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh** | 2024 INSC 562 (1 Aug 2024; seven judges, 6:1; Trivedi J. dissenting) | **Sub-classification within SCs** (and STs) is permissible if based on quantifiable data and the principle of inadequate representation and backwardness, to ensure more backward groups benefit; **overruled *E.V. Chinnaiah v. State of A.P.*** (2005) 1 SCC 394. Some judges, in separate opinions, said the creamy-layer principle should also be applied to SC/ST; this is not the Court's holding as a whole *(verify)* |

### 2.5 Residence requirements (Art. 16(3))
- ***A.V.S. Narasimha Rao v. State of A.P.*** (1969) 1 SCC 839: a State cannot prescribe residence requirements; only Parliament can (Art. 16(3)).
- ***Dr. Pradeep Jain v. Union of India*** (1984) 3 SCC 654: domicile reservations in medical admissions limited; "the whole country is one" in higher education.

## 3. Article 17: abolition of untouchability

### 3.1 Text
> **Art. 17.** *"Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of "Untouchability" shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.*

- **Enforceable against private persons** as well as the State.
- **"Untouchability"** is not defined in the Constitution; it refers to the **social practice** of treating certain castes as unclean (the historic Dalit exclusion), not to a literal ban on touching (e.g., quarantine).
- **Legislation:** the **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955**, renamed the **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** (1976); the **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** (amended 2015 and 2018).

### 3.2 Cases
- ***State of Karnataka v. Appa Balu Ingale*** 1993 Supp (4) SCC 469: Art. 17 and the 1955 Act provide a strong remedy; special sentencing for untouchability offences; *verify citation*.
- ***Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v. State of Maharashtra*** (2018) 6 SCC 454: the Court (20 Mar 2018) issued guidelines against **automatic arrest** under the SC/ST (PoA) Act and required preliminary inquiry; Parliament responded by the **2018 Amendment** (s. 18A) restoring the position.
- ***Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India*** (2020) 4 SCC 727: the 2018 Amendment upheld; anticipatory bail barred except where no prima facie case.

## 4. Article 18: abolition of titles

- **18(1):** The State shall not confer any **title** (other than a military or academic distinction).
- **18(2):** No citizen of India shall accept any title from a **foreign State**.
- **18(3):** A **non-citizen** holding an office of profit or trust under the State shall not accept any title from a foreign State without the **President's consent**.
- **18(4):** No person holding an office of profit or trust under the State shall accept any **present, emolument, or office** from a foreign State without the President's consent.
- ***Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India*** (1996) 1 SCC 361 (five judges): **Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri** are **not "titles"**; they are **awards** and cannot be used as prefixes or suffixes to a name; if so used, the awardee may be liable to forfeit the award.
- **Purpose:** to end hereditary and colonial privileges (e.g., "Sir", "Rai Bahadur") and keep a republic of equals.

## 6. Criticism and balance

1. **Merit v. social justice:** the Court insists on **efficiency** (Art. 335) and data-based reservations.
2. **Caste as the proxy for backwardness** (*Indra Sawhney*) is both necessary and contested; the creamy-layer doctrine seeks to prevent monopolisation of benefits.
3. **50% ceiling** is a judge-made rule, strained by State laws (e.g., Tamil Nadu 69% via the Ninth Schedule) and the EWS quota.
4. **Reservation in promotion** has produced a long line of conflicting judgments.
5. **EWS reservation** is criticised for excluding the poor among SC/ST/OBC; upheld 3:2.
6. **Art. 17** is robust in text but implementation (caste atrocities) remains weak.
7. **Defence:** reservation is a remedy for historic exclusion; Art. 15(4), 16(4) are enabling provisions to achieve **substantive equality**.

## 8. Quick-revision box

- **Art. 15:** (1) general bar (citizens; grounds *only*); (2) private access; (3) women & children; (4) SEBC/SC/ST (1st Amend.); (5) educational institutions (93rd); (6) EWS (103rd).
- **Art. 16:** (1) opportunity; (2) no discrimination; (3) residence (Parliament); (4) backward classes; (4A) promotion (77th); (4B) carry-forward (81st); (5) religious institutions; (6) EWS (103rd).
