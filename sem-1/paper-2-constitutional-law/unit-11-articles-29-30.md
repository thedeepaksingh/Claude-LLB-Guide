## 1. Text and background

> **Art. 29.** *(1) Any section of the citizens residing in the territory of India or any part thereof having a **distinct language, script or culture** of its own shall have the right to **conserve** the same.*
> *(2) No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution **maintained by the State or receiving aid out of State funds** on grounds **only of religion, race, caste, language or any of them**.*
> **Art. 30.** *(1) All **minorities**, whether based on **religion or language**, shall have the right to **establish and administer educational institutions of their choice**.*
> *(1A) In making any law providing for the **compulsory acquisition of any property** of an educational institution established and administered by a minority, referred to in clause (1), the State shall ensure that the **amount** fixed by or determined under such law for the acquisition of such property is such as would **not restrict or abrogate the right** guaranteed under that clause.* *(Inserted by the 44th Amendment, 1978.)*
> *(2) The State shall not, in granting aid to educational institutions, **discriminate** against any educational institution on the ground that it is under the management of a minority, whether based on religion or language.*

- **Placement:** Part III under the heading *Cultural and Educational Rights* (Arts. 29–30); the Constituent Assembly sought to give the minorities assurance after Partition (the Minorities Committee under Sardar Patel).
- **Related provisions (not in the syllabus):** Art. 347 (official recognition of a language), Art. 350A (facilities for instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage), Art. 350B (Special Officer for linguistic minorities), Art. 15(5).
- **Effect of the 44th Amendment (1978):** Art. 31 was repealed; clause (1A) was added to Art. 30 to protect against compulsory acquisition at inadequate compensation.

## 2. Article 29

### 2.1 Article 29(1): right to conserve language, script or culture
- **Holder:** any **section of citizens** (so, **not limited to minorities**: a majority section of a State may also have a distinct language, script or culture in another State). Only **citizens**.
- **Content:** the right to **conserve** (preserve and develop) a distinct language, script or culture; it includes the right to **agitate** for the protection of language by lawful means.
- ***Jagdev Singh Sidhanti v. Pratap Singh Daulta*** AIR 1965 SC 183: a political election promise to protect the Punjabi language in Gurmukhi script was not a corrupt practice; the right includes the right to **agitate** for conservation.
- ***D.A.V. College, Bhatinda v. State of Punjab*** (1971) 2 SCC 269: the Court defined the right to conserve and held that provisions of the Guru Nanak University Act making Punjabi in Gurmukhi script the sole medium of instruction for affiliated colleges violated Arts. 29(1) and 30(1); **the State is the unit** for determining a linguistic minority.
- ***State of Karnataka v. Associated Management of (Govt. Recognised, Unaided, English Medium) Primary and Secondary Schools*** (2014) 9 SCC 485 (Constitution Bench): the State cannot **impose** the mother tongue as the medium of instruction at the primary level; the choice of medium belongs to the **child/parents**, protected by Arts. 19(1)(a), 29(1), 30(1) *(verify citation)*.

### 2.2 Article 29(2): no denial of admission on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language
- **Holder:** **citizens** (individual right, not limited to minorities).
- **Applies to** institutions **maintained by the State** or **receiving aid** from the State.
- **"Only"**: admission can be denied on other grounds (merit, age, qualifications).
- ***State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan*** AIR 1951 SC 226: the **Communal G.O.** (reserving seats in medical and engineering colleges by caste and religion) violated Art. 29(2) → the **First Amendment (1951)** inserted **Art. 15(4)** to allow special provision for socially and educationally backward classes.
- ***State of Bombay v. Bombay Education Society*** AIR 1954 SC 561: a Government order barring children whose mother tongue was not English from English-medium schools violated Art. 29(2) (denial on the ground of **language**) even for a minority-run Anglo-Indian school.
- **Interplay with reservations:** Art. 15(4), 15(5) (93rd Amendment, 2005, allowing reservations in private unaided institutions **except minority institutions**), and Art. 15(6) (EWS, 103rd Amendment) are exceptions in support of backward classes.

## 3. Article 30: minority educational institutions

### 3.1 Who is a minority?
- The Constitution **does not define** "minority".
- ***In re Kerala Education Bill, 1957*** AIR 1958 SC 956: a minority is a **community numerically less than 50%** of the population; the unit was left open.
- ***D.A.V. College*** (1971): for linguistic minorities the **State** is the unit.
- ***T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka*** (2002) 8 SCC 481 (eleven judges): **the State is the unit** for determining both **linguistic and religious** minorities (since State Legislatures legislate on education and reorganisation of States is linguistic).
- ***Bal Patil v. Union of India*** (2005) 6 SCC 690: the Jains were not held to be a national minority at the time; the Court left the matter to the Government. Under the **National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992**, the Central Government has notified **Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Zoroastrians (Parsis)** (1993) and the **Jains** (27 Jan 2014) as minority communities *(verify)*.
- **National Commission for Minority Educational Institutions Act, 2004** (NCMEI): the Commission decides disputes and grants minority-status certificates.

### 3.2 "Establish and administer of their choice"
- **Establish** and **administer** go together (*Azeez Basha* held that an institution cannot claim to be "administered" by the minority if it was not "established" by it; this view was **overruled in 2024**, see §5).
- **Of their choice:** not only for the benefit of the minority: the institution may offer general (secular) education.
- **Not limited to religious education**: includes general education, professional and technical education.

### 3.3 Is the right absolute? Regulation v. maladministration

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| ***In re Kerala Education Bill, 1957*** | AIR 1958 SC 956 | Art. 30(1) is not absolute; **regulatory measures** for educational standards are allowed; conditions that destroy minority character are invalid; aided minority institutions must be allowed **to admit non-minority students** within limits |
| ***Rev. Sidhajbhai Sabhai v. State of Bombay*** | AIR 1963 SC 540 | The State cannot, under the guise of regulation, **destroy the minority character**; a regulation that reduced management to a mere cipher was invalid |
| ***Ahmedabad St. Xavier's College Society v. State of Gujarat*** | (1974) 1 SCC 717 (nine judges) | The right to **administer** is not the right to **maladminister**; regulation of affiliation conditions, standards and discipline allowed; a law compelling the college to refer disciplinary disputes with staff to a University Tribunal violated Art. 30(1); the right of administration includes **choosing the staff** (subject to qualifications), **disciplinary control** and **admission of students** (subject to regulations) |
| ***St. Stephen's College v. University of Delhi*** | (1992) 1 SCC 558 | A minority aided college may **reserve a percentage of seats** (up to 50%) for its community; but must admit others on **merit**; the University may regulate, but not impose a rigid scheme overriding the minority's right *(verify the percentage)* |
| ***T.M.A. Pai Foundation*** | (2002) 8 SCC 481 (eleven judges) | (i) Education is an **"occupation"** (Art. 19(1)(g)); (ii) **Unaided** institutions have greater autonomy in admissions and fee structure, but **no capitation fee** and no profiteering; (iii) **State** is the unit for minority status; (iv) **Aided** institutions may be subject to more regulation (qualifications and service conditions of teachers, merit); (v) a minority institution **may admit non-minority students** but must remain a minority institution; (vi) regulation to ensure educational excellence is allowed |
| ***Islamic Academy of Education v. State of Karnataka*** | (2003) 6 SCC 697 | Implementation of *T.M.A. Pai*: committees to supervise fees and admission in unaided professional colleges |
| ***P.A. Inamdar v. State of Maharashtra*** | (2005) 6 SCC 537 (seven judges) | The State **cannot impose reservation** on unaided private (including minority) professional institutions; admission through a fair and transparent process; fees determined by the institution subject to no profiteering |
| ***Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India*** | (2014) 8 SCC 1 (Constitution Bench) | The RTE Act, 2009 (including the 25% quota) and Art. 15(5) **do not apply to minority institutions**, aided or unaided |
| ***Anjuman Ishaat-e-Taleem Trust v. State of Maharashtra*** | order of **1 Sept 2025** *(verify)* | As reported, a two-judge bench questioned the **blanket exclusion** of minority institutions from the RTE Act in *Pramati* and **referred** the question to a larger bench; pending |

### 3.4 Article 30(2): aid without discrimination
- Once the State decides to aid educational institutions, it **cannot refuse aid** because the institution is run by a minority (religion or language).
- **Conditions:** the State may impose reasonable regulations on aided institutions (qualifications of teachers, accounts, merit-based admission of non-minority students), but not a condition that takes away the minority's character (*Kerala Education Bill*; *St. Xavier's*).
- **Conditions of aid cannot compel** the institution to give up Art. 30(1) rights (*Frank Anthony Public School Employees' Association v. Union of India*, (1986) 4 SCC 707: service conditions of teachers; verify).

### 3.5 Compulsory acquisition: Art. 30(1A)
Inserted by the **44th Amendment (1978)** to overcome the effect of the repeal of the right to property; compensation must not "restrict or abrogate" the right. *(Shortly: if the State acquires property of a minority institution, the amount must be adequate to enable it to continue.)*

## 4. Comparison: Art. 29 and Art. 30

| Basis | Art. 29 | Art. 30 |
|---|---|---|
| Holder | **Any section of citizens** (majority or minority) | **Minorities** (religious or linguistic); the text says "all minorities", not "citizens" |
| Content | Right to **conserve** language, script, culture (29(1)); no denial of admission on religion, race, caste, language (29(2)) | Right to **establish and administer** educational institutions of choice |
| Subject matter | Culture and admission to State-run/aided institutions | Educational institutions |
| Scope | Wider holder, narrower in content | Narrower holder, wider in content (administration) |
| Nature of 29(2) | Individual citizens' right | N/A |
| Limits | Reasonable regulation; "only" in 29(2) | Regulation (not maladministration); no destruction of minority character |
| Key cases | *Jagdev Singh Sidhanti*, *D.A.V. College*, *Champakam Dorairajan*, *Bombay Education Society* | *Kerala Education Bill*, *St. Xavier's*, *St. Stephen's*, *T.M.A. Pai*, *Inamdar*, *Pramati*, *AMU* |

## 5. Case study: Aligarh Muslim University and the minority status question

- ***S. Azeez Basha v. Union of India*** AIR 1968 SC 662 (20 Oct 1967; five judges): AMU was **established by an Act of Parliament** (AMU Act, 1920), not by the Muslim community, so it could not claim minority status under Art. 30(1). Parliament then amended the AMU Act in **1981** (inserting words that the University was established by the Muslim community of India), but the Allahabad High Court struck down the 1981 amendment (January 2006), and the matter reached the Supreme Court.
- ***Aligarh Muslim University v. Naresh Agarwal*** (reference decided on **8 November 2024**; seven judges; **4:3** majority): the Court **overruled *Azeez Basha*** insofar as it held that an institution incorporated by statute cannot be a minority institution. The majority (CJI Chandrachud writing for himself and three colleagues) held that **statutory incorporation does not by itself rule out** minority status, and that "**established**" in Art. 30(1) is not to be read narrowly; the Court must look to the **origin of the institution (the idea, funding, land, approvals), its purpose and its administration** to see if it is a minority institution (the **"indicia" test**). The question whether **AMU is in fact a minority institution** was **sent to a regular bench** to apply the test. Three judges (Surya Kant, Datta and Sharma JJ.) dissented in separate opinions.
- **Present position:** proceedings on the minority-status question before the regular bench *(verify the stage)*.

## 6. Criticism and balance

1. **No definition of "minority"**; the State-as-unit test leads to anomalies (a community that is a majority nationally may be a minority in a State, and vice versa).
2. **Minority institutions and social justice:** exemption from reservation (Art. 15(5)) and the RTE Act (*Pramati*) is criticised for leaving students in minority institutions without those safeguards; the larger-bench reference is reconsidering the RTE exclusion.
3. **Maladministration:** the line between regulation and interference is case-specific.
4. **Misuse:** claims of minority status to avoid regulation (fee, admission) are litigated frequently.
5. **Strengths:** protects pluralism, ensures that minority communities can preserve their culture through education; the *T.M.A. Pai* balance promotes autonomy with accountability.

---

## 7. Current relevance and recent developments

- **AMU (Nov 2024):** the "indicia" test replaces the strict *Azeez Basha* rule; final application pending.
- **Pramati reconsideration (Sept 2025 reference):** whether minority institutions can be exempt from the RTE Act is pending before a larger bench.
- **NEP 2020 and language policy:** the three-language formula and medium of instruction continue to engage Arts. 29(1), 30(1) and 350A.
- **Uttar Pradesh Board of Madarsa Education Act, 2004:** the Supreme Court in *Anjum Kadari v. Union of India* (5 Nov 2024) upheld the Act's validity (setting aside the Allahabad High Court's decision), holding that it is regulatory and consistent with Art. 30, while the power to confer degrees (Kamil, Fazil) was held to conflict with the UGC Act *(verify citation and holding)*.
- **Caution for the exam:** do not say Art. 30 gives minorities an unregulated right. It is subject to reasonable regulation (standards, merit for non-minority admissions, no maladministration).

## 8. Quick-revision box

- **Art. 29(1):** any **section of citizens**; conserve language, script, culture. **Art. 29(2):** no denial of admission to State-maintained or aided institutions on religion, race, caste, language **only**.
- **Art. 30(1):** **minorities** (religion or language) can **establish and administer** institutions of their choice; **30(1A)** (44th Amendment 1978): compulsory acquisition; **30(2)**: no discrimination in aid.
- **State = unit** for minority: *T.M.A. Pai* (2002, 11 judges); *D.A.V. College* (1971).
- **Regulation but not maladministration:** *Kerala Education Bill* (1958); *Sidhajbhai* (1963); *St. Xavier's* (1974); *St. Stephen's* (1992).
- **Unaided:** autonomy; no capitation; no reservation (*Inamdar*, 2005).
- **RTE exemption:** *Pramati* (2014) → larger-bench reference (2025).
- **AMU:** *Azeez Basha* (1967) overruled by 4:3 on **8 Nov 2024**; indicia test; sent to regular bench.
- **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)** → First Amendment → Art. 15(4).

**Examiner keywords:** minority, State as unit, conserve, establish and administer, regulation, maladministration, minority character, indicia of establishment, capitation fee, autonomy, aided and unaided.
