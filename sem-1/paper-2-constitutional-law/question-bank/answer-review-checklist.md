# Paper II · Answer-Review Checklist (use after every mock and every practice answer)

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-28 (IST) · **Law stated as on:** 2026-10-28 · **Branch:** `paper/02-constitutional-law`
> **What this is.** A checklist for marking your own answers against the [model answers](README.md) and for finding what to fix before the exam. **The mark splits are mine.** The university publishes no marking scheme, and I could not find one (see the [gaps document](../../../docs/01-gaps-and-open-questions.md)). Treat them as a guide to what a careful examiner would look for.

---

## 1. Before you review: set the answer beside the question

1. Did you **answer the question that was asked**? Compare your first paragraph with the verb in the question (*explain, discuss, distinguish, critically examine, comment, write a note*).
2. Did you keep to the **exam limit**? Section A ≤ 75 words, Section B ≤ 200 words, Section C: no limit printed ("answer is required in detail"), but 900–1,200 words fits a 30-minute answer.
3. Did you write the **right number of answers**? A: all five. B: two of three. C: three of five. An extra answer in B or C does not earn extra marks.

## 2. Section A (4 marks) — tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Definition, text of the Article or the meaning in the first sentence | 1 |
| Two or three main points (not a list of ten) | 2 |
| One case or one statute with the year | 1 |
| ≤ 75 words | required |

**Typical losses:** starting with a long introduction; no case; a case with the wrong year; writing 120 words and running out of time.

## 3. Section B (10 marks) — tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |

## 4. Section C (20 marks) — tick each box

| Check | Marks (mine) |
|---|---|
| Introduction: meaning, constitutional text, background | 3 |
| Five or six headings in a logical order | 5 |
| At least **five** decided cases, each with year and holding | 6 |
| Criticism or a balanced view | 3 |
| Recent development (a case, an Amendment or a statute) | 1 |
| Conclusion | 2 |

**Typical losses:** a good theory with no cases; cases listed without holdings; an answer that stops after the first half of a two-part question (for example, "against whom FRs are enforceable *and* what remedies"); a missing short note (b).

## 5. Accuracy check: the mistakes I found in my own drafts

Check that you have **not** made these mistakes. They were all found and corrected in this project.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *Shirur Mutt* held the s. 76 levy to be a **fee** | It held it a **tax**, void because the State could not impose it; Art. 27 was not breached |
| *Joseph Shine* is "(2018) 2 SCC 189" | The citation is (2019) 3 SCC 39 (decided 27 September 2018) |
| The Preamble is not part of the Constitution | That was *Berubari* (1960). *Kesavananda* (1973) held that it is part |
| *Gian Kaur* upheld the right to die | It held that the right to life **does not include** the right to die; *Common Cause* (2018) allows passive euthanasia and living wills |
| *Santa Bai* was "overruled" | It is a High Court decision that the Supreme Court rejected (*Rajasthan SEB*) |
| *Indra Sawhney* permitted reservation in promotions | It did not; the 77th Amendment (1995) added Art. 16(4A) |
| The 50% ceiling is fixed | It is a rule with an "extraordinary situations" exception; EWS (2022) and State laws go beyond it |
| Art. 15(5) covers minority institutions | It does not (*Pramati*, 2014) |
| Art. 21 protects life by "procedure established by law" only | After *Maneka Gandhi* (1978) the procedure must be fair, just and reasonable |
| Fundamental Duties are enforceable | They are not justiciable; Parliament may legislate to enforce them |
| Sedition survives as s. 124A | The BNS (2023) has no s. 124A; see s. 152, and check any challenge |
| Art. 30 gives minorities an absolute right | It is subject to regulation that does not destroy minority character (*St. Xavier's*, 1974) |

## 8. The 24-hour plan before the paper

1. Read the [leading-cases digest](leading-cases-digest.md) index and the one-line holdings in [`../case-and-quote-bank.md`](../case-and-quote-bank.md).
2. Re-read Section A answers for Units 1–5 (they are the compulsory questions. In Dec 2022–2025 federalism came up in every year, Fundamental Duties in three, and the Preamble and suspension of FRs in two each; ex post facto laws came in Dec 2023 (B) and Dec 2025 (A)).
3. Rehearse **PIL, Directive Principles v Fundamental Rights, Art. 21 and speech/press** as 20-mark answers; each has appeared in at least three of the last four years.
4. Do the currency check in section 6.
5. Sleep.
