**LL.B. (Semester I) · Constitutional Law of India – I**  
**Time: 3 hours · Maximum marks: 100**

*Note: Answer all the questions in Section A. In Section B answer any two of the three questions. In Section C answer any three of the five questions.*

### Section A (very short answer) · 5 × 4 = 20 marks
*Attempt all questions. Each answer must not exceed 75 words.*

1. Is the Preamble a part of the Constitution? Explain briefly.  *(4)*
2. What is meant by cooperative federalism?  *(4)*
3. Explain the doctrine of severability.  *(4)*
4. What is the 'creamy layer'?  *(4)*
5. Write a short note on the writ of habeas corpus.  *(4)*

### Section B (short answer) · 2 × 10 = 20 marks
*Attempt any two questions. Each answer must not exceed 200 words.*

6. Distinguish between Article 25 and Article 26 of the Constitution.  *(10)*
7. Discuss the right to privacy as a fundamental right. Refer to decided cases.  *(10)*
8. Discuss the guidelines laid down in *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India* on reservation.  *(10)*

### Section C (long answer) · 3 × 20 = 60 marks
*Attempt any three questions. The answer is required in detail.*

9. Parliament may amend Fundamental Rights but cannot destroy the basic structure of the Constitution. Discuss with decided cases.  *(20)*
10. What is public interest litigation? Discuss its development, its value and its abuse.  *(20)*
11. Discuss, with the help of decided cases, the contribution of the judiciary to environment protection through Article 21.  *(20)*
12. Freedom of the press is not expressly mentioned in Article 19(1)(a). Discuss its scope and the restrictions on it.  *(20)*
13. Write short notes on: (a) Article 23 (b) Article 24.  *(20)*

## Time plan (180 minutes)

| Part | Time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Read the paper and choose | 10 min | Count the cases you can name for each question before you choose. |
| Section A (5 answers) | 25 min | About 5 minutes each: definition or text, two points, one case. |
| Section B (2 answers) | 35 min | About 17 minutes each: 150–200 words, with 2–3 cases. |
| Section C (3 answers) | 95 min | About 30 minutes each: introduction, five or six headings, cases, a short conclusion. |
| Final check | 15 min | Question numbers, case names, word limits in A and B. |

## Choice strategy
- **Section B and C:** choose the questions where you can name at least three decided cases with years. A question you half-know costs more marks than one you know well.
- **Do not exceed the word limits in A and B.** An answer far above the limit wastes time; a good A answer is 60–75 words.
- **Section C is on a compulsory-detail basis:** write the headings in the order the question asks. Include a recent development if you know one (see the digest).
