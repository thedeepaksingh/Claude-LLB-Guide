## 5. How to use the bank

1. **Read the note** for the unit in [`../`](../) once.
2. **Write Section A answers from memory** in 75 words; then compare with the model answer and count the words.
3. **Write Section B answers** in 200 words, in 10 minutes each.
4. **For Section C**, read the "Model rubric (mine)" at the top of the answer, write your own, then read the model. The models run 900–1,400 words; aim for 700–1,000 handwritten words (my estimate) with the same headings and key cases.
5. **Check cases** in the [digest](leading-cases-digest.md) (facts, issues, held, principle, later history).
6. **Sit a mock** ([1](mock-exam-1.md), [2](mock-exam-2.md), [3](mock-exam-3.md)) under time.
7. **Mark your answers** with the [checklist](answer-review-checklist.md).
8. **Before the exam**, check the list of points where the law has moved (end of the checklist).

## 6. Things you should know about this bank

- **No official marking scheme.** None was found. Every rubric is my own and is labelled so.
- **Paper code.** The Paper IV printed codes changed between 2023 and 2024; Paper II's code K-1002 has not. See the verification log.
- **Citations.** The case name, year and court earn the credit. Where I could not confirm an SCC reference against a law report I have marked it **†** in the digest.
- **Currency.** Where a position was changing in 2026 (for example the Sabarimala reference, UCC legislation in States, the DPDP Act rules) the answer says "as on" a date. Verify before the exam.
