## How to use this pack

**Three-pass plan**
1. **Understand (pass 1):** read each unit once in syllabus order (Units 1–3 give the frame; Units 4–12 are the rights; Units 13–14 close the loop).
2. **Memorise (pass 2):** use the *quick-revision boxes* and the one-page sheets below. For every Article, be able to write: *text → who can claim → against whom → restrictions → three leading cases → one recent development*.
3. **Practise (pass 3):** write the Section A, B and C answers from the [question bank](question-bank/README.md) under timed conditions, then compare with the model answer; sit the [mock papers](question-bank/mock-exam-1.md); mark yourself with the [checklist](question-bank/answer-review-checklist.md).

**Answer-writing template for a Section C answer (20 marks, "in detail"; about 900–1,200 words)**

| Step | What to write | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | The Article number, its key words and its place in the scheme (one or two lines) | 2–3 lines |
| 2. Text | The exact words of the Article (at least the key phrases) | 3–5 lines |
| 3. Scope / ambit | What is included; who can claim; against whom | 5–8 lines |
| 4. Restrictions | Express limits (e.g. Art. 19(2)); judicial tests (reasonable classification, proportionality, essential religious practice) | 5–8 lines |
| 5. Case law | At least **4–5 cases** in chronological order, each with a one-line holding; one **recent** case | the bulk |
| 6. Criticism / current position | 3–4 points; newest developments | 4–6 lines |
| 7. Conclusion | A balanced one-liner | 2–3 lines |

**Habits that earn marks:** quote the Article number; give the case name, year and court; show the chain *Gopalan → Maneka*, *Champakam → Minerva Mills*, *Golaknath → Kesavananda*; draw a table or flowchart; end with the present position. **Do not** invent a citation: if unsure, write the case name and year only.
