## 1. Article 23: traffic in human beings and forced labour

### 1.1 Text

> **Art. 23.** *(1) Traffic in human beings and **begar** and other similar forms of **forced labour** are prohibited and any contravention of this provision shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.*
> *(2) Nothing in this article shall prevent the State from imposing **compulsory service for public purposes**, and in imposing such service the State shall not make any discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste or class or any of them.*

### 1.2 Background
- **Historical setting:** slavery, serfdom, *begar* (labour without payment, extracted by landlords or the State), *devadasi* dedication, and the *zamindari* practices of forced service. The framers prohibited them at the constitutional level to give practical meaning to human dignity.
- **Art. 35(a)(ii):** only **Parliament** (not State Legislatures) may make laws punishing acts declared offences under Art. 23, ensuring uniformity.
- **Persons protected:** any person (citizen or non-citizen).
- **Against whom:** the **State and private individuals** (a rare Fundamental Right with direct horizontal effect): *People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India* (1982).

### 1.3 Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Traffic in human beings** | Buying and selling of human beings like goods; includes **trafficking for prostitution, labour or other exploitation**, and the sale of women and children. The Court has treated **immoral traffic** as covered (see *Vishal Jeet v. Union of India*, (1990) 3 SCC 318, on devadasi practice and child prostitution) |
| **Begar** | Labour or service **without payment** that a person is compelled to perform (Bhagwati J. in *PUDR*) |
| **Other similar forms of forced labour** | Any labour obtained by **force**, including **economic compulsion**; includes **payment below the minimum wage**, since a worker who accepts less because of poverty is compelled |
| **Compulsory service for public purposes** (Art. 23(2)) | Permissible, e.g., **conscription, national service, jury duty** or flood relief, but **without discrimination** on religion, race, caste or class only (**Art. 23(2)**). Note: the clause does not mention "sex" or "place of birth" |

### 1.4 Leading cases

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India** (Asiad Workers case) | (1982) 3 SCC 235 (Bhagwati J.) | Art. 23 is **enforceable against private individuals**; "**force**" is not limited to physical or legal force; it includes **economic circumstances**; payment of less than the **minimum wage** is forced labour; the State has a duty to ensure that labour laws (Minimum Wages Act, Equal Remuneration Act, Contract Labour Act) are enforced; the Court appointed Commissioners to inspect conditions |
| **Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan** | (1983) 1 SCC 525 | Workers on **famine relief work** paid below minimum wages: the State cannot take advantage of the helplessness of the poor; the Rajasthan Famine Relief Works Employees (Exemption from Labour Laws) Act, 1964 was held invalid to the extent it denied minimum wages |
| **Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India** | (1984) 3 SCC 161 | Bonded labour in stone quarries (Faridabad); the State's duty to identify, release and rehabilitate; the right to live with dignity is part of Art. 21; a public interest litigation (PIL) example (letter to the Court treated as writ) |
| **Deena v. Union of India** | (1983) 4 SCC 645 | **Prison labour without wages** is forced labour; prisoners must be paid **reasonable wages** for work |
| **State of Gujarat v. Hon'ble High Court of Gujarat** | (1998) 7 SCC 392 | Rigorous imprisonment may involve labour as part of punishment, so that labour as such is not forced labour under Art. 23, but prisoners should be paid fair wages *(verify holding in text)* |
| **Neerja Chaudhary v. State of M.P.** | (1984) 3 SCC 243 | After identification and release, **rehabilitation** of bonded labourers is a constitutional obligation |
| **Vishal Jeet v. Union of India** | (1990) 3 SCC 318 | Directions on **devadasi** practice and child prostitution; the State must rescue and rehabilitate |
| **Gaurav Jain v. Union of India** | (1997) 8 SCC 114 | Rehabilitation and education of **children of sex workers**; the Court discussed the duty to prevent trafficking and sexual exploitation |
| **Bachpan Bachao Andolan v. Union of India** | (2011) 5 SCC 1 | Trafficking of children for circus and entertainment; strict guidelines for prevention and rehabilitation |

### 1.5 Statutes implementing Art. 23

| Subject | Statute |
|---|---|
| **Bonded labour** | **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976**: abolishes the system, extinguishes debts, makes it an offence; Vigilance Committees at district and sub-divisional level; rehabilitation under the **Central Sector Scheme for Rehabilitation of Bonded Labourers** (revised 2016; amounts to be verified) |
| **Trafficking** | **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956** (ITPA); **BNS 2023** ss. 143–144 (trafficking and exploitation of a trafficked person; earlier IPC s. 370 and s. 370A, inserted by the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013) *(verify section numbers)* |
| **Wages** | Earlier: Minimum Wages Act, 1948; Equal Remuneration Act, 1976; Payment of Wages Act. Now consolidated in the **Code on Wages, 2019** (in force; the four Labour Codes took effect on **21 Nov 2025**, as reported) |
| **Contract labour; migrant workers** | Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970; Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act, 1979 (now in the **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020**) |
| **Children** | Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015; POCSO Act, 2012 |
| **Devadasi** | State laws (e.g., Karnataka Devadasis (Prohibition of Dedication) Act, 1982) |

### 1.6 Exceptions and limits
- **Compulsory service** for public purposes (Art. 23(2)): conscription, civil defence, national service, jury service. The State must not discriminate **only** on grounds of religion, race, caste or class.
- **Prison labour** as part of rigorous imprisonment is not forced labour (but wages should be fair).
- **Not covered:** voluntary work, apprenticeship under valid contracts, or labour imposed by lawful court orders.

### 1.7 Criticism
1. **Enforcement gap:** bonded labour persists (agricultural, brick kilns, construction, domestic work); conviction rates are low.
2. **Rehabilitation:** delay in release certificates and payments.
3. **Informal economy:** minimum wage non-payment is widespread; the Labour Codes' compliance and coverage are debated.
4. **Trafficking:** no comprehensive law; the Trafficking of Persons (Prevention, Protection and Rehabilitation) Bill, 2018 lapsed.
5. **Strengths:** the breadth of *PUDR*; horizontal effect; use of PIL.

## 2. Article 24: prohibition of employment of children in factories, etc.

### 2.1 Text

> **Art. 24.** *No child below the age of **fourteen years** shall be employed to work in any **factory or mine** or engaged in any other **hazardous employment**.*

### 2.2 Features
- **Narrow on its face:** it prohibits child employment only in **hazardous** settings; it does not prohibit all child work.
- **Absolute prohibition for hazardous employment:** no exception, even with parental consent.
- **Available against the State and private employers.**
- **Linked provisions:** Art. 39(e)–(f) (DPSP: children not forced by economic necessity into unsuited work; opportunities for healthy development); Art. 21A (education for 6–14); Art. 45 (early childhood care); Art. 51A(k).

### 2.3 Cases

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **People's Union for Democratic Rights** (Asiad) | (1982) 3 SCC 235 | **Construction work** is hazardous employment; a child below 14 cannot be employed in it; the Employment of Children Act, 1938 was held not to cover construction, but the constitutional prohibition applies directly |
| **Salal Hydro Project v. State of J&K** | (1983) 2 SCC 181 | Children in hydro-power construction: directions for the welfare of child workers; the Court repeated that Art. 24 is self-executing in hazardous employment |
| **M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu** | (1996) 6 SCC 756 | **Sivakasi match and fireworks** factories: children cannot be employed in hazardous processes; non-hazardous work only after education; directions: **offending employers to pay compensation of Rs. 20,000 per child** to a Child Labour Rehabilitation-cum-Welfare Fund; alternative employment or Rs. 5,000 contribution by the State; education for all children; survey of child labour |
| **Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India** | (1997) 10 SCC 549 | Further directions on child labour (carpet industry in Uttar Pradesh): working hours limited, education of working children, enforcement and monitoring *(verify citation)* |
| **Bachpan Bachao Andolan v. Union of India** | (2011) 5 SCC 1 | Child trafficking for the circus; compensation, rescue and rehabilitation protocols |

### 2.4 Statutory framework

| Statute | Main content |
|---|---|
| **Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986** (CLPRA) | Prohibited employment of children (under 14) in listed hazardous occupations and processes; regulated other work |
| **Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016** (in force **1 Sept 2016**; it renamed the principal Act the *Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986*) | **Total prohibition on employing children below 14 in any occupation** except (i) helping the **family or family enterprise** after school hours and during vacations, in non-hazardous work, and (ii) as a **child artist** in audio-visual entertainment (not circus) with safeguards; **adolescents (14–18) prohibited from hazardous occupations and processes**; hazardous list narrowed to mines, inflammable substances or explosives, and hazardous processes under the Factories Act; **higher penalties** (imprisonment of six months to two years and/or fine of Rs. 20,000–50,000 for employing a child; higher for repeat offenders); offences reported to be made **cognizable** *(verify)* |
| **Factories Act, 1948** (s. 67) | No child below 14 in a factory; the 'young person' provisions |
| **Mines Act, 1952** | No person under 18 in a mine (with limited exceptions) |
| **Right to Education Act, 2009** | Compulsory schooling for 6–14, which supports the policy of eliminating child labour |
| **Juvenile Justice Act, 2015** | Penalises exploitation of a child employee (s. 79) and protects children in need of care |
| **ILO Conventions** | India ratified Convention 138 (minimum age) and Convention 182 (worst forms of child labour) in **June 2017** *(verify)* |
| **Labour Codes (2019–2020)** | The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 replaces the Factories Act and the Mines Act, among others; confirm how child and adolescent employment is treated in the final text and rules *(verify)* |

### 2.5 Criticism
1. **Narrow text:** it does not prohibit non-hazardous child labour; the 2016 Act's family-enterprise exception is criticised for allowing child work in agriculture, home-based industry and small enterprises, hindering schooling.
2. **Implementation:** child labour persists in agriculture, domestic work, brick kilns and the informal sector; inspections and prosecutions are weak.
3. **Gap between 14 and 18:** adolescents above 14 are protected only from hazardous work.
4. **Strengths:** a comprehensive ban (2016), higher penalties, convergence with RTE and the *M.C. Mehta* fund and rehabilitation directions.

## 3. Comparison of Arts. 23 and 24

| Basis | Art. 23 | Art. 24 |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Traffic in human beings, begar, forced labour | Employment of children in hazardous settings |
| Who is protected | Any person | Children below 14 |
| Against whom | State and private individuals | State and private employers |
| Exceptions | Compulsory service for public purposes | None for hazardous work |
| Leading case | *PUDR v. Union of India* (1982) | *M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu* (1996) |
| Key statutes | Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976; ITPA, 1956 | CLPRA 1986 (amended 2016); RTE Act, 2009 |
| DPSP link | Art. 39(e), 39(f), 41, 43 | Art. 39(e)–(f), 45 |

## 4. Current relevance and recent developments

- **Labour Codes (in force 21 Nov 2025, as reported):** the Code on Wages, 2019 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 replace many older statutes that implemented Arts. 23–24. Check whether the conditions of child and adolescent work are preserved in the final text and rules.
- **Trafficking:** BNS ss. 143–144; the absence of a standalone anti-trafficking law is still debated.
- **Bonded labour:** the Government's 2016 rehabilitation scheme, and the target of eradication by 2030 (SDG 8.7).
- **Gig and platform workers:** the Code on Social Security, 2020 introduces recognition of gig and platform workers; Art. 23's reasoning on economic compulsion is cited in the debate on conditions of work *(commentary; no case law settled)*.
- **Caution for the exam:** do not say Art. 24 bans all child labour. Say that Art. 24 bans hazardous child employment; the **2016 Act** goes further statutorily.

## 5. Quick-revision box

- **Art. 23(1):** traffic in human beings, begar and other forced labour prohibited; offence. **Art. 23(2):** compulsory service for public purposes allowed, with no discrimination on religion, race, caste or class.
- **Applies to private persons too** (*PUDR*, 1982). Any person. **Art. 35(a)(ii):** only Parliament legislates.
- **Forced labour includes less than minimum wage** (*PUDR*; *Sanjit Roy*, 1983). Prison labour: *Deena* (1983).
- **Bonded labour:** *Bandhua Mukti Morcha* (1984); Act of 1976.
- **Trafficking:** ITPA 1956; BNS ss. 143–144.
- **Art. 24:** below 14; factory, mine, hazardous employment. *PUDR* (construction hazardous); *M.C. Mehta* (1996, Sivakasi; Rs. 20,000 fund).
- **CLPRA 1986, amended 2016:** total ban below 14 with exceptions; adolescents 14–18 barred from hazardous work.

**Examiner keywords:** begar, forced labour, economic compulsion, bonded labour, minimum wage, trafficking, horizontal application, compulsory service, hazardous employment, rehabilitation.
