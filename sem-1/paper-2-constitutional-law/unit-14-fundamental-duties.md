## 1. Background and origin

- The Constitution as adopted (1950) had **rights but no duties**; Part IV contained duties of the **State** only.
- **Emergency period.** After the Emergency was declared (1975), the Government appointed the **Swaran Singh Committee** (1976) to study amendments of the Constitution. It recommended a chapter on citizens' duties (usually stated as **eight** duties; the Amendment as enacted had **ten**) and, with respect to enforcement, suggested that **Parliament may provide for penalty or punishment** for non-compliance and that no law imposing such a penalty be questioned on ground of Art. 14, 19 or 31. The Government did not adopt the penalty recommendation. *(Verify the number of duties and the penalty recommendation.)*
- **42nd Amendment (1976)** inserted **Part IVA** with **Art. 51A**, containing **ten** duties.
- **86th Amendment (2002)** added **clause (k)** (duty of parents or guardians to provide educational opportunities to a child aged 6–14), making **eleven**.
- **Sources of inspiration:** the Constitution of the **USSR (1977)** (which paired rights with duties), the Japanese Constitution, and Art. 29 of the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948** ("Everyone has duties to the community"). India's debate has also drawn on Gandhi's emphasis that rights flow from the performance of duties.
- **Placement:** Part IVA (Art. 51A), after Part IV.

## 2. Text: the eleven duties

> **Art. 51A.** *It shall be the duty of every **citizen** of India—*

| Clause | Duty |
|---|---|
| **(a)** | to **abide by the Constitution** and respect its ideals and institutions, the **National Flag** and the **National Anthem** |
| **(b)** | to **cherish and follow the noble ideals** which inspired our national struggle for freedom |
| **(c)** | to **uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity** of India |
| **(d)** | to **defend the country** and render **national service** when called upon to do so |
| **(e)** | to promote **harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood** amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic, regional or sectional diversities; to **renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women** |
| **(f)** | to **value and preserve the rich heritage** of our composite culture |
| **(g)** | to **protect and improve the natural environment** including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have **compassion for living creatures** |
| **(h)** | to develop the **scientific temper, humanism** and the spirit of inquiry and reform |
| **(i)** | to **safeguard public property** and to **abjure violence** |
| **(j)** | to **strive towards excellence** in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement |
| **(k)** | *(86th Amendment, 2002)* who is a **parent or guardian**, to provide **opportunities for education** to his child or, as the case may be, ward between the age of **six and fourteen years** |

*Memory aid for the eleven: **Constitution, Struggle, Sovereignty, Defend, Harmony, Heritage, Environment, Science, Public property, Excellence, Kids' education.***

---

## 3. Nature and features

1. **Duties of citizens only** (not of non-citizens; not of the State).
2. **Moral and civic duties**; the text contains **no sanction** and **no enforcement mechanism**.
3. **Non-justiciable:** a writ cannot be sought to enforce them; but they can be **supported by legislation**.
4. **Some are vague or aspirational** ("scientific temper", "excellence"); others are readily capable of legislation (public property, environment, flag, women's dignity).
5. **Not conditions to the enjoyment of rights:** a citizen's failure to perform a duty does not extinguish a Fundamental Right.
6. **Link with Part III and Part IV:** duties are the **counterpart** of rights; they reflect the values underlying Part III (e.g., Art. 51A(g) with Art. 21; 51A(k) with Art. 21A; 51A(e) with Arts. 14–15).

| Basis | Fundamental Rights | Fundamental Duties | DPSP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Addressed to | Individuals (citizens/persons) | **Citizens** | **State** |
| Enforceability | Justiciable | **Non-justiciable** | Non-justiciable |
| Origin | US Bill of Rights | USSR (1977) / UDHR | Irish Constitution |
| Added by | Original Constitution | **42nd Amendment (1976)**; 86th (2002) | Original Constitution |

## 4. How the courts have used Article 51A

*The duties are not directly enforceable, but courts use them (i) to interpret rights and laws, (ii) to uphold a law that gives effect to a duty as a reasonable restriction, and (iii) to issue directions to the State to promote the values.*

| Case | Citation | Use of Art. 51A |
|---|---|---|
| **Rural Litigation and Entitlement Kendra v. State of U.P.** (Doon Valley case) | (1985) 2 SCC 431 *(verify)* | Art. 48A and **Art. 51A(g)** invoked to stop unlawful limestone quarrying; environment is a matter of public duty; the Court acted on a letter |
| **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India** (Ganga pollution I) | (1988) 1 SCC 471 | Art. 51A(g) cited to direct tanneries to set up effluent treatment plants *(verify)* |
| **Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala** | (1986) 3 SCC 615 | Art. 51A(a) (respect for the National Anthem) does not compel a person to **sing**; standing quietly out of conscience is protected by Arts. 19(1)(a) and 25 |
| **A.I.I.M.S. Students' Union v. A.I.I.M.S.** | (2002) 1 SCC 428 *(verify)* | Fundamental duties, though not enforceable by writ, are **equally important** as Fundamental Rights; citizens should perform duties as a matter of conscience; the Court may **take them into account** and the State may enforce them by law |
| **State of Gujarat v. Mirzapur Moti Kureshi Kassab Jamat** | (2005) 8 SCC 534 | Art. 51A(g) (compassion for living creatures) read with Art. 48 to **uphold a ban** on slaughter of cows and progeny |
| **Animal Welfare Board of India v. A. Nagaraja** (*Jallikattu*) | (2014) 7 SCC 547 | Art. 51A(g) and the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act; Jallikattu and bullock-cart races prohibited; "animal rights" part of Art. 21 values. In **18 May 2023** a Constitution Bench upheld the Tamil Nadu amendment legalising Jallikattu *(verify citation)* |
| **Union of India v. Naveen Jindal** | (2004) 2 SCC 510 | Right to fly the National Flag is an aspect of Art. 19(1)(a), subject to the **Flag Code** and the duty of respect in Art. 51A(a) |
| **Shyam Narayan Chouksey v. Union of India** | (2017) 1 SCC 421 (order of 30 Nov 2016) | Interim order making the **playing of the National Anthem before films compulsory** on the strength of Art. 51A(a); later modified in 2017–2018 to make it **optional/directory** *(verify the sequence of orders)* |
| **Re: Noise Pollution** | (2005) 5 SCC 733 | Noise pollution violates Art. 21; reference to environmental duty under Art. 51A(g) *(verify)* |

**Use of duties by the High Courts and the Supreme Court:** to impose **reasonable restrictions** (e.g., on destruction of public property), to read duties as a **limit** on the exercise of rights, and to justify **PIL directions** for environment, heritage and education.

| Duty | Statute / measure |

## 6. Verma Committee (1999)

- The **Justice J.S. Verma Committee** (set up in 1998 under the National Campaign for Operationalising Fundamental Duties) submitted its report in **1999**: it **identified existing legal provisions** relating to certain duties (e.g., the 1971 Act on national honour, penal provisions on promoting enmity, Protection of Civil Rights Act) and recommended that the duties be **taught in schools** and publicised, and that a mechanism be created to operationalise them.
- **Action:** the report was circulated; no constitutional change was made. Several education bodies have included Fundamental Duties in the curriculum.

---

## 7. Criticism and importance

### 7.1 Criticism
1. **Non-enforceable and without sanction:** reduced to "moral exhortations".
2. **Vague:** terms such as "composite culture", "scientific temper", "excellence" have no definite meaning.
3. **Placed after the DPSPs** and outside Part III, despite their importance; many say it should have been in the original text.
4. **Not exhaustive:** duties such as paying taxes, voting, or respecting others' rights are not explicit.
5. **Introduced during the Emergency** and viewed with suspicion as a counterweight to the Fundamental Rights; the 1977 Janata Government retained them.
6. **Citizens only.**

### 7.2 Importance
1. **Reminder** that rights come with civic responsibilities.
2. **Interpretive aid** for courts; ground for restrictions on rights and for upholding laws.
3. **Educational value:** curriculum and public awareness.
4. **Warning** against anti-national activities and destruction of public property.
5. **Basis of legislation** (list in §5).
6. **Balance** to the Fundamental Rights, completing the triangle of Parts III, IV and IVA.

## 8. Current relevance and recent developments

- **Animal welfare and Jallikattu:** the 2023 Constitution Bench decision (verify) shows the weight given to Art. 51A(g) in arguments.
- **Environment and climate:** *M.K. Ranjitsinh* (2024) (Unit 8) connects Arts. 48A, 51A(g) and 21.
- **Flag and Anthem controversies:** the cinema anthem order (2016–2018) is the best-known example of a court-ordered application of Art. 51A(a).
- **Education policy:** NEP 2020 recommends inclusion of duties and constitutional values in the curriculum.
- **Caution for the exam:** do not say the Fundamental Duties are enforceable by writ. They are enforceable only through ordinary legislation.

## 9. Quick-revision box

- **Part IVA, Art. 51A.** Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)**; clause (k) by the **86th Amendment (2002)**. **Ten → eleven.**

- **Eleven duties:** (a) Constitution/Flag/Anthem · (b) freedom struggle ideals · (c) sovereignty, unity, integrity · (d) defend, national service · (e) harmony; women's dignity · (f) composite heritage · (g) environment; compassion for living creatures · (h) scientific temper · (i) public property; non-violence · (j) excellence · (k) parents/guardians: education of 6–14.
