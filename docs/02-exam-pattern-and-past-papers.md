# Exam Pattern and Previous-Year Papers (CCSU LL.B. Semester I)

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-28 (IST) · **Branch:** `docs/exam-pattern-and-past-papers`
> **Based on:** all 20 papers for December 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 across the five Semester I subjects (5 subjects × 4 sittings). Every PDF was read page by page on 2026-10-28. Verbatim questions are in [`past-papers/`](past-papers/).
> **Not found:** an **official marking scheme or model-answer key** from the university. See section 3. Nothing in this repo claims one exists.

---

## 1. The pattern, as printed on every one of the 20 papers

| Item | What the paper says |
|---|---|
| Time / marks | 3 hours; maximum 100 marks |
| Language | Every question printed in English and Hindi |
| Instruction | "Attempt questions from all the Sections as per instructions" |
| **Section A** — Very Short Answer | **Attempt all 5**; 4 marks each (5 × 4 = 20); "not exceeding **75 words**" |
| **Section B** — Short Answer | **Any 2 of 3**; 10 marks each (2 × 10 = 20); "not exceeding **200 words**" |
| **Section C** — Detailed Answer | **Any 3 of 5**; 20 marks each (3 × 20 = 60); "answer is required in detail" (no word limit printed) |
| Question numbers | A = Q1–5, B = Q6–8, C = Q9–13 |

Things worth knowing that the pattern hides:

- **Only Section A is compulsory.** In B you may leave one of the three questions and in C two of the five, so you can drop your weakest topics.
- **Section C's last question (Q13) is sometimes a pair or a "comment on any two of three"**: Constitutional Law Dec 2025 (a)(b); Contract Dec 2022 (a)(b); Crimes Dec 2023 (a)(b) and Crimes Dec 2024 (a)(b)(c). Prepare the pair topics as 10-mark notes.
- **Problem questions appear** in Torts (Dec 2025 B7 and C10), Contract (Dec 2025 A3) and Crimes (Dec 2023 B7). Answer them in IRAC form: issue, rule, application, conclusion.
- **Printing quirks** (the paper is wrong, not you): Jurisprudence Dec 2022 says "five questions" in English and "six" in Hindi for Section C (five are printed). Contract Dec 2022 Q9 prints "(1885) A.I.R. 1959 S.C. 781"; the case is *Gherulal Parekh v. Mahadeo Das*, AIR 1959 SC 781. Torts Dec 2023 B7 prints "Injuria sine Damnum". Torts Dec 2022 and Dec 2024 still say "Consumer Disputes Redressal **Forum**", a name the 1986 Act used; the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 says "**Commission**". If the paper says Forum, write "District Commission (formerly Forum)".

## 2. Paper codes as printed (a discrepancy to settle)

| Subject | Syllabus map | Printed code (all four sittings unless noted) |
|---|---|---|
| Jurisprudence – I | K-1001 | 11200 |
| Constitutional Law – I | K-1002 | 11201 |
| Torts and Consumer Protection | K-1003 | 11202 |
| Law of Crimes – I | K-1004 | **Dec 2022 and Dec 2023:** 11203, K-1004, "LAW-IV, I.P.C. (Law of Crime)". **Dec 2024 and Dec 2025:** **11313, K-1006**, "Law of Crimes (Bhartiya Nyay Sanhita-2023)" |
| Contract – I | K-1005 | 11204 |

**What this means.** The official syllabus I mapped (CCSU `2025/08` upload) still describes Paper IV as "Indian Penal Code", but the last two examinations were **set on the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023** and carry a different code. I searched for a revised syllabus and found only third-party pages that disagree with each other (one lists a "comparative study of BNS and IPC"; another still says IPC), so I could not confirm it. **Paper IV in this repo will therefore be written BNS-first with the IPC section alongside**, which is safe under either reading. Please confirm the current syllabus with your department (item A2 in [`01-gaps-and-open-questions.md`](01-gaps-and-open-questions.md)).

## 3. The marking scheme: what was and was not found

- **Found:** the printed per-section instructions above, which are the only "marking" information on the papers: marks per question and the 75 and 200-word limits.
- **Not found:** any university-issued marking scheme, model answer key, or examiner's guidelines for these papers. The university website, the question-paper listing and the search results I could reach carried none. Answer keys of this kind are normally given to examiners, not published.
- **Consequence.** Any mark split I show (for example "introduction 2, cases 6, criticism 2") is **my own rubric, built from the printed marks and ordinary examining practice**, and is labelled "model rubric" wherever it appears. It is a way to self-check, not what an examiner will necessarily use. Where your teacher gives a scheme, follow your teacher.

## 4. Planning a 3-hour sitting (suggested; not official)

| Block | Suggested time | Target length |
|---|---|---|
| Read paper, choose questions | 10 min | — |
| Section A: five answers | 25 min (5 min each) | 60–75 words each. **The paper's limit is 75; do not exceed it.** |
| Section B: two answers | 30 min (15 min each) | 150–200 words each. **Limit 200.** |
| Section C: three answers | 105 min (35 min each) | No printed limit. A well-built 20-mark answer is usually about 900–1,300 words (about 5–7 pages of an answer book). This range is my estimate. |
| Review | 10 min | — |

A 20-mark answer should carry: a one-line definition or provision, the rule's development, 4–6 cases with the point each proves, criticism, current position, and a two-line conclusion.

## 6. How this analysis is used in the repo

1. **Every previous-year question becomes a bank item with a complete model answer** in its paper's question bank, tagged with the year and section it appeared in.
2. **Topic coverage is not narrowed to past questions.** Each syllabus unit also carries questions on points the four sittings did not ask, so a fresh question can still be answered.
3. **"Likely" labels are replaced by the counts above.** If a topic is marked "asked in 4 of 4 sittings", that is a fact about the past, not a forecast.
4. **Answers follow the printed limits.** Section A answers stay within 75 words and Section B within 200. Section C answers are written long, with cases set out in full.
