# CCSU LL.B. (3-Year, Six-Semester) — Semester I Syllabus Map

> **Source:** Ch. Charan Singh University, LL.B. 3-Year (Six Semester) Course syllabus, published on the university CDN:
> <https://cdn.ccsuniversity.ac.in/public/pdf/2025/08/2%20llb%20syllabus.pdf>
> **Retrieved:** 2026-10-27 (IST)
> **Version caution:** the file sits in the university's `2025/08` upload folder. I could not confirm whether the syllabus was revised for the 2026-27 session. Confirm with the college office / department notice board before relying on it for exams.

Semester I carries **500 marks: five theory papers of 100 marks each.** The official syllabus does not state a question-paper pattern, an internal-assessment split, or a practical component for Semester I (see `01-gaps-and-open-questions.md`).

---

## Paper I — Jurisprudence – I (Legal Theory) · `K-1001`

1. Introduction: definition, nature and scope of jurisprudence; importance of its study
2. Natural Law School: classical natural law; revival of natural law — Rudolf Stammler and Kohler
3. Analytical School: John Austin, Hans Kelsen and H.L.A. Hart
4. Historical School: Frederick Karl von Savigny, Sir Henry Sumner Maine
5. Sociological School: background and characteristics; Roscoe Pound; Leon Duguit
6. American Modern Realism
7. Economic approach to law: Marxist economic approach to law

**Recommended books:** Bodenheimer; Dias; Friedmann (*Law in Changing Society*); Lloyd; V.D. Mahajan; Paton; Salmond.

---

## Paper II — Constitutional Law of India – I (Nature of the Constitution and Fundamental Rights) · `K-1002`

1. Nature of the Indian Constitution, with special emphasis on (a) federal structure and (b) form of government
2. Preamble
3. Fundamental Rights
   - (a) General: scheme of Fundamental Rights; who can claim them; against whom available; suspension
   - (b) Specific: Equality (Arts. 14–18); freedom of speech and expression (Art. 19(1)(a)); protection in respect of conviction for offences (Art. 20); life and personal liberty (Art. 21); education (Art. 21A); against exploitation (Arts. 23–24); freedom of religion (Arts. 25–28); cultural and educational rights of minorities (Arts. 29–30); constitutional remedies (Art. 32)
4. Directive Principles of State Policy — importance and relationship with Fundamental Rights
5. Fundamental Duties

**Recommended books:** Chander Pal (two titles); Hidayatullah; M.P. Jain; J.N. Pandey; Seervai; V.N. Shukla; T.R. Tope.

---

## Paper III — Law of Torts (and Consumer Protection Laws) · `K-1003`

1. Torts and tortious liability — definition, nature, characteristics; constituents of tort; fault; *damnum sine injuria* / *injuria sine damno*; mental element (malice, negligence, motive); who may sue and who may not be sued; strict and absolute liability
2. Justifications in tort — *volenti non fit injuria*; necessity; inevitable accident and Act of God; statutory authority
3. Liability for torts committed by others — vicarious liability; liability of the State (sovereign immunity); joint tort-feasors
4. Specific torts — negligence; nuisance; trespass; defamation
5. Consumer Protection Act — consumer: definition and concept; contract *of* service and contract *for* service; enforcement of consumer rights

**Recommended books:** V.K. Aggarwal (consumer protection); R.K. Bangia; S.K. Kapoor; Ratanlal & Dhirajlal; N.V. Paranjape; Salmond; Winfield & Jolowicz.

---

## Paper IV — Law of Crimes – I (Indian Penal Code) · `K-1004`

1. General principles of crime — concept; elements; stages; strict liability, constructive liability, common intention and common object
2. Inchoate crimes / preliminary offences — abetment, conspiracy, attempt
3. General exceptions — mistake, accident, necessity, young age, unsoundness of mind, drunkenness, right of private defence
4. Specific offences
   - Human body: culpable homicide; murder; death by rash or negligent act; hurt and grievous hurt; kidnapping and abduction; wrongful restraint and confinement; assault; criminal force
   - Property: theft; extortion; robbery; dacoity; cheating
   - State and public tranquillity: sedition; unlawful assembly; rioting and affray
   - Marriage: bigamy and adultery

**Recommended books:** Hari Singh Gaur; K.D. Gaur; Ratanlal & Dhirajlal; S.N. Misra; R.C. Nigam.

> **Currency note:** the syllabus is written around the IPC, 1860. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 replaced the IPC for offences from 1 July 2024, and the "sedition" and "adultery" items have changed in law. This repo gives IPC ↔ BNS section mapping throughout. See `01-gaps-and-open-questions.md`.

## Paper V — Contract – I (General Principles of Contract) · `K-1005`

1. Purpose, evolution and scope of the law of contracts
2. Proposal and promise — communication of proposal, acceptance, and revocation
3. Consideration and the doctrine of privity of contract
4. Lawful object and consideration
5. Capacity to contract — minors, persons of unsound mind, others disqualified by law; minor's agreements; doctrine of restitution
6. Consent and free consent
7. Standard form of contracts
8. Agreements declared void and voidable under the Contract Act
9. Contingent contracts
10. Quasi-contracts (relations resembling those created by contract)
11. Discharge of contracts, with special reference to frustration
12. Remedies in the form of compensation

**Recommended books:** Anson; R.K. Bangia; S.T. Desai; S.K. Kapoor; A.C. Moitra; Pollock & Mulla; Avtar Singh.
